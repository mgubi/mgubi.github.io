Relativistic quantum fields and imaginary time

Massimiliano Gubinelli | University of Oxford

SISSA lectures, Lecture 1 — notes: Lecture 1, §§1–2

What is constructive QFT?

  • Goal: construct, with full rigour, interacting relativistic quantum fields, i.e. models satisfying axioms encoding quantum mechanics, special relativity and causality.

  • Status:

    • \(d = 2\): \(P (\varphi)_2\), Yukawa\(2\), sine-Gordon, …

    • \(d = 3\): \(\Phi^4_3\), …

    • \(d = 4\): no interacting example; critical lattice \(\varphi^4_4\) has Gaussian scaling limits (Aizenman–Duminil-Copin 2021).

  • Lesson of the late 1960s–70s: work in imaginary time. A QFT becomes a probability measure on \(\mathcal{S}' (\mathbb{R}^d)\), which looks like the continuum limit of a classical spin system at a critical point.

  • Modern tools (this course): stochastic quantisation, singular SPDEs, variational methods; RG and cluster expansions as complements.

Relativistic quantum fields

Lecture 1

Kinematics

  • Minkowski space \(\mathbb{R}^{1, s}\), \(x = (x^0, \textbf{x})\), \(x \cdot y = x^0 y^0 - \textbf{x} \cdot \textbf{y}\).

  • \(x, y\) spacelike separated if \((x - y)^2 < 0\).

  • Forward cone \(V_{\hspace{-0.17em} +} = \{p : p^0 > | \textbf{p} |\}\), closure \(\bar{V}_{\hspace{-0.17em} +}\).

  • Restricted Lorentz group \(\mathcal{L}^{\uparrow}_+\); Poincaré group \(\mathcal{P}^{\uparrow}_+ =\mathbb{R}^d \rtimes \mathcal{L}^{\uparrow}_+\) acting by \(x \mapsto \Lambda x + a\).

  • Wigner: a relativistic quantum system carries a (projective) unitary representation of \(\mathcal{P}^{\uparrow}_+\).

Wightman axioms (I)

(W0) Relativistic quantum theory

Separable Hilbert space \(\mathcal{H}\); strongly continuous unitary representation \(U\) of \(\mathcal{P}^{\uparrow}_+\); \(U (a, \mathbb{1}) = \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} P \cdot a}\), \(H = P^0\).
Spectrum condition: \(\text{spec} (P) \subset \bar{V}_{\hspace{-0.17em} +}\), so \(H \geqslant | \textbf{P} | \geqslant 0\).
Vacuum: unique (up to phase) unit vector \(\Omega\) with \(U (a, \mathbb{1}) \Omega = \Omega\).

(W1) Fields

Dense \(\mathcal{D} \ni \Omega\); for \(f \in \mathcal{S} (\mathbb{R}^d)\) an operator \(\varphi (f)\) on \(\mathcal{D}\), \(\varphi (f) \mathcal{D} \subset \mathcal{D}\), \(U\mathcal{D} \subset \mathcal{D}\); \(f \mapsto \langle \chi, \varphi (f) \psi \rangle\) tempered; \(\langle \chi, \varphi (f) \psi \rangle = \langle \varphi (\bar{f}) \chi, \psi \rangle\).

Wightman axioms (II)

(W2) Covariance, (W3) Locality, (W4) Cyclicity

\(U (a, \Lambda) \varphi (f) U (a, \Lambda)^{- 1} = \varphi (f \circ (a, \Lambda)^{- 1})\);\([\varphi (f), \varphi (g)] = 0\) if supports are spacelike separated;polynomials in \(\varphi\) applied to \(\Omega\) are dense.

Formally \(\varphi (f) = \int \varphi (x) f (x) \mathrm{d} x\), \(\varphi (x^0, \textbf{x}) = \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} Hx^0} \varphi (0, \textbf{x}) \mathrm{e}^{- \mathrm{i} Hx^0}\).

Wightman functions \(W_n (f_1, \ldots, f_n) = \langle \Omega, \varphi (f_1) \cdots \varphi (f_n) \Omega \rangle \in \mathcal{S}' (\mathbb{R}^{dn})\):

  • positivity \(\sum_{j, k} W_{j + k} (g_j^{\ast} \otimes g_k) \geqslant 0\);Poincaré invariance;

  • spectral condition: Fourier support in \(\bar{V}_{\hspace{-0.17em} +}^{\hspace{0.17em} n - 1}\) (difference variables);

  • locality; cluster property \(\Leftrightarrow\) unique vacuum.

Reconstruction

Theorem. (Wightman reconstruction; Streater–Wightman 1964) A sequence \((W_n)\) of tempered distributions with \(W_0 = 1\) satisfying positivity, invariance, spectral condition and locality is the sequence of Wightman functions of a Wightman theory, unique up to unitary equivalence.

  • To construct a QFT it suffices to construct its Wightman functions.

  • Difficulty: positivity involves all \(W_n\) at once and refers to real time, while what we can construct lives in imaginary time.

  • The Euclidean approach replaces it by reflection positivity, checkable on lattice approximations and stable under limits.

  • The axioms say nothing about dynamics: no Lagrangian; the interaction is hidden in the higher \(W_n\).

The free field

\(\omega (\textbf{p}) = (| \textbf{p} |^2 + m^2)^{1 / 2}\),\(\mathrm{d} \Omega_m (\textbf{p}) = \dfrac{\mathrm{d} \textbf{p}}{(2 \pi)^s \hspace{0.17em} 2 \omega (\textbf{p})}\) (invariant measure on \(H_m^+ = \{p^2 = m^2, p^0 > 0\}\)), Fock space over \(L^2 (\mathrm{d} \Omega_m)\):

\(\displaystyle \varphi (x) = \int \mathrm{d} \Omega_m (\textbf{p}) [a (\textbf{p}) \mathrm{e}^{- \mathrm{i} p \cdot x} + a^{\ast} (\textbf{p}) \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} p \cdot x}]_{p^0 = \omega (\textbf{p})},\)

with \([a (\textbf{p}), a^{\ast} (\textbf{q})] = (2 \pi)^s 2 \omega (\textbf{p}) \hspace{0.17em} \delta (\textbf{p} - \textbf{q})\), \(a (\textbf{p}) \Omega = 0\). Then

\(\displaystyle W_2 (x - y) = \int \mathrm{d} \Omega_m (\textbf{p}) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{e}^{- \mathrm{i} p \cdot (x - y)}, \qquad \| \varphi (f) \Omega \|^2 = \int \mathrm{d} \Omega_m | \tilde{f} |^2 < \infty,\)

but

\(\displaystyle \| \varphi (x) \Omega \|^2 = W_2 (0) = \int \mathrm{d} \Omega_m = + \infty \qquad (s \geqslant 1) .\)

UV divergence: logarithmic for \(s = 1\), power-like for \(s \geqslant 2\). Not an artefact of the free field.

No fields at a point

Proposition. (Wightman 1964) Assume (W0)–(W2) and \(\varphi (f) \Omega = \int f (x) U (x, \mathbb{1}) \psi \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} x\) for some \(\psi \in \mathcal{H}\) (“\(\varphi (0) \Omega = \psi\) exists”). Then \(\psi \in \mathbb{C} \Omega\), hence \(\varphi (f) \Omega = c \hspace{0.17em} (\int f) \hspace{0.17em} \Omega\).

Lemma. For \(d \geqslant 2\), a finite \(\mathcal{L}^{\uparrow}_+\)-invariant measure on \(\bar{V}_{\hspace{-0.17em} +}\) is \(c \hspace{0.17em} \delta_0\).

  • Combined with locality and Reeh–Schlieder: \(\varphi\) is a multiple of \(\mathbb{1}\).

  • Quantum fields are operator-valued distributions: nonlinear expressions in \(\varphi (x)\) need a definition \(\Rightarrow\) renormalisation.

Proofs

Lemma. A boost of rapidity \(\theta\) acts as \(p^0 \pm p^1 \mapsto \mathrm{e}^{\pm \theta} (p^0 \pm p^1)\). The sets

\(\displaystyle S_j^{\pm} = \{p \in \bar{V}_{\hspace{-0.17em} +} : 2^j \leqslant p^0 \pm p^1 < 2^{j + 1} \}, \qquad j \in \mathbb{Z},\)

are mapped to each other by the boost with \(\mathrm{e}^{\theta} = 2\); they are disjoint and cover \(\bar{V}_{\hspace{-0.17em} +} \setminus \{0\}\). Invariance and finiteness force \(\mu (S^{\pm}_j) = 0\).

Proposition.

  1. Covariance gives \(\int f (x) U (\Lambda x) (U (0, \Lambda) \psi - \psi) \mathrm{d} x = 0\) for all \(f\), so \(U (0, \Lambda) \psi = \psi\).

  2. The spectral measure \(\mu\) of \(\psi\) for translations is finite, supported in \(\bar{V}_{\hspace{-0.17em} +}\) and Lorentz invariant, so \(\mu = \| \psi \|^2 \delta_0\).

  3. Hence \(U (a) \psi = \psi\) for all \(a\), and \(\psi \in \mathbb{C} \Omega\) by uniqueness of the vacuum.

The Källén–Lehmann representation

Let \(W_2^{(M)}\) be the free two-point function of mass \(M \geqslant 0\) and \(\langle \Omega, \varphi (f) \Omega \rangle = v \int f\).

Theorem. (Källén 1952, Lehmann 1954) Under (W0)–(W2) there is a positive, polynomially bounded measure \(\rho\) on \([0, \infty)\) (the spectral measure of the field) such that

\(\displaystyle W_2 (x - y) = v^2 + \int_{[0, \infty)} \rho (\mathrm{d} M^2) \hspace{0.17em} W_2^{(M)} (x - y) .\)

(\(\rho (\{0\}) = 0\) if \(d = 2\).)

Sketch. \(\psi_f = \varphi (f) \Omega - v (\int f) \Omega\) gives \(W_2 - v^2\) of positive type \(\Rightarrow\) (Bochner–Schwartz) Fourier transform of a positive measure \(\mu\), supported in \(\bar{V}_{\hspace{-0.17em} +}\), \(\mu (\{0\}) = 0\), Lorentz invariant. Orbits are the hyperboloids \(H_M^+\) (and the cone for \(M = 0\)), each with a unique invariant measure \(\mathrm{d} \Omega_M\). Disintegrate along \(M\).

Reading the spectrum off \(\rho\)

  • Free field: \(\rho = \delta_{m^2}\). Conversely \(\rho = \delta_{m^2}\), \(m > 0\), forces \(\varphi - v\) to be free, by locality (Jost–Schroer).

  • Generalised free fields \(\varphi = \sum_i c_i \varphi_{M_i}\) have \(\rho = \sum_i c_i^2 \delta_{M_i^2}\) and no interaction: \(\rho \neq \delta_{m^2}\) is necessary, not sufficient.

  • Particles: an isolated atom \(Z \delta_{m^2}\) is a particle of mass \(m\); multi-particle continuum from \((2 m)^2\) (or \((3 m)^2\) with \(\varphi \mapsto - \varphi\) symmetry). Weakly coupled \(P (\varphi)_2\): Glimm–Jaffe–Spencer 1974.

  • No point fields again: \(W_2^T (0) = \int \rho (\mathrm{d} M^2) \int \mathrm{d} \Omega_M = \infty\).

  • Canonical fields: CCR at sharp times \(\Rightarrow\) \(\int \rho = 1\), so \(Z \leqslant 1\). In \(d = 4\) perturbatively \(\int \rho = \infty\) (wave-function renormalisation).

The quantum wave equation

The free field solves, as an operator-valued distribution,

\(\displaystyle (\Box + m^2) \varphi = 0, \qquad \Box = \partial_t^2 - \Delta_{\textbf{x}},\)

and its sharp-time fields \(\varphi (t, g)\), \(\pi (t, g)\), \(\pi = \partial_t \varphi\), satisfy the CCR

\(\displaystyle [\varphi (t, \textbf{x}), \pi (t, \textbf{y})] = \mathrm{i} \hspace{0.17em} \delta (\textbf{x} - \textbf{y}), \qquad [\varphi, \varphi] = [\pi, \pi] = 0.\)

Picture

A linear wave equation with quantum initial data \((\varphi (0, \cdot), \pi (0, \cdot))\), “distributed” according to the vacuum.

Interacting version: \((\Box + m^2) \varphi + \lambda \varphi^3 = 0\) together with the CCR.

The free field on a circle

\(s = 1\), space \(\mathbb{T}_L =\mathbb{R}/ L\mathbb{Z}\), momenta \(k \in \frac{2 \pi}{L} \mathbb{Z}\), \(\omega_k = (k^2 + m^2)^{1 / 2}\):

\(\displaystyle \varphi (0, x) = \sum_k \frac{a_k \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} kx} + a_k^{\ast} \mathrm{e}^{- \mathrm{i} kx}}{\sqrt{2 L \omega_k}}, \qquad [a_k, a^{\ast}_{k'}] = \delta_{kk'}, \qquad H_0 = \sum_k \omega_k a_k^{\ast} a_k .\)

  • An infinite family of independent harmonic oscillators, one per mode.

  • With a UV cutoff \(|k| \leqslant R\):

    \(\displaystyle \langle \Omega, \varphi_R (0, x)^2 \Omega \rangle = \sum_{|k| \leqslant R} \frac{1}{2 L \omega_k} = \frac{1}{2 \pi} \log \frac{R}{m} + O (1) \to \infty .\)

  • Wick ordering \(: \varphi_R^2 := \varphi_R^2 - \langle \Omega, \varphi_R^2 \Omega \rangle\) removes exactly this divergence (Exercise 1.7).

Interactions: three problems

  1. Products of distributions. \(\varphi (x)^3\) is meaningless and must be renormalised: Wick ordering suffices in \(d = 2\); more counterterms in \(d = 3\); triviality in \(d = 4\).

  2. Haag's theorem. In infinite volume the interacting field at fixed time is not unitarily equivalent to the free one: there is no interaction picture.

  3. Semiboundedness. Even on \(\mathbb{T}_L\), \(H = H_0 + \lambda \int : \varphi (0, x)^4 : \mathrm{d} x\) is positive plus unbounded below. Nelson 1966: bounded below (hypercontractivity). Volume cutoff removed by Glimm–Jaffe; Wightman axioms for weak coupling \(P (\varphi)_2\) by Glimm–Jaffe–Spencer 1974.

The Euclidean approach (Symanzik, Nelson, Osterwalder–Schrader, Guerra–Rosen–Simon) simplifies this enormously.

From real to imaginary time

Lecture 1

A lattice Hamiltonian on the circle

Lattice \(\Lambda_{\varepsilon} = \varepsilon \mathbb{Z}/ L\mathbb{Z}\), \(N = L / \varepsilon\) sites, \(\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon} = L^2 (\mathbb{R}^{\Lambda_{\varepsilon}}, \mathrm{d} \varphi)\), \(\varphi_x\) multiplication, \(\pi_x = - \mathrm{i} \varepsilon^{- 1} \partial_{\varphi_x}\), so \([\varphi_x, \pi_y] = \mathrm{i} \varepsilon^{- 1} \mathbb{1}_{x = y}\).

\(\displaystyle H_{\varepsilon} = \sum_x \varepsilon \left[ \tfrac{1}{2} \pi_x^2 + \tfrac{1}{2} (\nabla_{\varepsilon} \varphi)_x^2 + \tfrac{1}{2} m^2 \varphi_x^2 + V (\varphi_x) \right] = - \frac{1}{2 \varepsilon} \Delta_{\mathbb{R}^N} + U_{\varepsilon} (\varphi) .\)

  • Heisenberg fields solve the lattice wave equation

    \(\displaystyle \partial_t^2 \varphi_x = (\Delta_{\varepsilon} \varphi)_x - m^2 \varphi_x - V' (\varphi_x)\)

    — the quantum wave equation, now rigorous.

  • Schrödinger operator with confining potential: self-adjoint, compact resolvent, \(\mathrm{e}^{- tH_{\varepsilon}}\) trace class with positive kernel.

  • Perron–Frobenius: simple ground state \(\Omega_{\varepsilon} > 0\), energy \(E_0\). \(\bar{H} := H_{\varepsilon} - E_0 \geqslant 0\).

Positivity of the energy \(\Rightarrow\) Wick rotation

For bounded \(F_j\) and \(F_j (t) = \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} tH} F_j \mathrm{e}^{- \mathrm{i} tH}\):

\(\displaystyle W (t_1, \ldots, t_n) = \langle \Omega_{\varepsilon}, F_1 \mathrm{e}^{- \mathrm{i} (t_1 - t_2) \bar{H}} F_2 \cdots F_n \Omega_{\varepsilon} \rangle .\)

Proposition. \(W\) extends analytically to \(\text{Im} (t_j - t_{j + 1}) < 0\). At \(t_j = - \mathrm{i} \tau_j\), \(\tau_1 \geqslant \ldots \geqslant \tau_n\), it gives the Schwinger functions

\(\displaystyle S (\tau_1, \ldots, \tau_n) = \langle \Omega_{\varepsilon}, F_1 \mathrm{e}^{- (\tau_1 - \tau_2) \bar{H}} F_2 \cdots F_n \Omega_{\varepsilon} \rangle,\)

which determine \(W\).

Where positivity of the energy is used

  1. Analyticity. \(\mathrm{e}^{- \mathrm{i} \zeta \bar{H}} = \int \mathrm{e}^{- \mathrm{i} \zeta \lambda} \mathrm{d} E (\lambda)\) is a contraction for \(\text{Im} \zeta \leqslant 0\), since \(\lambda \geqslant 0\): analytic in the interior, continuous up to the boundary.

  2. Uniqueness. An analytic function on the half plane vanishing on \(- \mathrm{i} \mathbb{R}_+\) vanishes; iterate variable by variable.

  3. Semigroup. \(\mathrm{e}^{- \tau \bar{H}}\) is a contraction semigroup, which can be represented as a path integral (Feynman–Kac).

  4. Projection onto the vacuum. \(\mathrm{e}^{- T \bar{H}} \to | \Omega \rangle \langle \Omega |\) as \(T \to \infty\).

Relativistic version: Wightman functions are boundary values of functions analytic in the forward tube; Lorentz covariance extends them to all non-coincident Euclidean points (Schwinger functions are symmetric and Euclidean invariant).

Discretising imaginary time

Kinetic semigroup:

\(\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\delta}{2 \varepsilon} \Delta} (\varphi, \varphi') = (2 \pi \delta / \varepsilon)^{- N / 2} \exp \left( - \frac{\varepsilon}{2 \delta} | \varphi - \varphi' |^2 \right)\)
.

Transfer matrix (symmetric Trotter step):

\(\displaystyle T_{\delta} = \mathrm{e}^{- \delta U / 2} \mathrm{e}^{\frac{\delta}{2 \varepsilon} \Delta} \mathrm{e}^{- \delta U / 2}, \qquad T_{\delta} (\varphi, \varphi') \propto \exp \left( - \frac{\varepsilon}{2 \delta} \sum_x (\varphi_x - \varphi_x')^2 - \frac{\delta}{2} (U (\varphi) + U (\varphi')) \right),\)

and \(T_{\delta}^{\lfloor \tau / \delta \rfloor} \to \mathrm{e}^{- \tau H_{\varepsilon}}\) (Trotter).

Space-time lattice \(\Lambda = (\delta \mathbb{Z}/ T\mathbb{Z}) \times (\varepsilon \mathbb{Z}/ L\mathbb{Z})\), \(T = J \delta\):

\(\displaystyle \text{Tr} (T_{\delta}^J) \propto \int \mathrm{e}^{- S_{\delta, \varepsilon} (\varphi)} \prod_{u \in \Lambda} \mathrm{d} \varphi_u,\)
\(\displaystyle S_{\delta, \varepsilon} (\varphi) = \sum_{(\tau, x)} \varepsilon \delta \left[ \tfrac{1}{2} (\partial^{\delta}_{\tau} \varphi)^2 + \tfrac{1}{2} (\nabla_{\varepsilon} \varphi)^2 + \tfrac{1}{2} m^2 \varphi^2 + V (\varphi) \right] .\)

The Minkowski metric has become Euclidean.

Feynman–Kac–Nelson

Lattice Gibbs measure \(\mu^{T, L}_{\delta, \varepsilon} (\mathrm{d} \varphi) = Z^{- 1} \mathrm{e}^{- S_{\delta, \varepsilon} (\varphi)} \prod_u \mathrm{d} \varphi_u\):

\(\displaystyle \frac{\text{Tr} (F_1 T_{\delta}^{k_1} F_2 \cdots F_n T_{\delta}^{k_n})}{\text{Tr} (T_{\delta}^J)} = \int F_1 (\varphi_{\tau_1}) \cdots F_n (\varphi_{\tau_n}) \hspace{0.17em} \mu^{T, L}_{\delta, \varepsilon} (\mathrm{d} \varphi) .\)

Proposition.

\(\displaystyle \langle \Omega_{\varepsilon}, F_1 \mathrm{e}^{- (\tau_1 - \tau_2) \bar{H}} F_2 \cdots F_n \Omega_{\varepsilon} \rangle = \lim_{T \to \infty} \lim_{\delta \to 0} \int F_1 (\varphi_{\tau_1}) \cdots F_n (\varphi_{\tau_n}) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \mu^{T, L}_{\delta, \varepsilon} .\)

Sketch. Gaussian factor \(=\) Brownian loops with diffusion \(\varepsilon^{- 1}\); \(\delta \sum_{\tau} U (\varphi_{\tau})\) is a Riemann sum for \(\int_0^T U (\omega_{\tau}) \mathrm{d} \tau\); then \(T \to \infty\) uses the spectral gap.

Vacuum expectations in imaginary time are moments of a classical probability measure.

Example: one oscillator and the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process

\(H = - \frac{1}{2} \partial_q^2 + \frac{1}{2} m^2 q^2\),\(\Omega (q) \propto \mathrm{e}^{- mq^2 / 2}\),\(E_0 = \frac{m}{2}\).

  • Ground-state transform: \(\Omega^{- 1} \bar{H} \hspace{0.17em} \Omega = - \tfrac{1}{2} \partial_q^2 + mq \partial_q\), minus the generator of \(\mathrm{d} X = - mX \mathrm{d} t + \mathrm{d} B\).

  • Schwinger function \(\langle \Omega, q \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{e}^{- \tau \bar{H}} q \hspace{0.17em} \Omega \rangle = \dfrac{\mathrm{e}^{- m \tau}}{2 m}\).

  • This is \((- \partial_{\tau}^2 + m^2)^{- 1}\): the GFF in \(d = 1\).

Notes, Exercise 2.2

The space-time lattice

Rows are quantum configurations at fixed imaginary time; the transfer matrix connects consecutive rows. As \(T \to \infty\), the rows form a stationary Markov process whose one-time marginal is \(\Omega_{\varepsilon} (\varphi)^2 \mathrm{d} \varphi\).

Classical statistical mechanics

Isotropic lattice \(\delta = \varepsilon\):

\(\displaystyle S_{\varepsilon} (\varphi) = \sum_{\langle u, v \rangle} \tfrac{1}{2} (\varphi_u - \varphi_v)^2 + \varepsilon^2 \sum_u \left[ \tfrac{1}{2} m^2 \varphi_u^2 + V (\varphi_u) \right] .\)

  • \(\mu_{\varepsilon} \propto \prod_{\langle u, v \rangle} \mathrm{e}^{- \frac{1}{2} (\varphi_u - \varphi_v)^2} \prod_u \nu_{\varepsilon} (\mathrm{d} \varphi_u)\), \(\nu_{\varepsilon} (\mathrm{d} a) = \mathrm{e}^{- \varepsilon^2 (\frac{1}{2} m^2 a^2 + V (a))} \mathrm{d} a\).

  • A ferromagnetic nearest-neighbour spin system with unbounded spins: short-range classical statistical mechanics.

  • In dimension \(d\): \(\varepsilon^{d - 2} \sum_{\langle u, v \rangle}\) and \(\varepsilon^d \sum_u\).

  • Nearest-neighbour \(\Rightarrow\) reflection positive: the Euclidean shadow of \(\mathrm{e}^{- \varepsilon H} \geqslant 0\). Conversely, RP \(\Rightarrow\) Hilbert space and \(H \geqslant 0\) (Exercises 2.5, 2.7).

Continuum limit \(=\) critical point

  • In lattice units the mass is \(\varepsilon m \to 0\): correlation length \(\xi \approx (\varepsilon m)^{- 1} \to \infty\) lattice spacings.

  • Continuum QFT \(=\) scaling limit of short-range spin systems approaching criticality (Wilson). Conversely, RP critical models with nontrivial scaling limits give candidate QFTs.

  • Griffiths–Simon: \(\varphi^4\) spins as limits of normalised sums of ferromagnetically coupled Ising spins; critical 4D Ising and \(\varphi^4_4\) have Gaussian scaling limits (ADC 2021).

Renormalisation already appears

For \(V = \lambda a^4\) and fixed \((m, \lambda)\) the limit is trivial: \(c_{\varepsilon} =\mathbb{E} [\varphi_u^2] = \frac{1}{2 \pi} \log \frac{1}{\varepsilon m} + O (1) \to \infty\). Take \(V_{\varepsilon} (a) = \lambda (a^4 - 6 c_{\varepsilon} a^2)\), i.e. \(\lambda : a^4\): up to a constant. Enough in \(d = 2\); not in \(d = 3\).

Path integrals

Formally, as \(\varepsilon \to 0\) and \(L, T \to \infty\):

\(\displaystyle \mu (\mathrm{d} \varphi) \backprime\backprime = "Z^{- 1} \exp \left( - \int_{\mathbb{R}^d} \left[ \tfrac{1}{2} | \nabla \varphi |^2 + \tfrac{1}{2} m^2 \varphi^2 + V (\varphi) \right] \mathrm{d} x \right) \mathcal{D} \varphi .\)

  • Neither \(\mathcal{D} \varphi\) nor the action make sense: typical \(\varphi\) are distributions.

  • The Gaussian part defines the Gaussian free field \(\mu_m\) (next lecture).

  • Then \(\mu \propto \mathrm{e}^{- \int V_{\text{ren}} (\varphi)} \mu_m\): meaningful in finite volume for \(d = 2\) (and, with more work, \(d = 3\)).

Question. Which measures on \(\mathcal{S}' (\mathbb{R}^d)\) correspond to relativistic QFTs?

Osterwalder–Schrader axioms

\(\hat{\mu} (f) = \int \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} \varphi (f)} \mu (\mathrm{d} \varphi)\),\(\Theta (\tau, \textbf{x}) = (- \tau, \textbf{x})\).

OS0

Analyticity: \(z \mapsto \hat{\mu} (\sum_j z_j f_j)\) entire.

OS1

Regularity: \(| \hat{\mu} (f) | \leqslant \exp (c (\|f\|_{L^1} +\|f\|^p_{L^p}))\), \(p \in [1, 2]\), complex \(f\).

OS2

Euclidean invariance.

OS3

Reflection positivity: for real \(f_i\) supported in \(\{\tau > 0\}\), \((\hat{\mu} (f_i - \Theta f_j))_{ij} \geqslant 0\).

OS4

Ergodicity under time translations.

Theorem. (Osterwalder–Schrader 1973/75) OS0–OS3 \(\Rightarrow\) the moments of \(\mu\) are the Schwinger functions of a unique Wightman theory; OS4 \(\Leftrightarrow\) unique vacuum.

Reconstruction: the idea

  • \(\mathcal{E}_+ =\) functions of \(\varphi |_{\{\tau > 0\}}\), with \((F, G) =\mathbb{E}_{\mu} [\overline{\Theta F} \hspace{0.17em} G] \geqslant 0\) by OS3.

  • Quotient by null vectors and complete: physical Hilbert space \(\mathcal{H}\).

  • Time translations by \(t \geqslant 0\) preserve \(\mathcal{E}_+\) and descend to a self-adjoint contraction semigroup \(\mathrm{e}^{- tH}\): positivity of the energy is recovered from reflection positivity.

  • Vacuum \(=\) class of \(1\); analytic continuation \(\tau \to \mathrm{i} t\) gives the Wightman functions.

The Euclidean strategy

(1) regularise (lattice, torus); (2) renormalise; (3) uniform estimates, remove cutoffs; (4) verify OS axioms (RP survives limits, Euclidean invariance recovered, regularity, clustering); (5) prove non-Gaussianity.
