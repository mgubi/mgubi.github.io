Where we are

At each lattice \(N\), exactly (Lecture 5):

\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned} \mathrm{d} Y_q & = [b_q (Y_q) + H_q (Y_q) Z_q] \mathrm{d} q + H_q (Y_q) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W_q,\\ \mathrm{d} Z_q & = - [b_q (Y_q) + H_q (Y_q) Z_q] \mathrm{d} q + \Xi_q \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W_q, \end{aligned} \qquad \psi = Y + Z = \int \text{Cov} \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W.\)

Inputs.

  1. Coercivity: \(H_q (y) \asymp (K_q + \gamma y^2)^{- 1}\), \(\|H_q \|_{p \to p} \le (1 + \varepsilon_q) / q\), \(\varepsilon_q = C (g_q + g_q^2)\).

  2. Source: \(\|b^{\text{b}}_q \|_{\infty} \le C \gamma^{7 / 2} q^{- 5 / 2}\), \(\|b^{\text{l}}_q (y)\|_2^2 \le C \gamma^4 q^{- 5} \|t_q (y)\|_6^6\).

  3. Coordinates: \(K_q (Y_q + \varrho_q (Y_q)) + \frac{\gamma}{3} Y_q^3 = P_q\), \(\|D \varrho_q \|_{p \to p} \le \varepsilon_q\).

Goal

\(Z\) small and \(Y\) rarely at large fields, uniformly in \(N\). Neither equation can be estimated alone.

How FBSDE estimates usually work, and why not here

Usual (Ma–Yong): Lipschitz coefficients, Itô on \(\|Z\|^2\) and Gronwall; close on a short interval or under monotonicity.

Here: infinite interval, coefficients not uniformly Lipschitz, the coupling is not small. Instead we use structure:

  • the backward equation is linear in \(Z\) given \(Y\): a conditional representation with an explicit propagator;

  • the forward equation is, by the field equation of Lecture 4, a cubic elliptic equation at each resolution: coercive, estimated pathwise;

  • the two estimates are linked by two weighted energies with matched weights.

At fixed \(N, T\) all moments are finite (finite dimensions): used only to justify absorptions. Ma–Yong 1999

The energies

Lecture 6

The noise of the field equation

Lecture 5: \(P_q = (q - q_0) \Phi_{\infty} + B_{q - q_0}\), a noisy measurement. Since \(\psi_q =\mathbb{E}_q \Phi_{\infty}\),

\(\displaystyle \eta_q := P_q - (q - q_0) \psi_q =\mathbb{E}_q [B_{q - q_0}] :\)

the noise observable of Lecture 4 is the filtered measurement noise.

Lemma. For convex \(\Psi\), \(\mathbb{E} \Psi (\eta_q) \leq \mathbb{E} \Psi (B_{q - q_0})\), whatever the interaction. So \(\zeta_q = C_q \eta_q\) has

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E} \mathrm{e}^{u \zeta_q (x)} \leq \exp \left( \frac{u^2}{2} (q - q_0) a^2 (q) \right), \qquad (q - q_0) a^2 (q) \lesssim \sqrt{q} .\)

  • Proof: conditional Jensen. One line.

  • \(\zeta\) is not Gaussian, but it has the tails of the free field, uniformly in \(N\) and in the interaction.

  • Dudley + Doob: \(\sup_q q^{- 1 / 4} (\log (2 + q))^{- 1 / 2} \| \zeta_q \|_{\infty}\) has Gaussian tails.

The field equation

Insert \(P_q = \eta_q + (q - q_0) (Y_q + Z_q)\) into the coordinate equation (C), with \(K_q = K_{q_0} + q - q_0\). With \(V_q = Y_q + \varrho_q (Y_q) - \zeta_q\):

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{|l|} \hline \hspace{0.27em} K_{q_0} V_q + \frac{\gamma}{3} Y_q^3 = (q - q_0) (\zeta_q - \varrho_q (Y_q) + Z_q), \qquad Y_q = V_q + \zeta_q - \varrho_q (Y_q) . \hspace{0.27em}\\ \hline \end{array}\)

  • The field equation of Lecture 4 in these coordinates: elliptic at each \(q\), with no memory. The past enters only through the noise.

  • The backward equation enters only through \((q - q_0) Z_q\), at the same resolution.

  • The mass is only \(q_0\): at large \(q\) all the coercivity must come from the cube.

  • The identity is pathwise.

Exercise 13.3: subtracting the Gaussian field \(X = \int C \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W\) instead gives an equation with memory.

Thresholds and energies

\(\displaystyle w_q = \text{sgn} (V_q) \left( |V_q | - \tfrac{3}{4} r_q \right)_+, \qquad t_q = Y_q \mathbb{1}_{|Y_q | > r_q}, \qquad \xi_q = \zeta_q \mathbb{1}_{| \zeta_q | > r_q / 32} .\)
\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned} E_T & = \tfrac{1}{\gamma} \int q^{1 / 2} \|Z_q \|_2^2 \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} q \qquad \text{backward energy}\\ J_T & = \int q^{- 3 / 2} [q\|w_q \|_4^4 + \gamma \|w_q \|_6^6 + \gamma \|t_q \|_6^6] \mathrm{d} q \qquad \text{large-field action}\\ G_T & = \int [q^{- 1 / 2} \| \xi_q \|_4^4 + \gamma q^{- 3 / 2} \| \xi_q \|_6^6] \mathrm{d} q \qquad \text{noise tails} \end{aligned}\)

quantity weight reason
\(\|Z_q \|^2\) \(q^{1 / 2}\) forcing: \(q^{- 3 / 2} \cdot \gamma^{- 1} q^2 \|Z\|_2^2\)
\(\gamma \|t_q \|_6^6\) \(q^{- 3 / 2}\) \(q^{7 / 2} \|b^{\text{l}} \|^2 \le C \gamma^3 \cdot \gamma q^{- 3 / 2} \|t\|_6^6\)
\(\|b^{\text{l}}_q \|^2\) \(q^{7 / 2}\) propagator \(s / q\), \(\int_q^{\infty} s^{- 3 / 2} = 2 q^{- 1 / 2}\)

Only thresholded sixth powers: unthresholded, \(\mathbb{E} \| \zeta_q \|_6^6\) can be of order \(q^{3 / 2}\) (free case), so \(\int \gamma q^{- 3 / 2} \mathbb{E} \| \zeta_q \|_6^6\) can reach \(\gamma N^2\), not uniform in \(N\).

Forward coercivity

Lecture 6

The scalar model

Lemma. (scalar forward coercivity) If \(a \geq 0\), \(0 \leq p \leq q\) and \(av + \frac{\gamma}{3} (v + g)^3 = p (g + z)\), then, with \(r = (q / \gamma)^{1 / 2}\),

\(\displaystyle \frac{q}{8} w^4 + \frac{\gamma}{16} w^6 \leq 54 q \xi^4 + \frac{4225}{6} \gamma \xi^6 + \frac{12}{\gamma} q^2 z^2,\)

where \(w =\operatorname{sgn} (v) (|v| - \frac{3}{4} r)_+\), \(\xi = g \mathbb{1}_{|g| > r / 32}\); and \(|t| \leq \frac{32}{7} (|w| + | \xi |)\).

  • The excess is controlled at each resolution separately by the noise tails and by the error \(Z\).

  • No smallness of the coupling, and no integration in \(q\): multiply by \(q^{- 3 / 2}\) only to match the energies.

Proof: test with \(w^3\)

Where \(w \neq 0\): \(|v| = |w| + \frac{3}{4} r\), same sign, so \(avw^3 \geq 0\).

  • Cube. \(\operatorname{sgn} (w) (v + g) \geq |w| + \frac{11}{16} r - | \xi |\), and \(\gamma r^2 = q\):

    \(\displaystyle \frac{\gamma}{3} w^3 (v + g)^3 \geq \frac{\gamma}{12} w^6 + \frac{q}{4} w^4 + \frac{qr}{13} |w|^3 - \frac{4225}{6} \gamma \xi^6 .\)

  • Noise. \(pgw^3 \leq \frac{qr}{32} |w|^3 + q | \xi | |w|^3\): the first term is absorbed by the cube, and \(q | \xi | |w|^3 \leq \frac{q}{8} w^4 + 54 q \xi^4\).

  • Error. \(q |z| |w|^3 \leq \frac{\gamma}{48} w^6 + \frac{12}{\gamma} q^2 z^2\).

Above the threshold the cube provides a sixth power, a mass \(q\) and a cubic term which dominates the noise below the threshold.

Two elementary inequalities

For \(x, \rho, u, r \geq 0\):

\(\displaystyle (x - \rho)^3 \geq \frac{3}{4} x^3 - 65 \rho^3,\)
\(\displaystyle \frac{1}{3} u^3 \left( u + \frac{11}{16} r - \rho \right)^3 \geq \frac{1}{12} u^6 + \frac{1}{4} r^2 u^4 + \frac{1}{13} r^3 u^3 - \frac{4225}{6} \rho^6 .\)

Proof. \(\frac{1}{4} x^3 + 64 \rho^3 - 3 x^2 \rho = \frac{1}{4} (x - 8 \rho)^2 (x + 4 \rho) \geq 0\). Then \(x = u + b\), \(b = \frac{11}{16} r\): \(\frac{3}{4} b^2 > \frac{1}{4} r^2\), \(\frac{1}{4} b^3 > \frac{1}{13} r^3\), and \(\frac{65}{3} u^3 \rho^3 \leq \frac{1}{6} u^6 + \frac{4225}{6} \rho^6\).

The lattice version

Proposition. (forward coercivity) For \(q_0\) large, at every \(N\), pathwise and for every \(q\),

\(\displaystyle q \|w_q \|_4^4 + \gamma \|w_q \|_6^6 + \gamma \|t_q \|_6^6 \leq C (q\| \xi_q \|_4^4 + \gamma \| \xi_q \|_6^6 + \gamma^{- 1} q^2 \|Z_q \|_2^2) .\)

So \(J_T \leq C (G_T + E_T)\), and \(\|G_T \|_{L^p} \leq C \gamma^3 p^5\).

  • Elliptic term: \(w^3 = \phi' (V)\), \(\phi\) convex, so \(\langle w^3, K_{q_0} V \rangle \geq 0\).

  • Coordinate correction: \(\varrho = \varrho^0 + \varrho^1\), \(\| \varrho^0 \|_{\infty} \leq \varepsilon r\) shifts the threshold, \(\| \varrho^1 \|_j \leq C \varepsilon (\|w\|_j +\| \xi \|_j)\) is absorbed.

  • Noise tails: \(\mathbb{P} (| \zeta_q (x) | > r_q / 32) \leq 2 \mathrm{e}^{- c \sqrt{q} / \gamma}\), by the lemma.

\(L^{\infty}\) coercivity

Proposition. For \(q_0\) large, at every \(N\), pathwise and for every \(q\),

\(\displaystyle \|Y_q \|_{\infty} \leq C \left[ \left( \frac{q}{\gamma} \right)^{1 / 3} \| \zeta_q \|_{\infty}^{1 / 3} +\| \zeta_q \|_{\infty} + q^{1 / 4} + q\|Z_q \|_2 \right] .\)

So \(t_q = 0\) whenever \(\| \zeta_q \|_{\infty} + q \|Z_q \|_2 \leq cr_q\).

  • Remove the error: \(\mathcal{H}= (q - q_0) C_{q_0} Z_q\), with \(\|\mathcal{H}\|_{\infty} \lesssim q \|Z_q \|_2\).

  • At a maximum of \(W = V -\mathcal{H}\), the elliptic term has a sign: \(\frac{\gamma}{3} Y^3 \leq q \|d\|_{\infty}\). The cube alone gives the bound.

  • Large fields occur only when the noise or the error is large.

  • With \(\| \zeta_q \|_{\infty} \lesssim q^{1 / 4}\) and \(\|Z_q \|_2 \lesssim q^{- 5 / 4}\) (Doob on \(\mathbb{E}_q A^{\text{l}}\)): \(\|Y_q \|_{\infty} \lesssim q^{5 / 12} \ll r_q\), up to logarithms.

Backward feedback and closure

Lecture 6

The backward representation

Propagator: \(\partial_s \mathcal{P}_{q, s} =\mathcal{P}_{q, s} H_s (Y_s)\), \(\mathcal{P}_{q, q} = I\), and by (A)

\(\displaystyle \|\mathcal{P}_{q, s} \| \le L_0 \hspace{0.17em} \frac{s}{q}, \qquad L_0 = \exp \left( \int_{q_0}^{\infty} \varepsilon_u \frac{\mathrm{d} u}{u} \right) \le \mathrm{e}^{C (g_{q_0} + g_{q_0}^2)} .\)

Lemma. (conditional representation)

\(\displaystyle Z_q =\mathbb{E}_q \left[ \mathcal{P}_{q, T} Z_T + \int_q^T \mathcal{P}_{q, s} \hspace{0.17em} b_s (Y_s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s \right] .\)

Proof: \(\mathrm{d}_s (\mathcal{P}_{q, s} Z_s) = -\mathcal{P}_{q, s} b_s \mathrm{d} s +\mathcal{P}_{q, s} \Xi_s \mathrm{d} W_s\), a true martingale at fixed \(N\).

\(Z\) = expected source on the unresolved scales, transported back, plus the boundary error. Compare \(F_t =\mathbb{E}_t [F_T]\) in Lecture 4: here the source is the defect.

Three contributions

Bounded fields (two correction steps!):

\(\displaystyle \|Z^{\text{b}}_q \| \le \int_q^{\infty} L_0 \frac{s}{q} \hspace{0.17em} C \gamma^{7 / 2} s^{- 5 / 2} \mathrm{d} s = 2 CL_0 \gamma^{7 / 2} q^{- 3 / 2}, \qquad \tfrac{1}{\gamma} \int q^{1 / 2} \|Z^{\text{b}} \|^2 \le C \gamma^6 q_0^{- 3 / 2} .\)

Boundary: \(q \|Z^{\text{t}}_q \| \le L_0 \mathbb{E}_q \|TZ_T \|\), contributing \(\frac{2 L_0^2}{\gamma q_0^{1 / 2}} \delta_{N, T, p}\), \(\delta_{N, T, p} = \|TZ_T \|^2_{L^{2 p} (\mathbb{P}; L^2)}\).

Large fields: \(A^{\text{l}} = \int s^{7 / 2} \|b^{\text{l}}_s \|^2 \le C \gamma^3 J_T\); Cauchy–Schwarz and Jensen,

\(\displaystyle \|Z^{\text{l}}_q \|^2 \le 2 L_0^2 q^{- 5 / 2} \mathbb{E}_q A^{\text{l}}, \qquad \left| \tfrac{1}{\gamma} \int q^{1 / 2} \|Z^{\text{l}} \|^2 \right|_{L^p} \le \frac{C \gamma^2}{q_0} \|J_T \|_{L^p} .\)

This is how the clipping error is controlled

\(b^{\text{l}}\) is not small, but it enters the backward energy only through the large-field action \(J_T\), with the small factor \(\gamma^2 / q_0\).

Closing the loop

Theorem. (cutoff-uniform energy estimate) One \(q_0 = q_0 (\gamma)\): for all \(N\), \(T\), \(p \ge 1\),

\(\displaystyle \|E_T \|_{L^p} + \|J_T \|_{L^p} \le C_{\gamma} (1 + p^5 + \delta_{N, T, p}) .\)

\(x = \|E_T \|_p\), \(y = \|J_T \|_p\):\(y \le A_0 (C \gamma^3 p^5 + x)\), \(x \le B_0 + B_1 \delta + \eta y\), \(\eta = C \gamma^2 / q_0 = Cg_{q_0}^2\). Choose \(A_0 \eta \le \frac{1}{2}\); \(y < \infty\) at fixed \(N, T\), absorb.

The smallness is in the feedback, independent of \(p\): one \(q_0\) for all \(p\); the growth \(p^5\) is inherited from \(G_T\).

Consequences

Once the boundary term is removed (Section 16):

\(\displaystyle \sup_N \|E^N_{\infty} \|_{L^p} + \sup_N \|J^N_{\infty} \|_{L^p} \le C_{\gamma} (1 + p^5), \qquad \sup_N \mathbb{E} \|Z^N_q \|_2^2 \le C_{\gamma} q^{- 5 / 2},\)

and \(\mathbb{E} \exp \{c (E_{\infty} + J_{\infty})^{1 / 5} \} < \infty\).

  • The approximate forecast differs from the exact one by \(O (q^{- 5 / 4})\) in \(L^2 (\mathbb{P}; L^2)\) (root mean square), uniformly in the lattice.

  • \(H_q Z_q = O (q^{- 9 / 4})\) in \(L^2 (\mathbb{P}; L^2)\): up to an integrable drift, the forward equation is the explicit SDE \(\mathrm{d} Y = b (Y) \mathrm{d} q + H (Y) \mathrm{d} W\).

Removing the cutoffs

So far, at every lattice \(N\) and upper scale \(T\):

\(\displaystyle \|E_T \|_{L^p} + \|J_T \|_{L^p} \le C_{\gamma} (1 + p^5 + \delta_{N, T, p}), \qquad \delta_{N, T, p} = \|TZ_T \|^2_{L^{2 p} (\mathbb{P}; L^2)} .\)

Three things remain:

  1. the boundary term vanishes: \(TZ_T \to 0\) at fixed \(N\);

  2. uniform energy bounds \(\Rightarrow\) compactness of processes and measures;

  3. identify the limit: coefficients, noise, equations.

The subtle point

Convergence of the fields does not pass an FBSDE to the limit: the backward martingale could acquire extra noise. The statistical meaning of the backward variable saves us.

The boundary at infinite resolution

Lecture 6

The boundary lemma

\(\bar{\Gamma}_q\) was built to converge to the bare action: \(\mu_q \to \mu_N\), \(\Theta_q \to 0\), corrections integrated from \(q = \infty\).

Lemma. (fixed \(N\), constants depend on \(N\))

\(\displaystyle \|Y_q - \Phi_{\infty} \|_{L^p} + \|qZ_q \|_{L^p} \le C_{N, p} \hspace{0.17em} q^{- 1 / 2}, \qquad \text{hence} \qquad \delta_{N, T, p} \le C_{N, p} T^{- 1} .\)

  • The field at resolution \(q\): \(\Phi_q = C_q P_q = (q - q_0) C_q \Phi_{\infty} + C_q B_{q - q_0} \to \Phi_{\infty}\).

  • \(\Phi_q = Y_q + C_q D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q)\), so \(Y_q - \Phi_q = O (q^{- 1})\) and \(D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q) \to DV_N (\Phi_{\infty})\).

  • Exact minus approximate coordinate identity:

    \(\displaystyle qZ_q = qC_q [D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q) -\mathbb{E}_q DV_N (\Phi_{\infty})] ;\)

    compare \(\mathbb{E}_q G\) with the observed \(G^0_q = DV_N (\Phi_q)\): \(\|\mathbb{E}_q G - G\| \le 2 \|G - G^0_q \|\).

No uniform estimate, no posterior concentration.

UV stability

Let \(T \to \infty\) at fixed \(N\) (paths and \(\nu_N\) do not depend on \(T\); monotone convergence; \(\delta_{N, T, p} \to 0\)). Only then take \(\sup_N\).

Theorem. (UV stability) For \(q_0 = q_0 (\gamma)\) large,

\(\displaystyle \sup_N (\|E^N_{\infty} \|_{L^p} +\|J^N_{\infty} \|_{L^p}) \le C_{\gamma} (1 + p^5), \qquad \sup_N \mathbb{E} \|Z^N_q \|_2^2 \le C_{\gamma} q^{- 5 / 2},\)

and \(\sup_N \mathbb{E} \exp \{c (E^N_{\infty} + J^N_{\infty})^{1 / 5} \} < \infty\).

  • \(C_{N, p}\) diverges with \(N\): never take \(\sup_N\) of \(C_{N, p} / T\).

  • The force: \(F_q =\mathbb{E}_q [- DV_N (\Phi_{\infty})] = K_q Z_q - D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q)\), the explicit force at the approximate forecast, corrected by \(K_q Z_q\).

Compactness

Lecture 6

Suprema by a maximum principle

Embed by cells \(\square_x\): \(\iota_N : H_N \to H = L^2 (\mathbb{T}^3)\) isometric, \(j_N = \iota_N^{\ast}\).

Lemma. (supremum after closure) Let \(\mathcal{Y}= \sup_q q^{- 5 / 12} (\log (2 + q))^{- 1 / 6} \|Y_q \|_{\infty}\), \(\mathcal{Z}= \sup_q q^{5 / 4} \|Z_q \|_2\). Uniformly in \(N\),

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{Y} \leq C_{\gamma} (1 +\mathcal{N}+\mathcal{Z}), \qquad \|\mathcal{N}\|_{L^p} \leq C \sqrt{p}, \qquad \|\mathcal{Z}^2 \|_{L^p} \leq C_{\gamma} (1 + p^5) .\)

  • From the \(L^{\infty}\) coercivity above: lattice maximum principle in the field equation, no memory.

  • \(\mathcal{N}\): supremum of the noise (convex domination, Dudley, Doob). \(\mathcal{Z}\): Doob on \(\mathbb{E}_q A^{\text{l}}\), after the boundary term is removed.

  • The exponent \(5 / 12 < \frac{1}{2}\) keeps \(Y\) below the threshold.

Finite-resolution suprema have all moments, uniformly in \(N\). At the terminal resolution there is no such bound: \(\Phi_{\infty}\) is a distribution.

Regularity and the terminal field

Space. \(K_{q_0} V_q = f_q\) with \(\mathbb{E} \int_I \|f\|_2^2 \le C_I\) (energies), so \(V \in L^2 (I ; H^1)\); \(Y = V + \zeta - \varrho (Y)\) has the regularity of the noise, that of the free field: \(H^{\sigma}\), \(\sigma < \frac{1}{2}\), in space; \(H^{\theta}\), \(\theta < \frac{1}{2}\), in scale; tight in space–time \(L^p\) (and in \(C (I ; H)\): \(C^{1 / 4}\) Hölder bound \(+\) uniform Fourier tails).

Terminal field. \(H_q^2 \le \frac{C}{q} C_q\) gives, for \(\frac{1}{2} < s < \frac{3}{2}\),

\(\displaystyle \int_Q^{\infty} \|(1 - \Delta)^{- s / 2} H_q \|_{\text{HS}}^2 \mathrm{d} q \le C \int_Q^{\infty} \frac{1}{q} \sum_k \frac{(1 + |k|^2)^{- s}}{q + c |k|^2} \mathrm{d} q \le C_s Q^{- (s - \frac{1}{2})},\)

and the drifts have squared tails \(O (Q^{- 3})\), \(O (Q^{- 5 / 2})\), \(O (Q^{- 3 / 2})\). Doob:

\(\displaystyle \sup_N \mathbb{E} \sup_{t \ge Q} \|Y_t - Y_Q \|_{H^{- s}}^2 \le C_s Q^{- (s - \frac{1}{2})} .\)

Corollary. \(\nu_N\) is tight in \(H^{- s} (\mathbb{T}^3)\) for every \(s > \frac{1}{2}\), with all moments uniformly in \(N\). (Threshold of the free field in \(d = 3\).)

Identifying the limit

Lecture 6

The deterministic coefficients

Uniform bounds do not identify a limit: a weakly convergent vertex cannot in general be contracted against a varying covariance. Three tools:

  • Weak vertex measures: bounded rooted norms \(\Rightarrow\) signed measures, weakly compact; the centred kernel in a first-moment topology. The limits solve the same fixed point, unique in its ball: the whole sequence converges.

  • Compact sandwich: \(G_N \to G\) in \(\text{HS}\), \(\textsf{B}_N \to \textsf{B}\) weakly, symmetric, uniformly bounded, \(\sup_N \|G_N \textsf{B}_N \| \le \theta < 1\), \(\|G \textsf{B} \| \le \theta\) \(\Rightarrow\) \((I + G_N \textsf{B}_N)^{- 1} G_N \to (I + G \textsf{B})^{- 1} G\) in \(\text{HS}\).

  • Contraction against a moving covariance: rows of the inverses are Hölder \(+\) HS convergence \(\Rightarrow\) \((H^N)^2 \to H^2\) uniformly; weak vertices can be contracted.

Hence \(H^N_q (Y^N_q) \to H_q (Y_q)\) in \(L^2 (I ; \text{HS})\) and \(b^N_q (Y^N_q) \to b_q (Y_q)\) in \(L^2 (I ; H)\).

Hidden noise

\(L^n_t = \int_0^t g_n (s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} B_s\), \(g_n = 1 + \sin (ns)\): integrands \(\to 1\) weakly, squares \(\to \frac{3}{2}\).

\(\displaystyle (L^n, B) \Longrightarrow (B + 2^{- 1 / 2} \tilde{B}, \hspace{0.27em} B), \qquad \tilde{B} \perp B.\)

Left: \((B_1, L^n_1 - B_1)\), \(n = 200\): correlation \(\approx 0\), variance of the gap \(\approx \frac{1}{2}\) — the residual does not vanish and is orthogonal to \(B\). Right: \(\int_0^t g_n \to t\) but \(\int_0^t g_n^2 \to \frac{3}{2} t\).

A limiting FBSDE obtained this way would carry an extra martingale orthogonal to the driving noise. We need strong compactness of the backward integrand \(\Xi = \text{Cov} - H (Y)\).

The Riccati equation of the posterior covariance

Lemma. \(\text{Cov}_q = D^2 \mathcal{K}_q (P_q) \ge 0\) and

\(\displaystyle \text{dCov}_q = - \text{Cov}_q^2 \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} q + D^3 \mathcal{K}_q (P_q) [\mathrm{d} W_q]\)

Lecture 4: conditional variance is resolved at rate \(\text{Cov}_t \dot{\mathcal{R}}_t \text{Cov}_t\); here \(\partial_q \mathcal{R}= 1\).

  • \(\langle f, \text{Cov}_q f \rangle \ge 0\): a nonnegative supermartingale, initial value \(\text{Var}_{\nu_N} \langle \Phi_{\infty}, f \rangle\) bounded uniformly (uniform moments, Corollary).

  • Stop at level \(K\): decreasing part and bracket have bounded expectations \(\Rightarrow\) time regularity \(W^{a, 1}\), \(a < \frac{1}{2}\) \(\Rightarrow\) strong compactness of each finite block.

  • Spatial tails: isometry \(+\) symmetry, and terminal averaging \(\psi_Q =\mathbb{E}_Q \int_L^{L + 1} (Y_s + Z_s) \mathrm{d} s\) (\(H^{\sigma}\) bound on \(Y\), \(\mathbb{E} \|Z_s \|^2 \le Cs^{- 5 / 2}\)).

\(\Rightarrow\) \(\text{Cov}^N\) tight in strong \(L^2 (I ; \text{HS})\); brackets \(\int \text{CovCov}^{\ast}\) and covariations with \(W\) converge.

Identification of the martingales

On a common filtered space (Skorokhod), along a subsequence:

  • \(\psi\) is a continuous square-integrable martingale for the joint filtration; its bracket and its covariation with \(W\) are those of \(\int \text{Cov} \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W\);

  • so \(\psi - \int \text{Cov} \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W\) has zero bracket and vanishes: no extra martingale;

  • same for the forward equation, and \(X = \int C \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W\) with the same \(W\).

\(\displaystyle \begin{aligned} Y_q & = \int_{q_0}^q [b (Y) + H (Y) Z] \mathrm{d} s + \int_{q_0}^q H (Y) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W,\\ Z_q & = - \int_{q_0}^q [b (Y) + H (Y) Z] \mathrm{d} s + \int_{q_0}^q \Xi \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W, \qquad X_q = \int_{q_0}^q C_s \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W_s . \end{aligned}\)
The result (I): convergence and the limiting FBSDE

Theorem. (subsequential construction) For \(\lambda > 0\), \(q_0\) large, along a subsequence of lattices:

  1. \((Y^N, Z^N, \psi^N, X^N)\), with \(X^N = \int C \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W^N\), converge in \(C (I ; H)\), \(W^N\) in \(C (I ; H^{- r})\), \(r > \frac{3}{2}\), \((\text{Cov}^N, \Xi^N)\) in strong \(L^2 (I ; \text{HS})\), jointly, a.s. on a new space;

  2. the limit solves the FBSDE on \([q_0, \infty)\) with the continuum coefficients \(b, H\) (unique in their ball), an \(H\)-cylindrical Brownian motion obtained as the limit of the lattice innovations, \(\text{Cov} = H (Y) + \Xi \ge 0\), and \(\mathbb{E} \|Z_q \|^2 \le Cq^{- 5 / 2}\);

\(\psi^N\) is tight in \(C (I ; H)\) because its brackets \(\int \text{Cov}^N \text{Cov}^{N \ast}\) are tight and its Fourier tails are uniformly small.

The result (II): the terminal law

Theorem. (subsequential construction, continued)

  1. \(Y_q \to \Phi\) in \(H^{- s}\), \(s > \frac{1}{2}\), a.s. and in mean square; \(\text{Law} (\Phi) = \nu\) is the weak limit of \(\nu_N\), and \(\langle Y_q + Z_q, h \rangle =\mathbb{E} [\langle \Phi, h \rangle \hspace{0.17em} | \hspace{0.17em} \mathcal{F}_q]\);

  2. the limiting thresholded energies (built with \(\zeta_q = C_q (W_q + \int \psi - (q - q_0) \psi_q)\)) obey the bounds of UV stability.

The posterior structure survives: \(\psi = Y + Z\) is a closed observable, \(Y\) is computed from the explicit action, \(Z\) is small at high resolution.

The renormalised field equation

Theorem. (integration by parts) \(G (\varphi) = g (\langle \varphi, h_1 \rangle, \ldots, \langle \varphi, h_k \rangle)\), \(g \in C_b^{\infty}\), \(h, h_i\) smooth:

\(\displaystyle \lim_N \mathbb{E} [G (\iota_N \Phi^N_{\infty}) \langle \mu_N \Phi^N_{\infty} + \lambda (\Phi^N_{\infty})^3, j_N h \rangle]\)

\(\displaystyle =\mathbb{E}_{\nu} [D_h G (\Phi) - G (\Phi) \langle \Phi, (q_0 - \Delta) h \rangle] .\)

Proof: Gaussian integration by parts in \(\mathrm{e}^{- V_N - \frac{1}{2} \langle \varphi, K_{q_0} \varphi \rangle}\); tightness and uniform moments.

  • The terminal case of the DS equations of Lecture 4.

  • The renormalised cube \(\mu_N \Phi + \lambda \Phi^3\) is identified only tested in expectation, not as a random distribution.

  • Limiting force without terminal representative: not proved.

  • At every finite resolution the force is explicit: \(F_q = K_q Z_q - D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q)\).

The field equation at resolution \(q\)

In the limit: \(\Phi_q = C_q P_q\), \(\eta_q = P_q - (q - q_0) \psi_q\), \(F_q = K_q \psi_q - P_q\), \(J_q = (q - q_0) C_q \to I\).

Theorem. For every \(q\), with no renormalisation constant, pathwise as distributions,

\(\displaystyle K_{q_0} \Phi_q = J_q F_q + \eta_q, \qquad \mathbb{E}D_{J_q h} G (\Phi_q) =\mathbb{E} [G (\Phi_q) \langle \eta_q, h \rangle] .\)

\(\langle F, h \rangle\), \(\langle \eta, h \rangle\) are martingales, and \(\| \langle F_q + D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q), h \rangle \|_{L^2} \lesssim \|h\|_{H^2} q^{- 1 / 4}\).

  • Proof of DS: \(\eta_q =\mathbb{E}_q B_{q - q_0}\) and \(\Phi_q = J_q \Phi_{\infty} + C_q B_{q - q_0}\): Gaussian integration by parts in \(B\), then \(N \to \infty\).

  • The renormalised cube is an observable: the unique martingale in the class of the explicit \(- D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q)\).

  • \(q \to \infty\) recovers the integration by parts formula. Not proved: that \(F\) is closed.

Exponential integrability: the tilt

Hairer–Steele. \(\hat{\nu} =\mathcal{Z}^{- 1} \mathrm{e}^W \nu\). For every set \(A\):

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{Z} \le \exp (\sup_A W) / \hat{\nu} (A) .\)

It is enough that the tilted measure gives mass \(\frac{1}{2}\) to a fixed set. They use the dynamics: for \(W = \frac{\beta}{4} \langle \varphi, \psi \rangle^4\) the force \(\beta \psi \langle \Phi, \psi \rangle^3\) is below the cube, and coming down from infinity is uniform.

In the filter. Same channel, same \(\bar{\Gamma}\), prior \(\hat{\nu}_N\):

  • the same \(b\), \(H\), the same noise bound, the same forward and backward estimates;

  • only the boundary changes: \(T \hat{Z}_T \to DW (\hat{\Phi})\), so

\(\displaystyle \| \hat{E}_{\infty} \|_{L^p} + \| \hat{J}_{\infty} \|_{L^p} \le C_{\gamma} (1 + p^5 + \|DW (\hat{\Phi})\|_{L^{2 p} (L^2)}^2) .\)
Closing: the scale adapts to the level

Test the coordinate equation with \(\psi\), \(X = \langle \hat{\Phi}, \psi \rangle\), \(P_q = (q - q_0) \hat{\Phi} + B\):

\(\displaystyle |X| \le C (r_q + \|t_q \|_6 + \frac{\gamma}{q} \|t_q \|_6^3 +\mathcal{N}_{\psi}) .\)

Choose \(Q\) with \(r_Q \sim |X|\), so \(Q \sim \gamma X^2\): then \(\|t_q \|_6 \gtrsim |X|\) on \([Q / 2, Q]\), and

\(\displaystyle |X|^5 \lesssim 1 +\mathcal{N}_{\psi}^5 + \hat{J}_{\infty} .\)

So \(W = \beta (1 + X^2)^{7 / 4}\) closes for small \(\beta\), uniformly in \(N\):

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E} \exp (\beta | \langle \Phi, \psi \rangle |^{7 / 2}) \le C, \quad \mathbb{E} \exp (\beta \| \Phi \|_{H^{- s}}^2) \le C \quad (s > \frac{1}{2}) .\)

Not quartic: on one site \(\hat{J} \sim |X|^5\) is sharp, while \(\|DW\|^2 \sim \beta^2 |X|^6\). The backward bound \(s / q\) ignores that \(H (Y)\) is small on large fields.

Scale-by-scale DS and uniqueness

DS along the scales. For \(G = g (\langle \Phi_{q_1}, h_1 \rangle, \ldots, \langle \Phi_{q_k}, h_k \rangle)\), \(q_i \le q\):

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E} \sum_i \partial_i g \langle J_{q_i} h, h_i \rangle =\mathbb{E}[G \langle \eta_q, h \rangle] =\mathbb{E}[G (\langle \Phi_q, K_{q_0} h \rangle + \langle D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q), J_q h \rangle)] + O (q^{- 1 / 4}) .\)

Uniqueness in finite volume, conditional (as in Hairer–Steele, Thm 2.2). \(\nu_N\) is invariant for the lattice equation with \(\mu_N = m_N + c_N\). Assume lattice convergence, ergodicity and tightness in our setting: if \(c_N \to c\), then \(\nu_N \to \pi_c\), the unique invariant measure.

  • The literature covers dyadic grids, unit coefficients and a fixed finite part; our odd \(N\) and varying \(c_N\) are assumed extensions.

  • If \(\nu = \pi_c\), quartic tails hold: \(\mathbb{E}_{\nu} \exp (\beta \langle \Phi, \psi \rangle^4) < \infty\) (Hairer–Steele).

  • Not proved here: convergence of \(c_N\) (lattice sums); uniqueness for the FBSDE as an equation; an intrinsic proof.

What is not proved

  • Uniqueness for the FBSDE as an equation; convergence of the finite part of the counterterm; an intrinsic proof.

  • Small mass / strong coupling: the construction needs \(\gamma q_0^{- 1 / 2}\) small, i.e. weak coupling \(\lambda / m \ll 1\). Removing it needs a priori estimates without the small feedback \(\gamma^2 / q_0\), and a coefficient construction outside the perturbative ball.

  • Axioms: reflection positivity holds on the lattice (lattice and mid-lattice planes) and passes to \(\nu\) for every coordinate reflection (standard; exercise); rotation invariance and clustering need infinite volume.

  • Infinite volume: the force and its Burgers flow are intensive; estimates must be made local. Uniform-in-volume coefficient bounds and local energy moments are open; retaining the full FBSDE also needs control of the noise from infinity in the nonlocal \(\Xi\).
