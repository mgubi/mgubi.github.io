Where we are

So far. Stochastic quantisation of the GFF; the field at resolution \(t\) is a martingale of smooth fields; observables are martingales in the filtration of finite-resolution measurements.

Today. Interactions, still indexed by the resolution \(t \in [0, T]\).

  1. Generate the measure three ways: renormalisation, optimal control, filtering.

  2. They meet in one system for a field and a force: the force coordinates.

  3. Potentials: the value function generates them; Legendre; Wetterich.

  4. Observables, and the field equation with its sub-Gaussian noise.

Caveat. Statements marked (formal) are exact in finite dimensions, with a terminal resolution \(T < \infty\); the limits \(T \to \infty\), \(L \to \infty\) are the real problems. Bailleul–Chevyrev–G. (in prep.); Polchinski 1984; Bauerschmidt–Bodineau–Dagallier 2024

One measure, three constructions

Fix \(V_T\) and \(\nu_T \propto \mathrm{e}^{- V_T} \mu^{Q_T}\). Let \(S_t = Q_T - Q_t\), the covariance still unresolved at resolution \(t\).

  • Renormalisation. Split the covariance into shells and integrate them out. At every resolution the field is again a Gibbs measure, driven by an effective force.

  • Optimal control. Steer the Gaussian field by a drift, at the least cost. The effective potential is the value function, and the Polchinski equation is the dynamic programming equation.

  • Filtering. Measure the terminal field with increasing precision. The conditional law is the posterior, and the regulator is the information gained.

They produce the same process.

Three ways to generate the interacting measure

Lecture 4

Effective potentials

Finite volume \(\mathbb{T}^d_L\), terminal resolution \(T < \infty\) (UV cutoff), interaction \(V_T\) bounded below, e.g.

\(\displaystyle V_T (\varphi) = \int_{\mathbb{T}^d_L} [\lambda H_4 (\varphi ; c_T) + \gamma_T H_2 (\varphi ; c_T)], \qquad \nu_T (\mathrm{d} \varphi) = Z_T^{- 1} \mathrm{e}^{- V_T (\varphi)} \mu^{Q_T} (\mathrm{d} \varphi) .\)

Definition. (Wilson effective action)

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{e}^{- V_t (\varphi)} := \int \mathrm{e}^{- V_T (\varphi + \zeta)} \hspace{0.17em} \mu^{Q_{T, t}} (\mathrm{d} \zeta), \qquad t \in [0, T] .\)

The interaction felt at resolution \(t\) once the scales \([t, T]\) are integrated out; \(V_0 (0) = - \log Z_T\) is the free energy.

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{e}^{- V_t (X_t)} =\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}_0}_t [\mathrm{e}^{- V_T (X_T)}]\)
Marginals and stochastic localisation

Proposition. (Marginals) \(\mathrm{d} \mathbb{P}/ \mathrm{d} \mathbb{P}_0 = Z_T^{- 1} \mathrm{e}^{- V_T (X_T)}\). Then \(\text{Law}_{\mathbb{P}} (X_t) = \nu_t \propto \mathrm{e}^{- V_t} \mu^{Q_t}\), and \(\text{Law}_{\mathbb{P}} (X_T |\mathcal{F}_t) = \mu_t^{X_t}\), the tilt of \(\mu^{Q_{T, t}} (\cdot - X_t)\) by \(\mathrm{e}^{- V_T}\).

  • At every resolution the field is again a Gibbs measure: reference \(Q_t\), potential \(V_t\).

  • The measure-valued martingale \(t \mapsto \mu_t^{X_t}\) goes from \(\nu_T\) (\(t = 0\)) to \(\delta_{X_T}\) (\(t = T\)): a stochastic localisation of \(\nu_T\) (Eldan), adapted to the covariance profile.

Proof of the marginals. Under \(\mathbb{P}_0\), \(X_T = X_t + (X_T - X_t)\) with independent increment \(\sim \mu^{Q_{T, t}}\), so

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}} [G (X_t)] = Z_T^{- 1} \mathbb{E}_0 [G (X_t) \mathrm{e}^{- V_T (X_T)}] = Z_T^{- 1} \mathbb{E}_0 [G (X_t) \mathrm{e}^{- V_t (X_t)}] . \qquad\)
The Polchinski process

Theorem. (FBSDE for the interacting field) (formal) Under \(\mathbb{P}\), the interacting field at resolution \(t\), \(\Phi_t := X_t\), solves

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{d} \Phi_t = \dot{Q}_t \hspace{0.17em} F_t \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} t + \dot{Q}_t^{1 / 2} \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W^{\mathbb{P}}_t, \qquad F_t = - DV_t (\Phi_t) =\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}_t [- DV_T (\Phi_T)] .\)

The effective force \(F\) is a \(\mathbb{P}\)-martingale: an observable field of the interacting theory.

Proof. Density process \(M_t = Z_T^{- 1} \mathrm{e}^{- V_t (X_t)}\). It is a martingale, so by Itô it has no drift, \(\mathrm{d} M_t = - M_t \langle DV_t (X_t), \dot{Q}_t^{1 / 2} \mathrm{d} W_t \rangle\). Girsanov: \(W^{\mathbb{P}}_t = W_t + \int_0^t \dot{Q}_s^{1 / 2} DV_s (X_s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s\). Finally \(DV_t (\phi) = \mu_t^{\phi} (DV_T)\).

A forward–backward system

Read as an equation for an unknown pair \((\Phi, F)\):

\(\displaystyle \Phi_t = \int_0^t \dot{Q}_s F_s \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + \int_0^t \dot{Q}_s^{1 / 2} \mathrm{d} W_s, \qquad F_t =\mathbb{E}_t [- DV_T (\Phi_T)], \qquad t \in [0, T] .\)

  • The field is driven forward by a force determined backward from the terminal field.

  • No Polchinski equation needed; \(\nu_T = \text{Law} (\Phi_T)\) from the solution.

  • Strategy: solve with renormalised \(V_T\), estimates uniform in \(T\) and volume, take limits.

  • \(\Phi\) is not an observable (a semimartingale); observables are built from the noise and from \(F\).

The Polchinski equation

Theorem. (Polchinski 1984) (formal)

\(\displaystyle \partial_t V_t = \tfrac{1}{2} \langle DV_t, \dot{Q}_t \hspace{0.17em} DV_t \rangle - \tfrac{1}{2} \text{Tr} \left( \dot{Q}_t \hspace{0.17em} D^2 V_t \right), \qquad V_T \text{ given} .\)

Proof. \(u_t = \mathrm{e}^{- V_t} = \mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2} \Delta_{Q_{T, t}}} u_T\), \(\Delta_Q = \text{Tr} (QD^2)\), solves the backward heat equation \(\partial_t u_t = - \frac{1}{2} \text{Tr} (\dot{Q}_t D^2 u_t)\). Insert \(D^2 u = (DV \otimes DV - D^2 V) u\).

Nonlinear term: generates new, nonlocal interactions (trees).

Trace term: contracts pairs of fields with the shell covariance \(\dot{Q}_t \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} t\): loops.

A backward Hamilton–Jacobi equation; \(u = \mathrm{e}^{- V}\) is a Cole–Hopf transform.

The Boué–Dupuis formula

\(\mathbb{H}_a\): adapted drifts with \(\mathbb{E} \int_0^T \|u_s \|^2 < \infty\); \(X^u = X + \int_0^{\cdot} \dot{Q}_s^{1 / 2} u_s \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s\).

Theorem. (Boué–Dupuis 1998; Polchinski \(=\) HJB) (formal)

\(\displaystyle - \log \mathbb{E}_0 [\mathrm{e}^{- V_T (X_T)}] = V_0 (0) = \inf_{u \in \mathbb{H}_a} \mathbb{E}_0 \left[ V_T (X^u_T) + \frac{1}{2} \int_0^T \|u_s \|^2 \mathrm{d} s \right] .\)

Optimal feedback \(u^{\ast}_s = - \dot{Q}_s^{1 / 2} DV_s (X^{u^{\ast}}_s)\), \(\text{Law} (X^{u^{\ast}}_T) = \nu_T\). \(V_t\) is the value function; Polchinski is the HJB equation.

Proof (verification). Itô \(+\) Polchinski:

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}_0 \left[ V_T (X^u_T) + \tfrac{1}{2} \int_0^T \|u_t \|^2 \mathrm{d} t \right] = V_0 (0) + \tfrac{1}{2} \mathbb{E}_0 \int_0^T \|u_t + \dot{Q}_t^{1 / 2} DV_t (X^u_t)\|^2 \mathrm{d} t. \qquad\)
The control problem: PDE, dynamics, variational

Euler–Lagrange. Perturb \(u \to u + \varepsilon v\):

\(\displaystyle u_s = - \dot{Q}_s^{1 / 2} \hspace{0.17em} \mathbb{E}_s [DV_T (X^u_T)] = \dot{Q}_s^{1 / 2} F_s : \quad \text{the FBSDE.}\)

Entropy. \(\frac{1}{2} \mathbb{E}_0 \int \|u\|^2 \geqslant \text{Ent} (\text{Law} (X^u) | \text{Law} (X))\): a pathwise lift of the Gibbs principle \(- \log Z_T = \inf_{\nu} \{ \int V_T \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \nu + \text{Ent} (\nu | \mu^{Q_T})\}\). Useful because adapted drifts can be built scale by scale, as functionals of \(X\).

Renormalisation in the variational picture

Generating functional:

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{W}_T (f) = - \log \int \mathrm{e}^{- f} \mathrm{d} \nu_T = \inf_u \mathbb{E}_0 \left[ (f + V_T) (X^u_T) + \tfrac{1}{2} \int_0^T \|u_s \|^2 \right] - V_0 (0) .\)

Bounds uniform in \(T\) \(\Rightarrow\) tightness, moments, identification of limits.

\(\Phi^4_3\). The leading drift \(u_s \approx - 4 \lambda \dot{Q}_s^{1 / 2} : X_s^3\): has divergent cost,

\(\displaystyle \tfrac{1}{2} \mathbb{E} \int_0^T \|4 \lambda \dot{Q}_s^{1 / 2} : X_s^3 : \|^2 \mathrm{d} s = 12 \lambda^2 L^3 \int Q_T (z)^4 \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} z \sim T.\)

Change variables \(u = - 4 \lambda \dot{Q}^{1 / 2} : X^3 : + r\), then paracontrolled changes of variables: the singular terms become explicit polynomials in Wick powers of \(X\), cancelled by the counterterms, and the remainder is coercive. Uniform control in Besov/paracontrolled norms \(=\) construction of \(\Phi^4_3\). Barashkov–Gubinelli, Duke 2020; EJP 2021

Pontryagin's maximum principle

Minimise \(J (u) = g (x_T) + \int_t^T \ell (x_s, u_s) \mathrm{d} s\) subject to \(\dot{x} = b (x, u)\), \(x_t = x\). Hamiltonian:

\(\displaystyle H (x, p, u) = \langle p, b (x, u) \rangle - \ell (x, u) .\)

Theorem. (Pontryagin) Along an optimal pair there is a costate \(p\) with

\(\displaystyle \dot{x} = \partial_p H, \qquad \dot{p} = - \partial_x H, \qquad p_T = - Dg (x_T), \qquad u \in \arg \max H (x, p, \cdot) .\)

  • State forward, costate backward: a two-point boundary problem.

  • The costate is the Lagrange multiplier of the constraint \(\dot{x} = b\); an optimal control is a critical point of the Hamiltonian.

  • Next: why this holds, and what \(p\) is.

Pontryagin: first variation and HJB

First variation. Perturb \(u \to u + \varepsilon v\); the state varies by \(\xi = \partial_{\varepsilon} x\),

\(\displaystyle \dot{\xi} = \partial_x b \xi + \partial_u bv, \qquad \xi_t = 0.\)

The costate equation \(\dot{p} = - \partial_x H\) is exactly what makes

\(\displaystyle \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} s} \langle p, \xi \rangle = \langle \partial_x \ell, \xi \rangle + \langle p, \partial_u bv \rangle .\)

Integrating, with \(p_T = - Dg (x_T)\):

\(\displaystyle \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} \varepsilon} J (u + \varepsilon v) |_{\varepsilon = 0} = - \int_t^T \langle \partial_u H (x_s, p_s, u_s), v_s \rangle \mathrm{d} s.\)

HJB. Dynamic programming over \([t, t + \mathrm{d} t]\):

\(\displaystyle V (t, x) = \min_u [\ell \mathrm{d} t + V (t + \mathrm{d} t, x + b \mathrm{d} t)] \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \partial_t V = H_{\ast} (x, - DV), \quad H_{\ast} = \max_u H.\)

Hamilton's equations for \(H_{\ast}\) are its characteristics, and along them \(p_s = - DV (s, x_s)\): the costate is the force the future exerts on the present.

The Hamiltonian of the field

State \(=\) field, \(b = \dot{Q}_s^{1 / 2} u\), \(\ell = \frac{1}{2} \|u\|^2\), \(g = V_T\). \(H\) is maximised at \(u = \dot{Q}_s^{1 / 2} p\):

\(\displaystyle H_{\ast} (p) = \frac{1}{2} \langle p, \dot{Q}_s p \rangle :\)

a free particle with mass matrix \(\dot{Q}_s^{- 1}\), and no dependence on the state \(x\).

  • Without noise: \(\dot{p} = 0\), \(\dot{x} = \dot{Q} p\): the shear \(\Sigma_t : (x, p) \mapsto (x + S_t p, p)\), with \(S_t := Q_T - Q_t\) the covariance still unresolved. All interaction is in \(p = - DV_T (x + S_t p)\): trees. HJB \(=\) Polchinski without the trace.

  • With noise (Bismut 1973, Peng 1990): the costate solves a BSDE

    \(\displaystyle \mathrm{d} p_s = - \partial_x H \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + \Upsilon_s \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W_s,\)

    with \(\Upsilon \mathrm{d} W\) the price of adaptedness. Here \(\partial_x H = 0\): the costate is a martingale, \(p_t = - DV_t (X_t) = F_t\).

  • The Itô term turns HJB into Polchinski: quadratic part \(=\) trees, trace term \(=\) loops.

The FBSDE is the stochastic Hamiltonian system: field \(=\) state, force \(=\) costate.

State and costate

Here \(\dot{S}_t = - \dot{Q}_t\), \(S_T = 0\). Optimal control \(u_t = \dot{Q}_t^{1 / 2} p_t\), with costate \(p_t = F_t\):

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{d} F_t = \Upsilon_t \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W_t, \qquad \Upsilon_t = DF_t (\Phi_t) \dot{Q}_t^{1 / 2} .\)

  • \(F_t (\cdot)\) is the decoupling field, \(F_t = F_t (\Phi_t)\); Burgers is its quasilinear PDE, and \(\Upsilon\) the martingale representation integrand of the costate.

  • In Burgers: transport term \(- DF [\dot{Q} F]\) \(\leftarrow\) drift of the state; Itô term \(- \frac{1}{2} \text{Tr} (\dot{Q} D^2 F)\) \(\leftarrow\) noise.

  • The shear \(\Sigma_t\) is the flow of the transport part: its characteristics.

Filtering: measuring the field

Assume all covariance operators are functions of \(A\). Let \(\mathcal{R}_t = S_t^{- 1} - Q_T^{- 1}\), and \(j_t = S_t^{- 1} \Phi_t\). By the Gaussian bridge,

\(\displaystyle j_t =\mathcal{R}_t \Phi_T + N_t, \quad \mathbb{E} [N_s \otimes N_t] =\mathcal{R}_{s \wedge t}, \quad N \text{ independent of } \Phi_T :\)

a noisy measurement of the terminal field, whose signal-to-noise ratio is the regulator. Let \(\mathcal{K}_t (j) = \text{log} \int \mathrm{e}^{\langle j, y \rangle - \frac{1}{2} \langle y, \mathcal{R}_t y \rangle} \nu_T (\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} y)\).

  • \(\mu_t^{\Phi_t}\) is the Bayesian posterior; \(\psi_t =\mathbb{E}_t \Phi_T = D\mathcal{K}_t (j_t)\) and \(\text{Cov}_t = D^2 \mathcal{K}_t (j_t)\).

  • \(W\) is the innovation: \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} j_t = \dot{\mathcal{R}}_t \psi_t \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} t + \dot{\mathcal{R}}_t^{1 / 2} \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W_t\) and \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \psi_t = \text{Cov}_t \dot{\mathcal{R}}_t^{1 / 2} \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W_t\).

  • \(\partial_t \mathcal{K}+ \frac{1}{2} \text{Tr} (\dot{\mathcal{R}} D^2 \mathcal{K}) + \frac{1}{2} \langle D\mathcal{K}, \dot{\mathcal{R}} D\mathcal{K} \rangle = 0\): Polchinski in the variable \(j\).

Eldan's basic scheme is the profile with \(\mathcal{R}_t = t\): the profile of Lectures 5–6.

Closed versus local

At finite \(T\) the force is a closed observable, \(F_t =\mathbb{E}_t [F_T]\), and for \(\Phi^4\) it is local:

\(\displaystyle F_T (x) = - 4 \lambda H_3 (\Phi_T (x) ; c_T) - 2 \gamma_T H_1 (\Phi_T (x) ; c_T) .\)

As \(T \to \infty\)

\(F\) should converge as an observable. In \(d = 2\) it is closed, \(F_{\infty} = - 4 \lambda : \Phi_{\infty}^3\):. In \(d = 3\) a closed limit \(F_t =\mathbb{E}_t F_{\infty}\) would let the DS identity pass to \(t \to \infty\), and conditioning on \(\sigma (\Phi_{\infty})\) would give an integrable logarithmic derivative of \(\Phi^4_3\) as a function of the field: not expected (Albeverio–Liang–Zegarlinski 2006: the regularised ones do not converge in \(L^2\)). Locality of the limiting force is the crux of the construction.

Force coordinates and potentials

Lecture 4

The force: a Burgers equation

Differentiating Polchinski, \(F_t = - DV_t\) solves the backward viscous Burgers equation

\(\displaystyle \partial_t F_t = - DF_t [\dot{Q}_t F_t] - \tfrac{1}{2} \text{Tr} (\dot{Q}_t D^2 F_t), \qquad F_T = - DV_T .\)

  • Cole–Hopf \(F = D \log u\) linearises it (\(u = \mathrm{e}^{- V}\) solves a heat equation).

  • Uses only the force: intensive, while \(V_t\) is extensive (\(\sim\) volume). Matters in infinite volume.

  • Flows for different covariances commute when \(DF\) is symmetric, i.e. \(F\) a gradient. Non-gradient forces (general Wilson–Itô diffusions): curvature.

Removing the predictable drift: the forecast

Proposition. (formal) The conditional mean of the terminal field is

\(\displaystyle \psi_t := \mathbb{E}_t [\Phi_T] = \Phi_t + S_t F_t, \qquad (\psi_t, F_t) = \Sigma_t (\Phi_t, F_t),\)

a martingale with \(\mathrm{d} \psi_t = \dot{Q}_t^{1 / 2} \mathrm{d} W_t + S_t \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} F_t = (\mathbb{1} + S_t DF_t (\Phi_t)) \dot{Q}_t^{1 / 2} \mathrm{d} W_t\).

Proof. \(F\) martingale \(\Rightarrow \mathbb{E}_t \int_t^T \dot{Q}_s F_s \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s = S_t F_t\). In the differential, \(\dot{Q}_t F_t \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} t\) cancels with \(\dot{S}_t F_t \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} t\).

  • \(\Phi_t = \psi_t - S_t F_t\): a martingale (the field observable) minus the drift still expected on the unresolved scales.

  • Noise of \(\psi\): fresh shell fluctuation \(+\) revision of the forecast. The forward–backward coupling moves from the drift to the volatility.

\(\psi\) is the interaction picture w.r.t. the classical dynamics.

The FBSDE in forecast coordinates

Invert \(x \mapsto \psi = x + S_t F_t (x)\): \(x = \psi + S_t \mathcal{F}_t (\psi)\), \(\mathcal{F}_t (\psi) := - F_t (x)\). Response and regulator:

\(\displaystyle H_t (\psi) = (S_t^{- 1} + D\mathcal{F}_t (\psi))^{- 1}, \qquad \mathcal{R}_t = S_t^{- 1} - Q_T^{- 1}, \qquad \dot{\mathcal{R}}_t = S_t^{- 1} \dot{Q}_t S_t^{- 1} \geqslant 0.\)

Theorem. (formal)

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{d} \psi_t = H_t (\psi_t) \hspace{0.17em} S_t^{- 1} \dot{Q}_t^{1 / 2} \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W_t, \qquad \mathcal{F}_t (\psi_t) =\mathbb{E}_t [DV_T (\psi_T)],\)

\(\psi_T = \Phi_T\). No drift; covariance \(\mathcal{C}_t = H_t \dot{\mathcal{R}}_t H_t^{\ast}\). The decoupling field solves the pure diffusion equation

\(\displaystyle \partial_t \mathcal{F}_t + \tfrac{1}{2} \text{Tr} \left( \mathcal{C}_t \hspace{0.17em} D^2 \mathcal{F}_t \right) = 0, \qquad \mathcal{F}_T = DV_T .\)

Proof. \(\mathbb{1} + S_t DF_t = H_t S_t^{- 1}\); \(\mathcal{F}_t (\psi_t)\) is a martingale and \(\psi\) has no drift: Itô.

What the change of coordinates did

Compare with Burgers:

\(\displaystyle \underbrace{\partial_t F = - DF [\dot{Q} F] - \tfrac{1}{2} \text{Tr} (\dot{Q} D^2 F)}_{\text{field coordinate}} \qquad \longrightarrow \qquad \underbrace{\partial_t \mathcal{F}= - \tfrac{1}{2} \text{Tr} (\mathcal{C} \hspace{0.17em} D^2 \mathcal{F})}_{\text{forecast coordinate}}\)

  • The transport term is absorbed exactly: dropping the Itô term, \(\mathcal{F}_t = DV_T\) does not move. All trees live in the coordinate change \(x = \psi + S_t \mathcal{F}_t (\psi)\).

  • Loops come only from the quadratic variation \(\mathcal{C}_t\) of the forecast; the nonlinearity has moved into its field dependence.

  • No gradient structure, no Gaussian convolution used: valid for any FBSDE with a Markov martingale force, whenever the coordinate change is invertible.

Conditional moments

Proposition. (formal) If \(F_t = - DV_t\), then

\(\displaystyle H_t (\psi_t) = \text{Cov}_t := \text{Cov}^{\mathbb{P}} (\Phi_T \hspace{0.17em} | \hspace{0.17em} \Phi_t) = S_t - S_t D^2 V_t (\Phi_t) S_t .\)

So \(H_t > 0\), \(\mathbb{1} + S_t DF_t\) is always invertible, and \(H_t \leqslant S_t\) for convex \(V_T\) (Brascamp–Lieb).

The forecast coordinates are the first two conditional moments of the stochastic localisation: \((\psi_t, \text{Cov}_t) = (\mathbb{E}[\Phi_T \hspace{0.17em} | \hspace{0.17em} \Phi_t], \text{Cov} (\Phi_T \hspace{0.17em} | \hspace{0.17em} \Phi_t))\). The second moment

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}_t [\Phi_T \otimes \Phi_T] = \psi_t \otimes \psi_t + \text{Cov}_t\)

is a martingale, hence

\(\displaystyle \mathrm{d} \langle \psi, \psi \rangle_t = \text{Cov}_t \dot{\mathcal{R}}_t \text{Cov}_t \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} t, \qquad \text{dCov}_t = - \text{Cov}_t \dot{\mathcal{R}}_t \text{Cov}_t \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} t + \mathrm{d} (\text{mart.}) .\)

Gaussian reference: \(S_t \dot{\mathcal{R}}_t S_t = \dot{Q}_t\).

Brascamp–Lieb bounds on the conditional covariance

Conditional law \(\mu^{\phi}_t \propto \mathrm{e}^{-\mathcal{U}}\), \(D^2 \mathcal{U}= S_t^{- 1} + D^2 V_T\). Then

\(\displaystyle (\mathbb{E}_t [S_t^{- 1} + D^2 V_T])^{- 1} \leqslant \operatorname{Cov}_t \leqslant \mathbb{E}_t [(S_t^{- 1} + D^2 V_T)^{- 1}] .\)

  • Lower: Cramér–Rao, no convexity needed: \(\mathbb{E} [(y - m) \otimes D\mathcal{U}] = 1\) and Cauchy–Schwarz.

  • Upper: Brascamp–Lieb (1976), for \(D^2 \mathcal{U}> 0\): \(\operatorname{Var} (f) \leqslant \mathbb{E} \langle Df, (D^2 \mathcal{U})^{- 1} Df \rangle\).

  • Equal for quadratic \(V_T\). Via \(\operatorname{Cov}_t = S_t - S_t D^2 V_t S_t\): two-sided bounds on \(D^2 V_t\), i.e. on the Wetterich propagator \(H_t\).

Phase space and Lagrangian graphs

Phase space of pairs (field, force) \((x, p) \in \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}^n\), with the symplectic form

\(\displaystyle \omega = \sum_i \mathrm{d} x_i \wedge \mathrm{d} p_i .\)

  • \(L\) (dimension \(n\)) is Lagrangian if \(\omega |_L = 0\).

  • A graph \(p = F (x)\) is Lagrangian \(\Leftrightarrow\) \(DF\) symmetric \(\Leftrightarrow\) \(F\) is a gradient. Gradient sector = Lagrangian graphs.

  • Symplectic maps send Lagrangian submanifolds to Lagrangian ones. The shear \(\Sigma_t (x, p) = (x + S_t p, p)\) is symplectic since \(S_t\) is symmetric: \(\Sigma_t^{\ast} \omega = \omega + \sum S_{ij} \mathrm{d} p_j \wedge \mathrm{d} p_i = \omega\).

So \(\Sigma_t\) maps the graph of \(F_t = - DV_t\) to a Lagrangian submanifold; if it is a graph over the forecast \(\psi\), it is the graph of a gradient.

The free action generates the shear

The cheapest way to reach \(\psi\) at \(T\) from \(x\) at \(t\), without interaction, costs the free action

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{A}_t (x, \psi) = \frac{1}{2} \langle \psi - x, S_t^{- 1} (\psi - x) \rangle, \quad p = S_t^{- 1} (\psi - x) \text{ constant.}\)

  • \(D_x \mathcal{A}_t = - p\), \(D_{\psi} \mathcal{A}_t = p\): so \(\mathcal{A}_t\) is the generating function of the shear \(\Sigma_t\).

  • \(\partial_t \mathcal{A}_t = \frac{1}{2} \langle D_x \mathcal{A}_t, \dot{Q}_t D_x \mathcal{A}_t \rangle\): a complete integral of the noiseless Polchinski equation.

  • Jacobi: in the new coordinates the Hamiltonian vanishes, and \(\psi\), \(p\) are constants of motion.

\((\psi_t, F_t)\) are the Jacobi coordinates of the free Hamiltonian of the field. With noise, the constants of motion become martingales.

The gradient sector

\(F_t = - DV_t\). The map \(x \mapsto x + S_t F_t (x)\) is \(S_t\) times the gradient of

\(\displaystyle \frac{1}{2} \langle x, S_t^{- 1} x \rangle - V_t (x), \qquad \text{with Hessian } S_t^{- 1} \text{Cov}_t S_t^{- 1} > 0,\)

so it is injective: the image of the graph of \(F_t\) is a graph over \(\psi\), that of \(-\mathcal{F}_t\) with

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{F}_t = D \Gamma_t, \qquad H_t = (S_t^{- 1} + D^2 \Gamma_t)^{- 1}, \qquad \Gamma_T = V_T .\)

Generating function: the Moreau transform

\(\displaystyle V_t (x) = \inf_{\psi} \left\{ \Gamma_t (\psi) + \tfrac{1}{2} \langle x - \psi, S_t^{- 1} (x - \psi) \rangle \right\},\)

optimality: \(x = \psi + S_t D \Gamma_t (\psi)\), \(DV_t (x) = D \Gamma_t (\psi)\).

No convexity of \(V_T\) or \(\Gamma_t\) is used: only \(S_t^{- 1} + D^2 \Gamma_t =\operatorname{Cov}_t^{- 1} > 0\). Without noise, caustics and shocks.

The Wetterich equation

Theorem. (formal)

\(\displaystyle \partial_t \Gamma_t = \tfrac{1}{2} \text{Tr} [\dot{\mathcal{R}}_t ((S_t^{- 1} + D^2 \Gamma_t)^{- 1} - S_t)], \qquad \Gamma_T = V_T .\)

Equivalently, for the effective average action \(\Gamma^{\text{EAA}}_t = \Gamma_t + \frac{1}{2} \langle \psi, Q_T^{- 1} \psi \rangle\),

\(\displaystyle \partial_t \Gamma^{\text{EAA}}_t = \tfrac{1}{2} \text{Tr} [\dot{\mathcal{R}}_t (D^2 \Gamma^{\text{EAA}}_t +\mathcal{R}_t)^{- 1}] + c (t),\)

from the bare action (\(t = T\), \(\mathcal{R}_T = \infty\)) to the 1PI effective action of \(\nu_T\) (\(t = 0\), \(\mathcal{R}_0 = 0\)).

Proof. \(D_v \text{Tr} (\dot{\mathcal{R}} H) = - \text{Tr} (\mathcal{C} \hspace{0.17em} D^2 \mathcal{F}) [v]\) by symmetry of \(D^3 \Gamma\), so the force equation reads \(D \left( \partial_t \Gamma - \frac{1}{2} \text{Tr} (\dot{\mathcal{R}} H) \right) = 0\). The constant: envelope theorem \(+\) Polchinski.

\(\displaystyle \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} V_t = \langle D \Gamma_t, \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \psi \rangle + \langle p, \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \psi \rangle - \langle p, \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} x \rangle = - \langle F_t, \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} x \rangle .\)

Legendre. With \(\mathcal{K}_t (S_t^{- 1} x) = \frac{1}{2} \langle x, S_t^{- 1} x \rangle - V_t (x)\) up to a function of \(t\),

  • \(V_t\) in the field \(x\); \(\mathcal{K}_t\) in the measurement \(j = S_t^{- 1} x\); \(\Gamma_t\) in the forecast \(\psi\).

  • Polchinski is the Hamilton–Jacobi equation of \(\mathcal{K}\); Wetterich is its Legendre dual. The regulator is the information gained.

The regulator of the heat profile

For \(T = \infty\), \(S_t = C (1 - \mathrm{e}^{- A / t^2})\) and

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{R}_t = \frac{A}{\mathrm{e}^{A / t^2} - 1} .\)

  • \(\approx t^2\) (a mass term) on the modes \(|k| \ll t\);

  • exponentially small on \(|k| \gg t\).

The exponential regulator of the functional RG.

Other profiles give other regulators, e.g. the mass regulator \(\mathcal{R}_t = t^2\).

\(\Gamma\) as a rate function

At \(t = 0\): the 1PI effective action of \(\nu_T\) (Jona-Lasinio 1964),

\(\displaystyle \Gamma^{\text{EAA}}_0 (\psi) = V_0 (0) + \sup_j \left\{ \langle j, \psi \rangle - \log \int \mathrm{e}^{\langle j, \varphi \rangle} \mathrm{d} \nu_T \right\} .\)

  • Cramér: the mean of \(N\) independent samples is \(\approx \psi\) with probability \(\approx \mathrm{e}^{- N (\Gamma^{\text{EAA}}_0 (\psi) - V_0 (0))}\): a large-deviation rate function.

  • Convex even when \(V_T\) is not; minimal at the mean; Hessian there \(=\) inverse covariance.

  • Formally, the same for the field averaged over a large volume: \(\Gamma\) per unit volume (the effective potential of QFT, not \(V_t\)) is the rate function of the magnetisation; flat parts may appear (Maxwell).

  • The renormalisation group as a probabilistic object: limit theorems for strongly dependent variables.

Variational meaning and the tree level

Constrained free energy. Gibbs variational principle for the conditional law:

\(\displaystyle \Gamma_t (\psi) = \inf \left\{ \int V_T \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \nu + \text{Ent} \left( \nu \hspace{0.17em} | \hspace{0.17em} \mu^{S_t} (\cdot - \psi) \right) : \int \varphi \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \nu = \psi \right\} :\)

the free energy of the unresolved shell, constrained to the forecast. \(V_t\) is the value function; \(\Gamma_t\) is its Moreau transform.

Tree level. Drop the Itô term: \(\Gamma_t = V_T\), and the Moreau transform becomes the Hopf–Lax formula

\(\displaystyle V^{\text{tree}}_t (x) = \inf_y \left\{ V_T (y) + \tfrac{1}{2} \langle y - x, S_t^{- 1} (y - x) \rangle \right\},\)

which solves \(\partial_t V = \frac{1}{2} \langle DV, \dot{Q} DV \rangle\): the saddle point of the Gaussian convolution. The minimiser solves the classical field equation \(S_t^{- 1} (y - x) + DV_T (y) = 0\) in the shell \([t, T]\); expanding in \(V_T\) gives exactly the tree diagrams.

Observables of the interacting theory

For \(G\) a functional of the terminal field, \(O_t =\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}_t [G (\Phi_T)] = G_t (\Phi_t)\), \(G_t (\phi) = \mu_t^{\phi} (G)\), and

\(\displaystyle \partial_t G_t + \tfrac{1}{2} \text{Tr} (\dot{Q}_t D^2 G_t) - \langle \dot{Q}_t DV_t, DG_t \rangle = 0, \qquad G_T = G :\)

the backward Kolmogorov equation, a Doob transform of the Gaussian heat equation. Any solution gives an observable, closed at finite \(T\), not necessarily as \(T \to \infty\). The force is \(G = - DV_T\).

Tangent observables. \(V_T^{\theta}\) depending on a coupling, \(U_t = \partial_{\theta} V_t^{\theta}\):

\(\displaystyle U_t (\Phi_t) =\mathbb{E}^{\theta}_t [\partial_{\theta} V_T^{\theta} (\Phi_T)], \qquad \partial_{\theta} \mathbb{E}^{\nu^{\theta}_T} [G] = - \text{Cov}^{\nu_T^{\theta}} (G, \partial_{\theta} V^{\theta}_T) .\)

E.g. the running \(: \varphi^4\): for \(\theta = \lambda\): observables conjugate to the coordinates of theory space.

Dyson–Schwinger equations at every scale

All operators are functions of \(A\) (so they commute); \(K = Q_T^{- 1}\), \(J_t = KQ_t\) (heat profile: a heat semigroup, \(\to \mathrm{e}^{- A / t^2}\)).

The noise observable is the filtered measurement noise: \(\eta_t = K \Phi_t - J_t F_t = j_t -\mathcal{R}_t \psi_t =\mathbb{E}_t N_t\).

Theorem. (formal) The noise observable \(\eta_t = K \Phi_t - J_t F_t\) is a centred martingale, \(\mathrm{d} \eta_t = K \dot{Q}_t^{1 / 2} \mathrm{d} W^{\mathbb{P}}_t - J_t \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} F_t\), and

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}} [D_{J_t h} G (\Phi_t)] =\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}} \left[ G (\Phi_t) \hspace{0.17em} \langle h, K \Phi_t - J_t F_t \rangle \right] .\)

At \(t = T\): the DS equation \(\mathbb{E}^{\nu_T} [D_h G] =\mathbb{E}^{\nu_T} [G \langle h, K \varphi + DV_T \rangle]\).

Proof. \(\dot{J}_t = K \dot{Q}_t\): drifts cancel. IBP for \(\mu^{Q_t}\) in direction \(J_t h\), using \(\Phi_t \sim \nu_t\).

The field equation at resolution \(t\)

As \(T \to \infty\), \(K \to A\), and \(\eta_t = K \Phi_t - J_t F_t\) becomes

\(\displaystyle A \Phi_t = AQ_t \hspace{0.17em} F_t + \eta_t \qquad (AQ_t = \mathrm{e}^{- A / t^2} \text{ for the heat profile}) :\)

the Euclidean field equation \(A \varphi = - V' (\varphi) +\)noise, seen at resolution \(t\). No products of distributions: the nonlinearity enters only through the force observable, smeared at scale \(1 / t\). For \(\Phi^4_3\), with \(F_t\) in the role of the renormalised cube, \(t \to \infty\) corresponds to the renormalised DS equation (Gubinelli–Hofmanová 2021).

Compare: stochastic quantisation (Lecture 3)

There the field equation is imposed in an auxiliary time; here it holds at each resolution, and the auxiliary parameter is the resolution itself.

Conditioning an SPDE solution on a scale filtration gives identities of the same shape but with a different noise: the martingale \(J_t \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} F_t\) in \(\mathrm{d} \eta_t\) is essential.

The noise is sub-Gaussian

\(\eta_t\) is, through \(J_t\), the score of the law of \(\Phi_t\): \(\eta_t = - J_t D \log p_t (\Phi_t)\). Given \(\Phi_T\), the interacting field is still the Gaussian bridge:

\(\displaystyle \Phi_t = J_t \Phi_T + \zeta_t, \quad \zeta_t \sim N (0, Q_t (1 - J_t)) \quad \Rightarrow \quad \eta_t =\mathbb{E} [J_t (Q_t (1 - J_t))^{- 1} \zeta_t | \Phi_t] .\)

Proposition. \(\eta_t\) is dominated in convex order by \(N (0, \mathcal{R}_t)\), with \(\mathcal{R}_t = S_t^{- 1} - Q_T^{- 1}\) the Wetterich regulator; in particular

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E} \mathrm{e}^{u \langle h, \eta_t \rangle} \leqslant \exp \left( \frac{u^2}{2} \langle h, \mathcal{R}_t h \rangle \right),\)

uniformly in the interaction, with no convexity (conditional Jensen).

  • Heat profile, \(T = \infty\) (finite spatial cutoff): \(\mathcal{R}_t = A / (\mathrm{e}^{A / t^2} - 1) \leqslant t^2\); \(\mathcal{R}_t \leqslant \frac{4}{3} t^2\) for \(T \geqslant 2 t\).

  • With \(\mathrm{d} F = \Upsilon \mathrm{d} W\) and Itô: \(\mathbb{E} \int_0^t \| \Upsilon_s^{\ast} J_s h\|^2 \mathrm{d} s \leqslant 2 (1 + \mathrm{e}^{- 1}) t^2 \|h\|^2\): a smoothed derivative of the force, without the terminal force.

  • \(\Phi^4_3\): the noise in \(A \Phi_t = AQ_t F_t + \eta_t\) is controlled a priori; what remains is coercivity of the renormalised force.

How rough is the noise?

Spectral densities, heat profile, \(T = \infty\), \(A = |k|^2 + m^2\):

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{cccc} \text{white noise} & \text{GFF} & \text{free noise } AX_t & \text{bound } \mathcal{R}_t\\ 1 & A^{- 1} & A \mathrm{e}^{- A / t^2} & \frac{A}{\mathrm{e}^{A / t^2} - 1} \end{array}\)

  • \(\mathcal{R}_t \leqslant t^2 \mathrm{e}^{- A / 2 t^2}\): at resolution \(t\), \(\eta_t \lesssim t \times\) white noise smoothed at scale \(1 / t\), so \(\eta_t (x) = O (t^{1 + d / 2})\).

  • At scale \(1 / t\) this is the size of regularity \(- 1 - d / 2\) (\(- 5 / 2\) in \(d = 3\)): one derivative rougher than white noise, like the free noise \(A^{1 / 2} \xi\), whatever the interaction.

  • Not a limit distribution: on a fixed \(h\) only \(\langle h, \eta_t \rangle = O (t)\).

  • Larger than the free noise by \(t^2 / A\) below \(t\), but saturated by a pinning potential.

  • Pathwise (convex domination of the norm, Doob): a.s. for \(t \geqslant 1\), at finite spatial cutoff, \(\sup_x (1 + |x|)^{- \varepsilon} | \eta_t (x) | \leqslant C (\omega) t^{1 + d / 2} \sqrt{\log (2 + t)}\).

Infinite volume

On \(\mathbb{T}^d_L\), \(V_0 (0)\) and \(V_t\) are extensive (\(\sim L^d\)). Three intensive objects survive:

  1. the force. For \(V_T = \int v_T (\varphi)\), on \(\mathbb{R}^d\):

    \(\displaystyle \Phi_t = \int_0^t \dot{Q}_s F_s \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + \int_0^t \dot{Q}_s^{1 / 2} \mathrm{d} W_s, \qquad F_t (x) = -\mathbb{E}_t [v_T' (\Phi_T (x))] .\)

    Everything is local; infinite-volume limits should solve this. Uniqueness of solutions \(\leftrightarrow\) uniqueness of the theory; non-uniqueness expected at phase transitions (\(\Phi^4_2\), deep double well).

  2. the Burgers flow of the force;

  3. differences of free energies \(\mathcal{W}_T^{(L)} (f)\), \(f\) local: bounded as \(L \to \infty\); basis of the infinite-volume analysis of \(\Phi^4_2\) and \(\exp (\Phi)_2\).

Estimates must be local (weighted norms, volume-independent constants): the analogue of “coming down from infinity”. Barashkov–Gubinelli, PMP 2023; Glimm–Jaffe–Spencer 1975

Differences of free energies

Perturb the optimal drift: \(u = u^{\ast} + w\), \(X^u = \Phi + I (w)\), \(I_t (w) = \int_0^t \dot{Q}^{1 / 2} w\). Euler–Lagrange gives the cross term

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E} \int \langle u^{\ast}, w \rangle = -\mathbb{E} \langle DV_T (\Phi_T), I_T (w) \rangle .\)

Proposition. (formal) The two extensive infima cancel exactly:

\(\displaystyle - \log \int \mathrm{e}^{- f} \mathrm{d} \nu_T = \inf_w \mathbb{E} \left[ f (\Phi_T + I_T (w)) + B_{V_T} (\Phi_T, I_T (w)) + \frac{1}{2} \int_0^T \|w\|^2 \right],\)

\(B_V (\phi, h) = V (\phi + h) - V (\phi) - \langle DV (\phi), h \rangle\): the first-order Taylor remainder.

  • Minimiser: \(\Phi^f = \Phi + I (w)\) solves the FBSDE with terminal potential \(V_T + f\), a perturbation of \(\Phi\):

    \(\displaystyle F^f_s - F_s = -\mathbb{E}_s [Df (\Phi^f_T) + DV_T (\Phi^f_T) - DV_T (\Phi_T)] .\)
Infinite-volume measures

Local interaction \(V_T = \int v_T (\varphi (x)) \mathrm{d} x\):

\(\displaystyle B_{V_T} (\phi, h) = \int b_T (\phi (x), h (x)) \mathrm{d} x, \qquad b_T (a, h) = v_T (a + h) - v_T (a) - v_T' (a) h.\)

Stochastic localisation: what and why

Eldan 2013: write a measure as a mixture of simpler ones, by a measure-valued martingale. Here

\(\displaystyle \mu_t^{\phi} (\mathrm{d} y) \propto \mathrm{e}^{\langle j, y \rangle - \frac{1}{2} \langle y, \mathcal{R}_t y \rangle} \nu_T (\mathrm{d} y), \qquad j_t = S_t^{- 1} \Phi_t =\mathcal{R}_t \Phi_T + N_t, \quad \mathbb{E} [N_t^{\otimes 2}] =\mathcal{R}_t :\)

a noisy measurement of \(\Phi_T\) with signal-to-noise \(\mathcal{R}_t\), and \(\mu_t\) the posterior. Basic (identity-control) case: \(\mathcal{R}_t = t\).

  • Martingale: Itô splits variance and entropy into revealed and hidden parts.

  • Simpler pieces: \(\nu_T\) log-concave \(\Rightarrow\) \(\mu_t\) is \(\mathcal{R}_t\)-uniformly log-concave.

  • One quantity: the loss is governed by \(\operatorname{Cov}_t\), with explicit dynamics. An inequality reduces to: the localisation is not too fast.

  • Algorithms: sampling (El Alaoui–Montanari–Sellke), denoising diffusions (Montanari); \(\eta_t\) is the score, up to \(J_t\).

    \(\displaystyle \operatorname{Ent}_{\nu_T} (\rho) =\operatorname{Ent} (M_t) +\mathbb{E}\operatorname{Ent}_{\mu_t^{\Phi_t}} (\rho) .\)

  • The increments see the conditional law: \(DG_t (\phi) = S_t^{- 1} \operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_t^{\phi}} (\rho, y)\).

  • Entropic stability (Chen–Eldan): if \(\dot{\mathcal{R}}_r \operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_r^{\phi}} \dot{\mathcal{R}}_r \leqslant \alpha_r \dot{\mathcal{R}}_r\) for all \(\phi\) and \(r \leqslant t\), then \(\operatorname{Ent}_{\nu_T} (\rho) \leqslant \mathrm{e}^{\int_0^t \alpha_r \mathrm{d} r} \mathbb{E}\operatorname{Ent}_{\mu_t^{\Phi_t}} (\rho)\): the low resolutions must not hide too much.

Bauerschmidt–Bodineau–Dagallier, Probab. Surveys 2024: their fluctuation measure is our stochastic localisation (scale reversed).

Multiscale Bakry–Émery

Theorem. (Bauerschmidt–Bodineau) (formal) If for all \(t, \phi\), with \(\kappa_t \in \mathbb{R}\) (possibly negative),

\(\displaystyle \dot{Q}_t D^2 V_t (\phi) \dot{Q}_t + \frac{1}{2} \ddot{Q}_t \geqslant \kappa_t \dot{Q}_t,\)

then \(\operatorname{Ent}_{\nu_T} (\rho) \leqslant \frac{2}{\gamma} \mathbb{E}_{\nu_T} \| \dot{Q}_T^{1 / 2} D \sqrt{\rho} \|^2\), \(\frac{1}{\gamma} = \int_0^T \mathrm{e}^{- 2 \int_t^T \kappa_r \mathrm{d} r} \mathrm{d} t\).

  • Gradient bound at each scale + entropy formula. Gaussian check: \(V_T = 0\), \(Q_t = tQ_T / T\), \(\kappa = 0\) \(\Rightarrow\) Gaussian LSI.

  • Convexity of the effective potentials, not of \(V_T\).

  • By \(D^2 V_t = S_t^{- 1} - S_t^{- 1} \operatorname{Cov}_t S_t^{- 1}\), for \(\dot{Q}_t\) invertible the hypothesis is equivalent to the bound, uniform in \(\phi\),

    \(\displaystyle \operatorname{Cov}_t \leqslant S_t - S_t \dot{Q}_t^{- 1} \left( \kappa_t \dot{Q}_t - \frac{1}{2} \ddot{Q}_t \right) \dot{Q}_t^{- 1} S_t :\)

    at most \(S_t\) iff \(\kappa_t \dot{Q}_t \geqslant \frac{1}{2} \ddot{Q}_t\) (linear profile: \(\kappa_t \geqslant 0\)).

Poincaré from the localisation

Proposition. If \(\dot{\mathcal{R}}_r \operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_r^{\phi}} \dot{\mathcal{R}}_r \leqslant \alpha_r \dot{\mathcal{R}}_r\) for all \(\phi\), \(r \leqslant t\), then

\(\displaystyle \operatorname{Var}_{\nu_T} (f) \leqslant \mathrm{e}^{\int_0^t \alpha} \mathbb{E}\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_t} (f),\)

and, for \(\nu_T\) log-concave, \(\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_T} (f) \leqslant \mathrm{e}^{\int_0^t \alpha} \mathbb{E} \langle Df, \mathcal{R}_t^{- 1} Df \rangle\).

Proof. \(v (r) =\mathbb{E}\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_r} (f)\) has \(v' = -\mathbb{E} \langle \chi, \dot{\mathcal{R}} \chi \rangle\), \(\chi =\operatorname{Cov} (f, y)\); Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\langle \chi, \dot{\mathcal{R}} \chi \rangle \leqslant \alpha \operatorname{Var} (f)\). Then Brascamp–Lieb for \(\mu_t\).

  • Gaussian, one mode: \(\mathrm{e}^{\int \alpha} = Q_T / S_t\); equality for linear \(f\).

  • KLS conjecture: isotropic log-concave on \(\mathbb{R}^n\), \(\mathcal{R}_t = t\): \(\operatorname{Var} (f) \leqslant t^{- 1} \mathrm{e}^{\int \alpha} \mathbb{E} |Df|^2\). Keep \(\|\operatorname{Cov}_r \|\) bounded as long as possible (only with high probability). Eldan 2013, Chen 2021, Klartag–Lehec 2022, Klartag 2023: Poincaré constant \(O (\log n)\).

Summary

  • Three constructions of the interacting measure, renormalisation, control and filtering, produce one forward–backward system.

  • Potentials: the value function generates the canonical transformation to these coordinates; the Legendre transform of the filter gives \(\Gamma_t\) and the Wetterich equation.
