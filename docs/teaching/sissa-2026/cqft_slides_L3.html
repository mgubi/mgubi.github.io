Stochastic quantisation and stochastic observables

Massimiliano Gubinelli | University of Oxford

SISSA lectures, Lecture 3 — notes: Lecture 3, §§4–6

Where we are

So far. A Euclidean field is a measure on \(\mathcal{S}' (\mathcal{M})\); the GFF \(\mu_m\) is its Gaussian part; local operators are functions of \(\varphi\). Wick powers \(: \varphi^n\): are random distributions iff \(n (d - 2) < d\), and not even tight otherwise.

Today. The GFF as an equilibrium; then index everything by a resolution \(t \in [0, \infty)\).

  1. Stochastic quantisation: the GFF as the stationary marginal of a Langevin dynamics, and as the one-point marginal of an elliptic equation.

  2. The GFF as the terminal value of a martingale of smooth fields; observables \(=\) martingales.

  3. Observables of the GFF: Wick powers in every dimension; products and OPE.

Parabolic stochastic quantisation

Lecture 3

Langevin dynamics (Parisi–Wu 1981)

Finite dimensions: \(\mathrm{d} X_t = - \nabla S (X_t) \mathrm{d} t + \sqrt{2} \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} B_t\) has generator \(\mathcal{L}= \Delta - \nabla S \cdot \nabla\) and

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{L}^{\ast} \mathrm{e}^{- S} = \nabla \cdot (\nabla \mathrm{e}^{- S} + \mathrm{e}^{- S} \nabla S) = 0 :\)

the Gibbs measure \(\mathrm{e}^{- S} \mathrm{d} x / Z\) is invariant.

Apply this to the Euclidean action, with a fictitious stochastic time \(t\):

\(\displaystyle \partial_t \Phi = - (m^2 - \Delta) \Phi - V' (\Phi) + \sqrt{2} \hspace{0.17em} \xi \qquad \text{on } \mathbb{R} \times \mathcal{M},\)

\(\xi\) space-time white noise: \(\mathbb{E} [\xi (u) \xi (v)] = \langle u, v \rangle_{L^2 (\mathbb{R} \times \mathcal{M})}\). (stationary solutions)

For \(V = 0\): the stochastic heat equation (infinite-dimensional OU process).

History: Parisi–Wu 1981 (gauge theories without gauge fixing); physics review Damgaard–Hüffel 1987–88; first mathematical construction for \(P (\varphi)_2\) on \(\mathbb{T}^2\): Jona-Lasinio–Mitter 1985. Review: Gubinelli, Encyclopedia of Mathematical Physics 2025.

DS \(\Rightarrow\) invariance

Itô's formula: for \(F = g (\varphi (f_1), \ldots, \varphi (f_n))\) the generator is

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{L}F (\varphi) = \sum_{i, j} \langle f_i, f_j \rangle \hspace{0.17em} \partial_{ij} g (\ldots) - \sum_i \varphi (Af_i) \hspace{0.17em} \partial_i g (\ldots) .\)

Proposition. \(\mathbb{E}_{\mu_m} [G \hspace{0.17em} \mathcal{L}F] = -\mathbb{E}_{\mu_m} [\langle DG, DF \rangle_{L^2 (\mathcal{M})}]\) for \(F, G \in \mathcal{F}C^{\infty}_b\); in particular \(\mathbb{E}_{\mu_m} [\mathcal{L}F] = 0\).

Proof. Apply DS to \(k \hspace{0.17em} \partial_i g\) with \(h = f_i\) and sum over \(i\):

\(\displaystyle \sum_i \mathbb{E} [k \hspace{0.17em} \partial_i g \hspace{0.17em} \varphi (Af_i)] = \sum_{i, j} \langle f_i, f_j \rangle \mathbb{E} \left[ \partial_j k \hspace{0.17em} \partial_i g + k \hspace{0.17em} \partial_{ij} g \right] . \qquad\)

Static (DS) and dynamic (Langevin) characterisations are two faces of the same integration by parts.

The stationary solution

Theorem. For \(t \in \mathbb{R}\), \(f \in \mathcal{S} (\mathcal{M})\) let \(\Phi_t (f) = \sqrt{2} \hspace{0.17em} \xi (K_{t, f})\), \(K_{t, f} (r, x) = \mathbb{1}_{r < t} (\mathrm{e}^{- (t - r) A} f) (x)\).

  1. \(\mathbb{E} [\Phi_t (f) \Phi_s (g)] = \langle f, \mathrm{e}^{- |t - s|A} Cg \rangle\): stationary, and \(\text{Law} (\Phi_t) = \mu_m\) for every \(t\).

  2. \(\Phi\) is a mild and weak solution: for \(s < t\), \(\Phi_t (h) = \Phi_s (h) - \int_s^t \Phi_r (Ah) \mathrm{d} r + \sqrt{2} \hspace{0.17em} \xi (\mathbb{1}_{(s, t)} \otimes h)\).

  3. From any initial datum \(\Psi_0 \in \mathcal{S}' (\mathcal{M})\) independent of \(\xi\): \(\mathbb{E} \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} \Psi_t (f)} \to \mathrm{e}^{- \frac{1}{2} \langle f, Cf \rangle}\). Hence \(\mu_m\) is the unique invariant measure.

Proof of (a). \(\langle K_{t, f}, K_{s, g} \rangle = \int_{- \infty}^{t \wedge s} \langle f, \mathrm{e}^{- (t + s - 2 r) A} g \rangle \mathrm{d} r = \langle f, \mathrm{e}^{- |t - s|A} (2 A)^{- 1} g \rangle\); an \(\mathcal{S}'\)-valued version exists by Itô–Nawata/Mitoma regularisation.

Proof of (b), (c)

(a), on \(\mathbb{T}^d\). The modes \(\Phi_t (e_n)\) are independent stationary OU processes \(\mathrm{d} X = - \lambda_n X \mathrm{d} t + \sqrt{2} \mathrm{d} B\) with variance \(\lambda_n^{- 1}\).

(b) Mild form from the semigroup identity \(K_{t, h} = K_{s, \mathrm{e}^{- (t - s) A} h} + \mathbb{1}_{(s, t)} \mathrm{e}^{- (t - \cdot) A} h\); weak form by stochastic Fubini (Exercise 4.3).

(c) By independence,

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E} \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} \Psi_t (f)} =\mathbb{E} [\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} \Psi_0 (\mathrm{e}^{- tA} f)}] \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{e}^{- \frac{1}{2} \langle f, (1 - \mathrm{e}^{- 2 tA}) Cf \rangle} .\)

For \(m > 0\), \(\mathrm{e}^{- tA} f \to 0\) in \(\mathcal{S} (\mathcal{M})\), so \(\Psi_0 (\mathrm{e}^{- tA} f) \to 0\) a.s. If \(\Psi_0 \sim \mu_m\) the product is exactly \(\mathrm{e}^{- \frac{1}{2} \langle f, Cf \rangle}\) (invariance); an invariant \(\nu\) must have the same characteristic functional.

Comments

  • As a field on \(\mathbb{R} \times \mathcal{M}\), the stationary solution has covariance \(2 (\partial_t + A)^{- 1} (- \partial_t + A)^{- 1} = 2 (- \partial_t^2 + A^2)^{- 1}\); its equal-time slice is \(2 \int_{\mathbb{R}} (\theta^2 + A^2)^{- 1} \frac{\mathrm{d} \theta}{2 \pi} = A^{- 1}\).

  • The stochastic time \(t\) has nothing to do with the Euclidean time \(\tau\).

  • Interacting case: a singular SPDE.

    • \(\Phi^4_2\): Da Prato–Debussche 2003 (also Jona-Lasinio–Mitter, Albeverio–Röckner).

    • \(\Phi^4_3\): Hairer 2014; Gubinelli–Imkeller–Perkowski 2015; Catellier–Chouk 2018.

    • Global bounds: Mourrat–Weber 2017; Gubinelli–Hofmanová 2019; construction of Euclidean \(\Phi^4_3\) via the dynamics: Gubinelli–Hofmanová 2021.

Elliptic stochastic quantisation

Lecture 3

Dimensional reduction (Parisi–Sourlas 1979)

The elliptic SPDE in \(d + 2\) dimensions

\(\displaystyle (m^2 - \Delta_{\mathbb{R}^2 \times \mathcal{M}}) \Psi + V' (\Psi) = \zeta, \qquad \zeta \text{white noise on } \mathbb{R}^2 \times \mathcal{M},\)

is related to the \(d\)-dimensional Gibbs measure: formally

\(\displaystyle \text{Law} \left( \Psi (z, \cdot)) \propto \exp \left( - 4 \pi \left[ \tfrac{1}{2} \langle \varphi, A \varphi \rangle + \int V (\varphi) \right] \right) \mathcal{D} \varphi . \right.\)

  • Mechanism: a hidden supersymmetry (Parisi–Sourlas; Klein–Landau–Perez 1984).

  • Rigorous nonlinear results: \(d = 0\) (Albeverio–De Vecchi–Gubinelli 2020); polynomial on \(\mathbb{T}^2\) and exponential on \(\mathbb{R}^2\) (ADG 2021); elliptic \(\Phi^4\) in 4 and 5 dimensions (Gubinelli–Hofmanová 2019).

  • For \(V = 0\) it is elementary, and it explains the \(4 \pi\).

The linear case

Theorem. Let \(\textsf{L} = m^2 - \Delta_{\mathbb{R}^2 \times \mathcal{M}} = - \Delta_{\mathbb{R}^2} + A\) and \(\zeta\) white noise on \(\mathbb{R}^2 \times \mathcal{M}\).

  1. \(\textsf{L} \Psi = \sqrt{4 \pi} \hspace{0.17em} \zeta\) has a unique tempered solution \(\Psi (u) = \sqrt{4 \pi} \hspace{0.17em} \zeta (\textsf{L}^{- 1} u)\).

  2. \(K_{z, f} = \textsf{L}^{- 1} (\delta_z \otimes f) \in L^2\), continuous in \(z\); \(\Psi (z, f) = \sqrt{4 \pi} \hspace{0.17em} \zeta (K_{z, f})\) is the section of \(\Psi\) at \(z\).

  3. \(\mathbb{E} [\Psi (z, f) \Psi (z, g)] = \langle f, Cg \rangle\): every section has law \(\mu_m\), and \((\Psi (z, \cdot))_{z \in \mathbb{R}^2}\) is stationary and isotropic.

Uniqueness: \(\textsf{L}\) maps \(\mathcal{S}\) onto \(\mathcal{S}\), so \(D (\textsf{L} u) = 0\) \(\forall u\) forces \(D = 0\).

Proof: where \(4 \pi\) comes from

Fourier transform in \(z\) only: \(\widehat{K_{z, f}} (q) = \mathrm{e}^{- \mathrm{i} q \cdot z} (|q|^2 + A)^{- 1} f\). By Plancherel and the spectral theorem,

\(\displaystyle \langle K_{z, f}, K_{z, g} \rangle = \left\langle f, \left[ \int_{\mathbb{R}^2} \frac{\mathrm{d} q}{(2 \pi)^2} (|q|^2 + A)^{- 2} \right] g \right\rangle = \frac{1}{4 \pi} \langle f, A^{- 1} g \rangle,\)

since

\(\displaystyle \int_{\mathbb{R}^2} \frac{\mathrm{d} q}{(2 \pi)^2} \frac{1}{(|q|^2 + a)^2} = \frac{1}{2 \pi} \int_0^{\infty} \frac{r \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} r}{(r^2 + a)^2} = \frac{1}{4 \pi a} .\)

Multiply by \(4 \pi\). Stationarity and isotropy: the covariance \(4 \pi \int \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} q \cdot (z - z')} \langle f, (|q|^2 + A)^{- 2} g \rangle \frac{\mathrm{d} q}{(2 \pi)^2}\) depends only on \(|z - z' |\).

Slices of Gaussian fields

Field on \(\mathbb{R}^k \times \mathcal{M}\) with covariance \((- \Delta_{\mathbb{R}^k} + A)^{- p}\); its slice \(\{0\} \times \mathcal{M}\) has covariance

\(\displaystyle c_{k, p} \hspace{0.17em} A^{k / 2 - p}, \qquad c_{k, p} = \frac{\Gamma (p - \frac{k}{2})}{(4 \pi)^{k / 2} \Gamma (p)} .\)

\((k, p)\) slice covariance interpretation
\((1, 1)\) \(\frac{1}{2} A^{- 1 / 2}\) time-zero field, \((2 \omega)^{- 1}\)
\((2, 2)\) \((4 \pi)^{- 1} A^{- 1}\) elliptic stochastic quantisation
parabolic \(2 (- \partial_t^2 + A^2)^{- 1} \to A^{- 1}\) Langevin dynamics
\((1, 2)\) \(\propto A^{- 3 / 2}\) not a GFF (e.g. codim-1 slices of the elliptic field)

The GFF arises iff \(p = 1 + \frac{k}{2}\). For \(\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{R}^d\), every codimension-two slice of the elliptic field is a GFF.

The idea

  • Wilson–Kadanoff: physics is organised scale by scale. The measure-on-\(\mathcal{S}'\) picture hides this, and forces singular objects from the start.

  • Observe the field at resolution \(t\): a smooth function \(\varphi_t\). Information at resolution \(t\): \(\mathcal{F}_t = \sigma (\varphi_s : s \leq t)\).

  • A physical quantity is not a random variable but a consistent family of finite-resolution estimates, i.e. a martingale.

Slogan

Observables are martingales in the filtration of finite-resolution measurements. They need not have a terminal value.

Setting: covariance profiles and observables

Lecture 3

Covariance profiles

Definition. A covariance profile is a family \((Q_t)_{t \geq 0}\) of symmetric, translation invariant operators with \(Q_0 = 0\), \(\dot{Q}_t := \partial_t Q_t \geq 0\), \(Q_t \to C = (m^2 - \Delta)^{- 1} = A^{- 1}\), and smooth kernels for \(t < \infty\).

Heat profile:

\(\displaystyle Q_t = C \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{e}^{- A / t^2}, \qquad \dot{Q}_t = \frac{2}{t^3} \mathrm{e}^{- A / t^2}, \qquad \dot{Q}_t (x - y) = \frac{2 t^{d - 3} \mathrm{e}^{- m^2 / t^2}}{(4 \pi)^{d / 2}} \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{e}^{- t^2 |x - y|^2 / 4} \text{on } \mathbb{R}^d .\)

\(\dot{Q}_t\) lives on distances \(\lesssim 1 / t\); \(Q_t\) keeps the modes \(|k| \lesssim t\): \(t\) is a momentum scale.

Others: sharp Fourier cutoff \(\mathbb{1}_{|k| \leq t} (|k|^2 + m^2)^{- 1}\); finite range decompositions, \(\dot{Q}_t (x, y) = 0\) for \(|x - y| \geq 1 / t\).

Notation: \(c_t = Q_t (x, x)\), \(Q_{T, t} = Q_T - Q_t\). For \(d \geq 2\), \(c_t \to \infty\) (\(\frac{1}{2 \pi} \log t\) in \(d = 2\), \(\asymp t^{d - 2}\) in \(d \geq 3\)).

The field at resolution \(t\)

\(W\) cylindrical Brownian motion on \(L^2 (\mathcal{M})\),

\(\displaystyle X_t := \int_0^t \dot{Q}_s^{1 / 2} \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W_s .\)

Proposition. \(X_t\) is a smooth Gaussian field, \(\text{Law} (X_t) = \mu^{Q_t}\); \((X_t (x))_t\) is a continuous martingale with \(\mathrm{d} \langle X (x), X (y) \rangle_t = \dot{Q}_t (x, y) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} t\); \(X_t \to X_{\infty}\) in \(H^{- s}_{\text{loc}}\), \(s > \frac{d}{2} - 1\), with \(\text{Law} (X_{\infty}) = \mu_m\); \(X_T - X_t \perp \mathcal{F}_t\), with law \(\mu^{Q_{T, t}}\).

The GFF is the terminal value of a Gaussian martingale of smooth fields.

Proof (torus). \(X_t = \sum_n \left( \int_0^t \dot{q}_n (s)^{1 / 2} \mathrm{d} B^n_s \right) e_n\): independent Gaussian martingales, variance \(q_n (t) \uparrow q_n (\infty)\); then as in Lecture 2.

Stochastic observables

\(\mathbb{E}_t =\mathbb{E}[\hspace{0.17em} \cdot \hspace{0.17em} |\mathcal{F}_t]\).

Definition. (Bailleul–Chevyrev–G.) An observable is an \((\mathcal{F}_t)\)-martingale \(O = (O_t)_{t \geq 0}\). Its expectation is \(\langle O \rangle =\mathbb{E}O_t\). An observable field is a family \((O (x))_{x \in \mathcal{M}}\).

  • \(Y \in L^1 (\mathcal{F}_{\infty})\) gives a closed observable \(O_t =\mathbb{E}_t Y\).

  • The point: observables need not be closed. \(X_t (x)\), \(d \geq 2\), diverges, yet is a perfectly good family of estimates.

Asymptotic uniqueness

(a) \(\|O^1_t - O^2_t \|_{L^1} \to 0 \Rightarrow O^1 = O^2\).(b) \(N\) adapted, \(\|N_t - O_t \|_{L^1} \to 0 \Rightarrow O_s = \lim_t \mathbb{E}_s N_t\).

Proof. \(\mathbb{E}_s\) is an \(L^1\) contraction.

Brownian observables

For standard one-dimensional Brownian motion:

\(\displaystyle B_t, \qquad B_t^2 - t, \qquad B_t^3 - 3 t B_t\)

are observables without terminal representatives.

The exponential martingale makes the loss of terminal information explicit:

\(\displaystyle Z_t = \exp [\lambda B_t - \lambda^2 t / 2], \qquad \lambda \neq 0,\)
\(\displaystyle Z_t \longrightarrow 0 \hspace{0.3em} \text{a.s.}, \qquad \mathbb{E}Z_t = 1.\)

The limiting random variable is zero; the observable is not.

Repair by the future drift

A semimartingale description of an observable can be repaired (Proposition 5.6):

Suppose \(\mathrm{d} N_t = \beta_t \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} t + \mathrm{d} M_t\) and

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E} \int_0^{\infty} | \beta_s | \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s < \infty .\)

Then the observable in its class is

\(\displaystyle O_t = N_t +\mathbb{E}_t \int_t^{\infty} \beta_s \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s.\)

The correction is the expected drift still to come.

\(\displaystyle N_t = B_t + e^{- t} \sin B_t \quad \Longrightarrow \quad O_t = B_t .\)

A delta at a Brownian endpoint

Fix \(T > 0\) and \(y \in \mathbb{R}\). With \(p_s (z) = (2 \pi s)^{- 1 / 2} e^{- z^2 / (2 s)}\), define

\(\displaystyle \Pi_t^y = p_{T - t} (B_t - y), \qquad t < T.\)

The heat semigroup gives \(\mathbb{E}_s \Pi_t^y = \Pi_s^y\). For bounded test functions,

\(\displaystyle \int_{\mathbb{R}} g (y) \Pi_t^y \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} y =\mathbb{E}_t g (B_T) .\)

At a fixed \(y\), \(\Pi_t^y \rightarrow 0\) almost surely, but \(\mathbb{E} \Pi_t^y = p_T (y) > 0\).

\(\delta (B_T - y)\) is represented by its conditional densities.

Here high resolution means \(t \uparrow T\); one may reparametrise by \(a = (T - t)^{- 1}\). Works for Markov processes.

Germs and locality

An observable is determined by its asymptotics.

  • A germ of \(O\): functionals \(\mathring{O}_t\) of smooth fields with \(\|O_t - \mathring{O}_t (X_t)\|_{L^1} \to 0\). A germ determines \(O\).

  • \(O (x)\) is local if it has germs

    \(\displaystyle \mathring{O}_t (X_t) (x) = \mathring{\mathcal{O}}_t (X_t (x), \nabla X_t (x), \ldots, \nabla^k X_t (x)) .\)

  • Germs are not unique: this freedom is the freedom of choosing counterterms.

Example. \(: X^n_t : (x) = H_n (X_t (x) ; c_t)\) is a local observable in every \(d\) (more later), although \(: \varphi^n\): does not exist as a random distribution when \(n (d - 2) \geq d\).

Observables and their products

Lecture 3

Wick observables in every dimension

Recall \(\partial_c H_n (a ; c) = - \frac{1}{2} \partial_a^2 H_n\), \(\partial_a H_n = nH_{n - 1}\).

Proposition. For every \(d\), \(n\), \(x\): \(: X^n_t : (x) := H_n (X_t (x) ; c_t)\) is a continuous martingale, \(\mathrm{d} \hspace{0.17em} : X^n_t : (x) = n \hspace{0.17em} : X^{n - 1}_t : (x) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} X_t (x)\), and

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E} \left[ : X^n_t : (x) \hspace{0.17em} : X^k_t : (y) \right] = \delta_{nk} \hspace{0.17em} n! \hspace{0.17em} Q_t (x, y)^n \xrightarrow{t \to \infty} \delta_{nk} \hspace{0.17em} n! \hspace{0.17em} G (x - y)^n \quad (x \neq y) .\)

Proof. Itô: the drift is \((\frac{1}{2} \partial_a^2 + \partial_c) H_n \hspace{0.17em} \dot{c}_t = 0\).

  • Lecture 2: \(: \varphi^n\): exists iff \(n (d - 2) < d\). As observables, Wick powers exist in every \(d\), unsmeared.

  • For \(n (d - 2) \geq d\), \(0 \leq f \mathrel{\not\equiv} 0\), \(: X^n_t : (f)\) is unbounded in \(L^2\) and (Paley–Zygmund) not tight: no terminal value; the observable is the whole family.

  • The singularity has moved to products at coincident points.

Products of observables

Definition. \(O^1 \star O^2\) is the observable with \(\|(O^1 \star O^2)_t - O^1_t O^2_t \|_{L^1} \to 0\); unique, and \((O^1 \star O^2)_s = \lim_t \mathbb{E}_s [O^1_t O^2_t]\).

Proposition. If \(O^1, O^2\) are continuous, square integrable on bounded intervals, and \(\mathbb{E} \int_0^{\infty} | \mathrm{d} \langle O^1, O^2 \rangle_s | < \infty\), then

\(\displaystyle (O^1 \star O^2)_t = O^1_t O^2_t +\mathbb{E}_t \left[ \int_t^{\infty} \mathrm{d} \langle O^1, O^2 \rangle_s \right] .\)

Proof. \(O^1 O^2 - \langle O^1, O^2 \rangle\) and \(\mathbb{E}_t \langle O^1, O^2 \rangle_{\infty}\) are martingales.

Brownian deltas at different targets

For \(y \neq z\), the exact product formula is

\(\displaystyle p_{\varepsilon} (B - y) p_{\varepsilon} (B - z) = c_{\varepsilon} e^{- (y - z)^2 / (4 \varepsilon)} p_{\varepsilon / 2} (B - \frac{y + z}{2}) .\)

Its \(L^1\) norm tends to zero as \(\varepsilon \downarrow 0\). Hence

\(\displaystyle \Pi^y \star \Pi^z = 0 \qquad (y \neq z) .\)

At equal targets, the exponential suppression disappears and the leading coefficient diverges.

The product depends dramatically on whether the singular supports meet.

Brownian OPE: resolve the delta square

Put \(\varepsilon = T - u\), fix \(t < u\), and set \(c_{\varepsilon} = (4 \pi \varepsilon)^{- 1 / 2}\). Since

\(\displaystyle p_{\varepsilon} (z)^2 = c_{\varepsilon} p_{\varepsilon / 2} (z),\)

conditioning on the coarse information gives the exact identity

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}_t [(\Pi_{T - \varepsilon}^y)^2] = c_{\varepsilon} p_{T - t - \varepsilon / 2} (B_t - y) .\)

Use \(\partial_s p_s = \frac{1}{2} \partial_y^2 p_s\) to obtain

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}_t [(\Pi_{T - \varepsilon}^y)^2] = c_{\varepsilon} \Pi_t^y - \frac{\varepsilon c_{\varepsilon}}{4} \partial_y^2 \Pi_t^y + \frac{\varepsilon^2 c_{\varepsilon}}{32} \partial_y^4 \Pi_t^y + O_{L^1, t} (\varepsilon^{5 / 2}) .\)
Products of point evaluations

\(\langle \textbf{X} (x), \textbf{X} (y) \rangle_t = Q_t (x - y)\) is deterministic. Heat profile, \(d \geq 2\): the condition holds iff \(x \neq y\), and then

\(\displaystyle (\textbf{X} (x) \star \textbf{X} (y))_t = X_t (x) X_t (y) + G (x - y) - Q_t (x - y), \qquad \langle \textbf{X} (x) \star \textbf{X} (y) \rangle = G (x - y) .\)

The correct two-point function, without ever discussing the regularity of \(X_{\infty}\). For \(x = y\): no product, since \(c_t \to \infty\).

OPE. At every fixed resolution, as \(y \to x\),

\(\displaystyle (\textbf{X} (x) \star \textbf{X} (y))_t - G (x - y) \longrightarrow X_t (x)^2 - c_t = : X^2_t : (x),\)

i.e. \(\textbf{X} (x) \star \textbf{X} (y) = G (x - y) \mathbb{1} + : \textbf{X}^2 : (x) + o (1)\): the Wick square is the regular part of the product.

The operator product expansion

For \(x \neq y\),

\(\displaystyle : \textbf{X}^n : (x) \star : \textbf{X}^k : (y) = \sum_{j \leq n \wedge k} j! \binom{n}{j} \binom{k}{j} \hspace{0.17em} G (x - y)^j \hspace{0.17em} : \textbf{X}^{n - j} (x) \textbf{X}^{k - j} (y) : .\)

  • Wick's theorem at the level of observables.

  • The coefficients \(G (x - y)^j\) carry all singularities; the observables on the right have regular limits as \(y \to x\).

Locality and factorisation

  • \(O^1, O^2\) with germs supported in \(U^1_{1 / t}\), \(U^2_{1 / t}\), \(\text{dist} (U^1, U^2) > 0\): the covariation \(\langle DO^1_t, \dot{Q}_t \hspace{0.17em} DO^2_t \rangle \mathrm{d} t\) sees \(\dot{Q}_t\) only between the supports.

  • Finite range decomposition: it vanishes for large \(t\), so the product exists and is asymptotically the pointwise product. Heat profile: exponentially small.

  • Observables on disjoint regions multiply freely; products at the same point need renormalisation.

Summary

  • Stochastic quantisation: the GFF is the stationary marginal of parabolic and elliptic Langevin dynamics.

  • The GFF is the terminal value of a martingale \(X_t\) of smooth fields; observables are martingales, possibly without terminal value.

  • Wick observables exist in every dimension; products and the OPE live at finite resolution.
