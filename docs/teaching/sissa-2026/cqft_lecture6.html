Abstract

We prove the central estimate of the construction: bounds, uniform in the lattice, on the error \(Z\) of the approximate forecast and on the time the forecast spends at large fields. The two halves of the FBSDE of Lecture 5 give two inequalities, neither of which closes alone. The forward equation becomes, through the field equation of Lecture 4, a cubic elliptic equation at each resolution, with no memory: its noise is the filtered measurement noise, sub-Gaussian uniformly in the interaction. This equation is coercive: testing it bounds the weighted large-field action, scale by scale, by the tails of the noise and by the backward energy. The backward equation is linear in \(Z\): a conditional representation formula bounds the backward energy by an integrable bounded-field source, a boundary term, and a small multiple of the large-field action. The small coefficient is \(C \gamma^2 / q_{\ast}\), independent of the lattice and of the moment order, so one choice of \(q_{\ast}\) closes the loop. The clipping error of Lecture 5 is controlled exactly in this way. We give complete proofs in the scalar (one-site) model, where all the mechanisms are visible with explicit constants, and indicate the changes needed on the lattice.

We then close the argument, for every coupling and every finite mass: the only large number is an auxiliary scale \(q_{\ast}\), internal to the proofs, and below it the estimates follow from those above it because the forecast is a martingale. At a fixed lattice the error of the approximate forecast vanishes at infinite resolution, because the explicit action matches the bare action there; this removes the boundary term of the energy estimate and gives bounds uniform in the lattice (UV stability). The order of limits is essential: first the upper scale at fixed lattice, then the lattice. A linear source changes only the field equation, and this gives quartic exponential tails for tested fields. The same estimates hold for the flow restarted from any field at any scale. So the exact flow is the explicit flow with control \(Z\), of finite relative entropy. This writes the limit measure as an explicit density over an explicit SDE, for the whole lattice sequence, without the stochastic dynamics. Girsanov's theorem then gives the FBSDE on the continuum torus, with no additional noise and with no compactness of the processes, and a renormalised integration by parts formula follows, at the terminal scale and along the scales. The forecast is a continuous function of the observation, the scalar form of the FBSDE has uniqueness in law, and there is a renormalised variational formula with no infinite constant. We end with what is not proved and with the infinite-volume problem. (Joint work with S.-J. Meyer + AI.)

Introduction

At the end of Lecture 5 we had, at each lattice cutoff \(N\), the exact system (Theorem 10.6)

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rl} \mathrm{d} Y_q & = [b_q (Y_q) + H_q (Y_q) Z_q] \mathrm{d} q + H_q (Y_q) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W_q,\\ \mathrm{d} Z_q & = - [b_q (Y_q) + H_q (Y_q) Z_q] \mathrm{d} q + \Xi_q \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W_q, \qquad q \geqslant q_0, \hspace{3cm} \text{(1)} \end{array}\)

where \(Y\) is the approximate forecast built from the explicit action \(\bar{\Gamma}_q\), \(Z = \psi - Y\) is its error, and \(\psi = Y + Z\) is the exact forecast, a martingale. The inputs from Lecture 5, all for \(q \geqslant q_{\ast}\), are:

  1. Global coercivity (Lemma 12.1): \(H_q (y)\) is comparable to \((K_q + \gamma y^2)^{- 1}\) and \(\|H_q (y)\|_{p \to p} \leqslant (1 + \varepsilon_q) / q\), \(\varepsilon_q = C (g_q + g_q^2)\).

  2. The source (Proposition 12.2): \(b = b^{\text{b}} + b^{\text{l}}\) with \(\|b^{\text{b}}_q \|_{\infty} \leqslant C \gamma^{7 / 2} q^{- 5 / 2}\) and \(\|b^{\text{l}}_q (y)\|_2^2 \leqslant C \gamma^4 q^{- 5} \|t_q (y)\|_6^6\), where \(t_q (y) = y \mathbb{1}_{|y| > r_q}\).

  3. The coordinate equation (12.5): \(K_q (Y_q + \varrho_q (Y_q)) + \frac{\gamma}{3} Y_q^3 = K_q \Phi_q = P_q\), with a small Lipschitz correction, \(\|D \varrho_q \|_{p \to p} \leqslant \varepsilon_q\).

The goal is to show that \(Z\) is small and that \(Y\) rarely visits large fields, uniformly in \(N\). This is UV stability. Neither equation can be estimated alone: the forward equation contains \(Z\), and the source of the backward equation is controlled by the large fields of \(Y\).

How a priori estimates for FBSDEs are usually obtained.
For an FBSDE with Lipschitz coefficients one estimates \(\mathbb{E} \|Z_q \|^2\) by Itô's formula and Gronwall, and closes either on a short time interval or under a monotonicity condition [MY99]. Here the time interval is infinite, the coefficients are not uniformly Lipschitz in the relevant norms, and the coupling is not small in a Lipschitz sense. What we have instead is structure:

  • the backward equation is linear in \(Z\), given \(Y\), so it can be solved by a conditional representation formula with an explicit propagator;

  • the forward equation is, by the field equation of Lecture 4, a cubic elliptic equation at each resolution, with no memory, which is coercive and can be estimated pathwise;

  • its noise is a conditional expectation of the measurement noise, hence sub-Gaussian whatever the interaction;

  • the two estimates are linked through two weighted energies whose weights are matched.

Origin of the results. The results of Lectures 5–6 are new. They were obtained recently, with the use of AI, in collaboration with Sarah-Jean Meyer. Detailed proofs of what these lectures cite or condense are in the Supplement.

Where the system starts. The system starts at \(q_0\) with \(Y_{q_0} = Z_{q_0} = 0\). The auxiliary scale \(q_{\ast}\), large depending on \(\lambda\), \(q_0\) and the finite part of the mass, is where the action stops being frozen (Section 12.5). The energies below are integrated from \(q_{\ast}\), and no value of \(Y\) at \(q_{\ast}\) is used: this is what the memoryless field equation makes possible. Below \(q_{\ast}\) everything follows from the martingale property of the forecast (Section 16.3).

Sections 13–15 prove these estimates at a fixed lattice and a finite upper scale. Sections 16–18 remove the cutoffs and identify the limit.

The role of the estimates. Each estimate has one job.

  • Noise bound (Lemma 13.2): the noise \(\zeta\) has free-field tails whatever the interaction. This is where the posterior structure enters.

  • Forward coercivity (Proposition 14.3): pathwise, at each scale, the cube bounds the large-field part of \(Y\) by the noise tails and \(Z\). It controls where the explicit action is inaccurate.

  • Backward representation (Proposition 15.2): \(Z\) is the transported defect. Its energy is bounded by the bounded-field source, a boundary term, and \(C \gamma^2 / q_{\ast}\) times the large-field action. This factor is the only smallness that closes the loop.

  • Boundary (Lemma 16.1): qualitative, at fixed \(N\). It removes the boundary term, and it is why the upper scale goes first.

  • UV stability (Theorem 16.2): the result of these four, \(\mathbb{E} \|Z_q \|_2^2 \leqslant Cq^{- 5 / 2}\) uniformly in \(N\). It says that the control turning the explicit flow into the exact one has finite entropy.

  • Restarted laws (Proposition 16.4): the same from any field at any scale, and below \(q_{\ast}\) by the martingale property of the forecast.

  • Defect on all fields and Lipschitz coordinate (Lemmas 18.1, 16.3): the defect is integrable along both flows, and the explicit flow is a strong continuum SDE, continuous in the cutoff.

  • Terminal tail (Section 17): tightness in \(H^{- s}\), \(s > \frac{1}{2}\).

  • Sources (Proposition 17.2): quartic and Gaussian tails. These are end results, and nothing else depends on them.

So the formula for \(\nu\) (Theorem 18.5) needs the first five at the start of the flow, and if \(q_0 < q_{\ast}\) the deduction below \(q_{\ast}\) of Proposition 16.4 there, all with first moments of the energies; plus the defect, the coordinate, the tail, the consistency of the coefficients, and moments of the explicit flow, which are Itô's formula. The FBSDE then follows by Girsanov's theorem (Theorem 18.6), with no further estimate on the flow, only the convergence of \(H\) and \(b\) and lower semicontinuity. The restart at every \((q, x)\) gives the kernel \(\Pi_q\): the formula (18.16), the statement that the forecast is a continuous function of the field, its convergence at each scale, and Section 18.7; the sources use it at \((q_0, hC_{q_0} f)\). Higher moments of the energies serve only for the stretched exponential bound, the higher moments in Corollary 17.1, and (16.10) with \(p > 1\).

13The energies

13.1Moments at fixed cutoff

All estimates below are first proved on a finite interval \([q_{\ast}, T]\) and at fixed \(N\). There, everything is finite.

Lemma 13.1. (Preliminary moments) At fixed \(N\) and \(T\), the maxima over \([q_{\ast}, T]\) of \(P, Y, \psi, Z\) have all moments, and so does \(\int_{q_{\ast}}^T (\| \text{Cov}_q \|_{\text{HS}}^2 +\| \Xi_q \|_{\text{HS}}^2) \mathrm{d} q\). Consequently the energies \(E_T\), \(J_T\) and \(G_T\) defined below have all moments at fixed \(N, T\).

Proof. \(P\) has all maximal moments, since \(\nu_N\) has all moments. Global convexity, \(D^2 \bar{\Psi}_q \geqslant cq\) and \(D \bar{\Psi}_q (0) = 0\), gives \(\|Y_q \| \leqslant C \|P_q \| / q\). Doob's inequality for the martingale \(\psi_q =\mathbb{E}_q \Phi_{\infty}\) gives its maximal moments, and \(Z = \psi - Y\). For the integrands, \(\|\operatorname{Cov}_q \|_{\text{HS}} \leqslant \text{Tr} \operatorname{Cov}_q \leqslant \mathbb{E}_q \| \Phi_{\infty} \|^2\), and \(\|H_q (Y_q)\|_{\text{HS}} \leqslant C \sqrt{\dim H_N} / q\). The energies involve, besides \(Y\) and \(Z\), the field \(\zeta_q\) of Section 13.2 and \(\varrho_q (Y_q)\); finite-dimensional norm equivalence and \(\| \varrho_q (y)\| \leqslant \varepsilon_q \|y\|\) give their moments.\(\Box\)

These constants depend on \(N\) and \(T\). They are used only to justify the absorptions below, never in the final bounds.

13.2The field equation

Lecture 5 realises the process as a measurement: by (10.5), \(P_q = (q - q_0) \Phi_{\infty} + B_{q - q_0}\), with \(B\) a Brownian motion. Since \(\psi_q =\mathbb{E}_q \Phi_{\infty}\), the noise seen at resolution \(q\) is explicit:

\(\displaystyle \eta_q := P_q - (q - q_0) \psi_q =\mathbb{E}_q [B_{q - q_0}] . \) (13.1)

This is the noise observable of Theorem B.1 of Lecture 4 in the profile (10.2), where \(S_q = C_q\) and the regulator is \(q - q_0\). Its bound (Proposition B.3 of Lecture 4) is here one line.

Lemma 13.2. (The noise) For every convex \(\Psi\) on \(H_N\), \(\mathbb{E} \Psi (\eta_q) \leqslant \mathbb{E} \Psi (B_{q - q_0})\), whatever the interaction. In particular \(\zeta_q := C_q \eta_q\) satisfies, for all \(x\) and \(u \in \mathbb{R}\),

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E} \mathrm{e}^{u \zeta_q (x)} \leqslant \exp \left( \frac{u^2}{2} (q - q_0) a^2 (q) \right), \qquad (q - q_0) a^2 (q) \leqslant C \sqrt{q}, \) (13.2)

uniformly in \(N\).

Proof. Conditional Jensen in (13.1), with \(\Psi (f) = \mathrm{e}^{u \langle C_q \delta_x, f \rangle}\), and (10.3).\(\Box\)

So \(\zeta_q\) has the tails of the free field at resolution \(q\). It is not Gaussian, and it depends on the interaction through \(\psi\), but its bounds do not. Insert \(P_q = \eta_q + (q - q_0) (Y_q + Z_q)\) into the coordinate equation (C), and use \(K_q = K_{q_0} + q - q_0\). With

\(\displaystyle V_q := Y_q + \varrho_q (Y_q) - \zeta_q, \) (13.3)

(\(V_q\) is not to be confused with the potentials \(V_t\), \(V_N\) of Lectures 4–5) we obtain the field equation

\(\displaystyle K_{q_0} V_q + \frac{\gamma}{3} Y_q^3 = (q - q_0) (\zeta_q - \varrho_q (Y_q) + Z_q), \qquad Y_q = V_q + \zeta_q - \varrho_q (Y_q) . \) (13.4)

This is the equation we estimate: the field equation (B.4) of Lecture 4 in these coordinates. Three remarks:

  • it is an elliptic equation at each resolution, with no memory: the past enters only through the noise \(\eta_q\);

  • the only trace of the backward equation is \((q - q_0) Z_q\), at the same resolution;

  • the identity is pathwise: no expectation has been taken.

The mass of (13.4) is only \(q_0\), so at large resolution all the coercivity must come from the cube. The next section shows that it does.

13.3Thresholds and energies

Define the signed excess of \(V\) over three quarters of the threshold, the large-field part of \(Y\), and the large values of the noise,

\(\displaystyle w_q = \text{sgn} (V_q) \left( |V_q | - \tfrac{3}{4} r_q \right)_+, \qquad t_q = Y_q \mathbb{1}_{|Y_q | > r_q}, \qquad \xi_q = \zeta_q \mathbb{1}_{| \zeta_q | > r_q / 32},\) (13.5)

all pointwise in space, with \(r_q = (q / \gamma)^{1 / 2}\). The three random energies are

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{rlll} E_T & = \frac{1}{\gamma} \int_{q_{\ast}}^T q^{1 / 2} \|Z_q \|_2^2 \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} q & & \text{(backward energy)},\\ J_T & = \int_{q_{\ast}}^T q^{- 3 / 2} [q\|w_q \|_4^4 + \gamma \|w_q \|_6^6 + \gamma \|t_q \|_6^6] \mathrm{d} q & & \text{(large-field action)},\\ G_T & = \int_{q_{\ast}}^T [q^{- 1 / 2} \| \xi_q \|_4^4 + \gamma q^{- 3 / 2} \| \xi_q \|_6^6] \mathrm{d} q & & \text{(noise tails)} . \hspace{3cm} \text{(13.6)} \end{array}\)

Only thresholded sixth powers are integrated over all scales: \(\mathbb{E} \| \zeta_q \|_6^6\) can be of order \(q^{3 / 2}\), so an unthresholded noise contribution to \(J\) would not be integrable uniformly in \(N\). The weights are matched to each other and to the source bounds:

quantity weight reason
\(\|Z_q \|_2^2\) \(q^{1 / 2}\) forcing: \(q^{- 3 / 2} \cdot \gamma^{- 1} q^2 \|Z\|_2^2\)
\(\gamma \|t_q \|_6^6\) \(q^{- 3 / 2}\) source: \(q^{7 / 2} \|b^{\text{l}} \|^2 \leqslant C \gamma^3 \cdot \gamma q^{- 3 / 2} \|t\|_6^6\)
\(\|b^{\text{l}}_q \|_2^2\) \(q^{7 / 2}\) backward propagator \(s / q\) and \(\int_q^{\infty} s^{- 3 / 2} \mathrm{d} s = 2 q^{- 1 / 2}\)

14Forward coercivity

14.1The scalar model

On one site the Laplacian disappears. We also drop the small Lipschitz correction \(\varrho\) (restored in Proposition 14.3) and treat the noise and the error as given numbers. Then (13.4) is a scalar equation at each \(q\), and all the constants can be checked by hand.

Lemma 14.1. (Scalar forward coercivity) Let \(\gamma, q > 0\), \(a \geqslant 0\), \(0 \leqslant p \leqslant q\) and \(v, g, z \in \mathbb{R}\) with

\(\displaystyle av + \frac{\gamma}{3} (v + g)^3 = p (g + z) . \) (14.1)

Let \(r = (q / \gamma)^{1 / 2}\), \(w =\operatorname{sgn} (v) (|v| - \frac{3}{4} r)_+\), \(\xi = g \mathbb{1}_{|g| > r / 32}\). Then

\(\displaystyle \frac{q}{8} w^4 + \frac{\gamma}{16} w^6 \leqslant 54 q \xi^4 + \frac{4225}{6} \gamma \xi^6 + \frac{12}{\gamma} q^2 z^2 . \) (14.2)

Moreover \(t = (v + g) \mathbb{1}_{|v + g| > r}\) satisfies \(|t| \leqslant \frac{32}{7} (|w| + | \xi |)\).

Proof. Multiply (14.1) by \(w^3\), where \(w \neq 0\); there \(|v| = |w| + \frac{3}{4} r\) and \(v, w\) have the same sign, so \(avw^3 \geqslant 0\). Since \(|g| \leqslant \frac{r}{32} + | \xi |\), \(\operatorname{sgn} (w) (v + g) \geqslant |w| + \frac{11}{16} r - | \xi |\) (we give up \(\frac{1}{32} r\) to leave room, on the lattice, for \(\varrho\)). The cube being increasing, (14.3) and \(\gamma r^2 = q\) give

\(\displaystyle \frac{\gamma}{3} w^3 (v + g)^3 \geqslant \frac{\gamma}{12} w^6 + \frac{q}{4} w^4 + \frac{qr}{13} |w|^3 - \frac{4225}{6} \gamma \xi^6 .\)

This is the large-field coercivity: above the threshold the cube provides a sixth power, a quartic term of the size of a mass \(q\), and a cubic term which dominates the noise below the threshold. On the right side, \(pgw^3 \leqslant \frac{qr}{32} |w|^3 + q | \xi | |w|^3\), and the first term is absorbed. By Young's inequality, \(q | \xi | |w|^3 \leqslant \frac{q}{8} w^4 + 54 q \xi^4\) and \(q |z| |w|^3 \leqslant \frac{\gamma}{48} w^6 + \frac{12}{\gamma} q^2 z^2\). This gives (14.2). For the last claim, \(|v + g| \leqslant |w| + \frac{3}{4} r + \frac{r}{32} + | \xi |\), so on \(\{|v + g| > r\}\) we have \(\frac{7}{32} |v + g| \leqslant |v + g| - \frac{25}{32} r \leqslant |w| + | \xi |\).\(\Box\)

Lemma 14.2. (Two elementary inequalities) For \(x, \rho \geqslant 0\), \((x - \rho)^3 \geqslant \frac{3}{4} x^3 - 65 \rho^3\). For \(u, \rho, r \geqslant 0\),

\(\displaystyle \frac{1}{3} u^3 \left( u + \frac{11}{16} r - \rho \right)^3 \geqslant \frac{1}{12} u^6 + \frac{1}{4} r^2 u^4 + \frac{1}{13} r^3 u^3 - \frac{4225}{6} \rho^6 . \) (14.3)

Proof. \(\frac{1}{4} x^3 + 64 \rho^3 - 3 x^2 \rho = \frac{1}{4} (x - 8 \rho)^2 (x + 4 \rho) \geqslant 0\), so \((x - \rho)^3 \geqslant x^3 - 3 x^2 \rho - \rho^3 \geqslant \frac{3}{4} x^3 - 65 \rho^3\). Apply it with \(x = u + b\), \(b = \frac{11}{16} r\): \(\frac{1}{3} u^3 (x - \rho)^3 \geqslant \frac{1}{4} u^3 x^3 - \frac{65}{3} u^3 \rho^3\), and \(x^3 \geqslant u^3 + 3 b^2 u + b^3\) with \(\frac{3}{4} b^2 = \frac{363}{1024} r^2 > \frac{1}{4} r^2\) and \(\frac{1}{4} b^3 = \frac{1331}{16384} r^3 > \frac{1}{13} r^3\). Finally \(\frac{65}{3} u^3 \rho^3 \leqslant \frac{1}{6} u^6 + \frac{4225}{6} \rho^6\), and \(\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{12}\).\(\Box\)

What the scalar lemma says.
The excess of the forecast over the threshold is controlled, at each resolution separately, by two things only: how large the noise is, whose tails are known and exponentially small at large \(q\), and the error \(Z\). There is no smallness in the coupling: the estimate is uniform in \(\gamma\) once the threshold scales like \(r_q = (q / \gamma)^{1 / 2}\). It is pathwise, and it needs no integration in \(q\).

14.2The lattice version

Proposition 14.3. (Forward coercivity) For \(q_{\ast}\) large (depending on \(\gamma\)), at every cutoff \(N\), pathwise and for every \(q \geqslant q_{\ast}\),

\(\displaystyle q \|w_q \|_4^4 + \gamma \|w_q \|_6^6 + \gamma \|t_q \|_6^6 \leqslant C (q\| \xi_q \|_4^4 + \gamma \| \xi_q \|_6^6 + \gamma^{- 1} q^2 \|Z_q \|_2^2) . \) (14.4)

Consequently, for every \(T\),

\(\displaystyle J_T \leqslant C (G_T + E_T), \qquad \|G_T \|_{L^p (\mathbb{P})} \leqslant C \gamma^3 p^5 \quad (p \geqslant 1), \) (14.5)

with \(C\) independent of \(N\), \(T\) and \(p\).

Proof: the changes from the scalar case. Test (13.4) with \(w_q^3\) in \(L^2 (\Lambda_N)\) and proceed as in Lemma 14.1, with Hölder's inequality in space. Three new terms appear.

The elliptic term. \(\langle w^3, K_{q_0} V \rangle \geqslant 0\). Indeed \(w^3 = \phi' (V)\) for the convex function \(\phi (v) = \frac{1}{4} (|v| - \frac{3}{4} r)_+^4\), \(Vw^3 \geqslant 0\), and for any nondecreasing \(\phi'\),

\(\displaystyle \langle \phi' (V), - \Delta_N V \rangle = N^{- 1} \sum_{x \sim x'} (\phi' (V (x)) - \phi' (V (x'))) (V (x) - V (x')) \geqslant 0,\)

summing over unordered nearest-neighbour pairs. So the elliptic term only helps.

The coordinate correction. Split \(\varrho_q (y) = \varrho_q^0 + \varrho_q^1\) according to \(|y| \leqslant r_q\) or not (writing \(\varrho_q (y) = L_q (y) y\) with \(L\) the averaged derivative). Then \(\| \varrho_q^0 \|_{\infty} \leqslant \varepsilon_q r_q \leqslant r_q / 32\) shifts the threshold, and \(\| \varrho_q^1 \|_j \leqslant \varepsilon_q \|t_q \|_j\), \(j = 4, 6\). Then \(|Y| \leqslant |w| + \frac{13}{16} r_q + | \xi | + | \varrho^1 |\); on \(\{|Y| > r_q \}\) this gives \(\frac{3}{16} |Y| \leqslant |w| + | \xi | + | \varrho^1 |\), so for small \(\varepsilon\)

\(\displaystyle \|t_q \|_j \leqslant C (\|w_q \|_j +\| \xi_q \|_j), \qquad \| \varrho_q^1 \|_j \leqslant C \varepsilon_q (\|w_q \|_j +\| \xi_q \|_j) .\)

This also supplies the \(t\) part of (14.4). These are operator bounds: \(\varrho\) is nonlocal, but small in every \(L^p\). In the cube and on the right side of (13.4) the error is \(\rho = | \xi | + | \varrho^1 |\). The bounded parts on the right cost \(\frac{qr}{16} \|w\|_3^3\), which is absorbed by \(\frac{qr}{13} \|w\|_3^3\) from the cube. The cube error is at most \(C \gamma (\| \xi \|_6^6 + \varepsilon^6 \|w\|_6^6)\), and Young's inequality gives \(Cq (\| \xi \|_4^4 + \varepsilon^4 \|w\|_4^4)\). The \(w\) parts are absorbed for small \(\varepsilon\): these are deterministic smallness conditions on \(g_{q_{\ast}}\), independent of \(p\).

The error. \(| \langle w^3, (q - q_0) Z \rangle | \leqslant \frac{\gamma}{48} \|w\|_6^6 + \frac{12}{\gamma} q^2 \|Z\|_2^2\). Multiplying (14.4) by \(q^{- 3 / 2}\) and integrating gives \(J_T \leqslant C (G_T + E_T)\).

Tails of the noise. By Minkowski's inequality in \(x\), for \(j = 4, 6\), \(\|\| \xi_q \|_j^j \|_{L^p (\mathbb{P})} \leqslant \sup_x \|| \xi_q (x) |^j \|_{L^p (\mathbb{P})}\), and by Hölder and (13.2), with threshold \(r_q / 32 = \frac{1}{32} (q / \gamma)^{1 / 2}\), this is at most

\(\displaystyle (\mathbb{E}| \zeta_q (x) |^{2 jp})^{\frac{1}{2 p}} \mathbb{P} (| \zeta_q (x) | > r_q / 32)^{\frac{1}{2 p}} \leqslant C_j p^{j / 2} q^{j / 4} \mathrm{e}^{- c \sqrt{q} / (p \gamma)},\)

and integrating in \(q\) with \(u = \sqrt{q} / (p \gamma)\) gives \(\|G_T \|_p \leqslant C \gamma^3 p^5\).\(\Box\)

No information on the interacting field has been used, apart from the equation itself and the posterior structure of the noise. The tails of \(\zeta\) are those of the free field whatever the interaction: this replaces the subtraction of a Gaussian field.

15Backward feedback and closure

15.1The backward representation

The backward equation in (1) is linear in \(Z\). Its propagator is the adapted operator solution of

\(\displaystyle \partial_s \mathcal{P}_{q, s} =\mathcal{P}_{q, s} H_s (Y_s), \qquad \mathcal{P}_{q, q} = I, \qquad s \geqslant q.\) (15.1)

By (A), \(\|\mathcal{P}_{q, s} \|_{2 \to 2} \leqslant \exp \int_q^s \frac{1 + \varepsilon_u}{u} \mathrm{d} u \leqslant L_0 \hspace{0.17em} \frac{s}{q}\), with \(L_0 = \exp (\int_{q_{\ast}}^{\infty} \varepsilon_u \frac{\mathrm{d} u}{u}) \leqslant \exp (C (g_{q_{\ast}} + g_{q_{\ast}}^2))\).

Lemma 15.1. (Conditional representation) For \(q_{\ast} \leqslant q \leqslant T\), at fixed \(N\),

\(\displaystyle Z_q =\mathbb{E}_q \left[ \mathcal{P}_{q, T} Z_T + \int_q^T \mathcal{P}_{q, s} \hspace{0.17em} b_s (Y_s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s \right] .\) (15.2)

Proof. By the product rule and (1), \(\mathrm{d}_s (\mathcal{P}_{q, s} Z_s) =\mathcal{P}_{q, s} H_s Z_s \mathrm{d} s -\mathcal{P}_{q, s} (b_s + H_s Z_s) \mathrm{d} s +\mathcal{P}_{q, s} \Xi_s \mathrm{d} W_s = -\mathcal{P}_{q, s} b_s \mathrm{d} s +\mathcal{P}_{q, s} \Xi_s \mathrm{d} W_s\). The stochastic integral is a true martingale by Lemma 13.1 and the deterministic bound on \(\mathcal{P}\). Integrate from \(q\) to \(T\) and take \(\mathbb{E}_q\).\(\Box\)

So \(Z\) is the expected source accumulated on the unresolved scales, transported back to \(q\), plus the boundary error. This is the conditional-expectation form of a backward equation, as in \(F_t =\mathbb{E}_t [F_T]\) for the force (Theorem 7.4); here the source is the defect.

15.2Three contributions

Split \(Z = Z^{\text{t}} + Z^{\text{b}} + Z^{\text{l}}\) according to the terminal term and the two parts of the source. We estimate each in the scalar manner: norms of the propagator times norms of the source.

Bounded fields. By (B),

\(\displaystyle \|Z^{\text{b}}_q \|_2 \leqslant \int_q^{\infty} L_0 \frac{s}{q} \hspace{0.17em} C \gamma^{7 / 2} s^{- 5 / 2} \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s = 2 L_0 C \gamma^{7 / 2} q^{- 3 / 2}, \qquad \frac{1}{\gamma} \int_{q_{\ast}}^{\infty} q^{1 / 2} \|Z^{\text{b}}_q \|_2^2 \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} q \leqslant C \gamma^6 q_{\ast}^{- 3 / 2} .\)

This is where the two correction steps of Lecture 5 are used: with one step the source would be \(O (s^{- 2})\) and the first integral would diverge logarithmically.

Boundary. \(q \|Z^{\text{t}}_q \|_2 \leqslant L_0 \hspace{0.17em} \mathbb{E}_q \|TZ_T \|_2\), so, with \(\delta_{N, T, p} := \|TZ_T \|^2_{L^{2 p} (\mathbb{P}; L^2)}\),

\(\displaystyle \left| \frac{1}{\gamma} \int_{q_{\ast}}^T q^{1 / 2} \|Z^{\text{t}}_q \|_2^2 \mathrm{d} q \right|_{L^p (\mathbb{P})} \leqslant \frac{L_0^2}{\gamma} \int_{q_{\ast}}^{\infty} q^{- 3 / 2} \mathrm{d} q \hspace{0.27em} \delta_{N, T, p} = \frac{2 L_0^2}{\gamma q_{\ast}^{1 / 2}} \hspace{0.17em} \delta_{N, T, p} .\)

At fixed \(N\), \(\delta_{N, T, p} \to 0\) as \(T \to \infty\) (Lemma 16.1).

Large fields. Let \(A^{\text{l}} := \int_{q_{\ast}}^T s^{7 / 2} \|b^{\text{l}}_s (Y_s)\|_2^2 \mathrm{d} s \leqslant C \gamma^4 \int s^{- 3 / 2} \|t_s \|_6^6 \leqslant C \gamma^3 J_T\) by (B). By Cauchy–Schwarz in \(s\),

\(\displaystyle \|Z^{\text{l}}_q \|_2 \leqslant \frac{L_0}{q} \hspace{0.17em} \mathbb{E}_q \int_q^T s \|b^{\text{l}}_s \|_2 \mathrm{d} s \leqslant \frac{L_0}{q} \left( \int_q^{\infty} s^{- 3 / 2} \mathrm{d} s \right)^{1 / 2} \mathbb{E}_q [(A^{\text{l}})^{1 / 2}],\)

so that, by Jensen's inequality, \(\|Z^{\text{l}}_q \|_2^2 \leqslant 2 L_0^2 q^{- 5 / 2} \hspace{0.17em} \mathbb{E}_q A^{\text{l}}\). Integrating, and using that \(\mathbb{E}_q\) is a contraction on \(L^p (\mathbb{P})\),

\(\displaystyle \left| \frac{1}{\gamma} \int_{q_{\ast}}^T q^{1 / 2} \|Z^{\text{l}}_q \|_2^2 \mathrm{d} q \right|_{L^p} \leqslant \frac{2 L_0^2}{\gamma} \int_{q_{\ast}}^{\infty} q^{- 2} \mathrm{d} q \hspace{0.17em} \|A^{\text{l}} \|_{L^p} \leqslant \frac{C \gamma^2}{q_{\ast}} \hspace{0.17em} \|J_T \|_{L^p} .\)

This is how the clipping error is controlled. The large-field source \(b^{\text{l}}\) is not small; but its squared \(L^2\) norm is controlled by the sixth power of the large-field part, \(\|b^{\text{l}}_q \|_2^2 \leqslant C \gamma^4 q^{- 5} \|t_q \|_6^6\) (B), and after transport by the propagator and integration with the energy weight it contributes to the backward energy only through \(J_T\), with the small factor \(\gamma^2 / q_{\ast}\).

Proposition 15.2. (Backward feedback) At every cutoff,

\(\displaystyle \|E_T \|_{L^p} \leqslant C \gamma^6 q_{\ast}^{- 3 / 2} + C \gamma^{- 1} q_{\ast}^{- 1 / 2} \hspace{0.17em} \delta_{N, T, p} + C \gamma^2 q_{\ast}^{- 1} \|J_T \|_{L^p},\) (15.3)

with \(C\) independent of \(N\), \(T\) and \(p\).

Proof. \(\|Z\|^2 \leqslant 3 (\|Z^{\text{t}} \|^2 +\|Z^{\text{b}} \|^2 +\|Z^{\text{l}} \|^2)\) and the three estimates above.\(\Box\)

15.3Closing the loop

Theorem 15.3. (Cutoff-uniform energy estimate) There is \(q_{\ast}\), depending on \(\gamma\), such that for every \(N\), \(T\) and \(p \geqslant 1\),

\(\displaystyle \|E_T \|_{L^p} + \|J_T \|_{L^p} \leqslant C_{\gamma} (1 + p^5 + \delta_{N, T, p}) .\) (15.4)

Proof. Let \(x = \|E_T \|_p\) and \(y = \|J_T \|_p\). By Propositions 14.3 and 15.2,

\(\displaystyle y \leqslant A_0 \hspace{0.17em} (C \gamma^3 p^5 + x), \qquad x \leqslant B_0 + B_1 \delta_{N, T, p} + \eta \hspace{0.17em} y, \qquad \eta = \frac{C \gamma^2}{q_{\ast}},\)

with \(A_0, B_0, B_1\) and the constant in \(\eta\) independent of \(N, T, p\), and bounded for all \(q_{\ast}\) beyond the thresholds already required. Choose \(q_{\ast}\) such that also \(A_0 \eta \leqslant \frac{1}{2}\). Then \(y \leqslant A_0 (C \gamma^3 p^5 + B_0 + B_1 \delta_{N, T, p}) + \frac{1}{2} y\). Since \(y < \infty\) by Lemma 13.1, it can be absorbed, and \(x\) follows.\(\Box\)

The structure of the argument is the point:

Forward coercivity estimates the large fields using the backward energy; the backward representation returns that energy with a small coefficient. Neither half closes on its own. The smallness is in the feedback, \(\gamma^2 / q_{\ast}\), not in the coupling of either equation, and it is independent of the moment order, so a single \(q_{\ast}\) closes the loop for every \(p\). The growth \(p^5\) is then inherited from the tails of the noise, \(G_T\), and gives stretched exponential moments \(\mathbb{E} \exp \{c (E_{\infty} + J_{\infty})^{1 / 5} \} < \infty\) once the boundary term is removed. Note that \(\gamma^2 / q_{\ast} = g_{q_{\ast}}^2\).

15.4Consequences

The boundary term \(\delta_{N, T, p}\) is removed in Section 16, at each fixed \(N\), by letting \(T \to \infty\). Assuming this for the moment, we obtain

\(\displaystyle \sup_N \|E^N_{\infty} \|_{L^p} + \sup_N \|J^N_{\infty} \|_{L^p} \leqslant C_{\gamma} (1 + p^5), \qquad \sup_N \mathbb{E} \|Z^N_q \|_2^2 \leqslant C_{\gamma} q^{- 5 / 2} .\) (15.5)

The second bound follows from the representation formula on \([q, \infty)\): the three contributions are, up to the factor \(3\), \(O (q^{- 3})\), \(0\) and, by the computation above, \(2 L_0^2 q^{- 5 / 2} \mathbb{E}A^{\text{l}}\). So the approximate forecast differs from the exact one by \(O (q^{- 5 / 4})\) in \(L^2 (\mathbb{P}; L^2)\) (root mean square), uniformly in the lattice. Since \(\|H_q \| \leqslant C / q\), the coupling term \(H_q Z_q\) in the forward equation is \(O (q^{- 9 / 4})\) in \(L^2 (\mathbb{P}; L^2)\), integrable in \(q\): the forward equation is, up to an integrable drift, the explicit SDE \(\mathrm{d} Y = b (Y) \mathrm{d} q + H (Y) \mathrm{d} W\). The same computation holds along the path. At \(T = \infty\) the boundary part vanishes (Lemma 16.1) and, with \(A^{\text{l}}\) now integrated up to \(\infty\) (it has all moments, since \(A^{\text{l}} \leqslant C \gamma^3 J_{\infty}\)), \(q^{5 / 2} \|Z_q \|_2^2 \leqslant 3 (C \gamma^7 q_{\ast}^{- 1 / 2} + 2 L_0^2 \mathbb{E}_q A^{\text{l}})\). Since \(\mathbb{E}_q A^{\text{l}}\) is a martingale, Doob's inequality gives, for \(p \geqslant 2\),

\(\displaystyle \| \sup_{q \geqslant q_{\ast}} q^{5 / 2} \|Z_q \|_2^2 \|_{L^p (\mathbb{P})} \leqslant C_{\gamma} (1 + p^5) . \) (15.6)

Remark 15.4. (Comparison with the variational method) In the Boué–Dupuis approach (Theorem 9.1) one estimates a drift \(u\) which is the costate up to \(\dot{Q}^{1 / 2}\), and the renormalisation is a change of variables \(u = - 4 \lambda \dot{Q}^{1 / 2} : X^3 : + r\) (in the normalisation \(\lambda \int : \varphi^4\): of Lecture 4) [BG20]. Here the explicit action \(\bar{\Gamma}\) plays the role of all such changes of variables at once, in the forecast coordinate, and \(Z\) plays the role of the remainder \(r\). The coercivity of the quartic term is used in both, but here it is used pathwise in the forward equation, while the remainder is estimated in expectation through the backward equation. The precise statement is Theorem 18.13.

Removing the cutoffs

Sections 13–15 proved, at every lattice cutoff \(N\) and every upper scale \(T\),

\(\displaystyle \|E_T \|_{L^p} + \|J_T \|_{L^p} \leqslant C_{\gamma} (1 + p^5 + \delta_{N, T, p}), \qquad \delta_{N, T, p} = \|TZ_T \|^2_{L^{2 p} (\mathbb{P}; L^2)},\) (2)

for the backward energy \(E_T\) and the large-field action \(J_T\) of (13.6). Three things remain.

  1. Show that the boundary term vanishes: \(TZ_T \to 0\) as \(T \to \infty\) at fixed \(N\) (Section 16).

  2. Turn the uniform energy bounds into tightness of the measures (Section 17).

  3. Identify the limit: the deterministic coefficients; then, from the restarted flow, the limit as an explicit density over an explicit flow, along the whole sequence; then the equations, read off by Girsanov's theorem (Section 18).

The structure of (iii) is instructive: convergence of the fields alone does not suffice to pass to the limit in an FBSDE, because the backward martingale might converge to something with extra noise (Remark 18.7). Here the statistical meaning of the backward variable, a posterior mean, saves us: the limit is written as an explicit density over an explicit flow, and the equations are read off from it.

16The boundary at infinite resolution

16.1The boundary lemma

At a fixed lattice, the terminal resolution is \(q = \infty\), and the field at resolution \(q\), \(\Phi_q = C_q P_q\), converges to the terminal field \(\Phi_{\infty}\). The approximate action \(\bar{\Gamma}_q\) was built to converge to the bare action: \(\mu_q \to \mu_N\), \(\Theta_q \to 0\), and the nonlinear corrections were integrated from \(q = \infty\), so that they vanish there. This is the boundary condition of the backward equation.

Lemma 16.1. (Boundary lemma) At fixed \(N\), for every \(p < \infty\), as \(q \to \infty\),

\(\displaystyle \|Y_q - \Phi_{\infty} \|_{L^p (\mathbb{P}; H_N)} + \|qZ_q \|_{L^p (\mathbb{P}; H_N)} \leqslant C_{N, p} \hspace{0.17em} q^{- 1 / 2}, \qquad \text{hence} \qquad \delta_{N, T, p} \leqslant C_{N, p} T^{- 1} .\) (16.1)

The constants depend on \(N\); this is not a uniform estimate.

Proof. All norms on \(H_N\) are equivalent, and we do not track the dependence on \(N\).

The field at resolution \(q\). By (10.5), \(\Phi_q = C_q P_q = (q - q_0) C_q \Phi_{\infty} + C_q B_{q - q_0}\), and \((q - q_0) C_q - I = - C_q K_{q_0} = O (q^{- 1})\), \(\|C_q B_{q - q_0} \|_{L^p} = O (q^{- 1 / 2})\). So \(\| \Phi_q - \Phi_{\infty} \|_{L^p} \leqslant C_{N, p} q^{- 1 / 2}\).

The approximate forecast. Global convexity gives \(\|Y_q \| \leqslant C \|P_q \| / q\), bounded in every \(L^p\) uniformly in \(q\). The coordinate equation \(\Phi_q = Y_q + C_q D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q)\) and the bounds \(| \mu_q | \leqslant C_N\), \(\| \Theta_q \|_{\text{row}} = O_N (q^{- 1 / 2})\) and \(\|D \tilde{h}_q (y)\|_{\infty} \leqslant C \textsf{S}_4 (h^{[2]}_q) \|y\|_{\infty}^3 \leqslant C \gamma^2 q^{- 1 / 2} \|y\|_{\infty}^3\) (Taylor from the fourth order, since the low jets vanish) show that \(D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q)\) is bounded in \(L^p\), so \(\|Y_q - \Phi_q \|_{L^p} = O (q^{- 1})\). Moreover \(| \mu_q - \mu_N | = O_N (q^{- 1})\), and therefore

\(\displaystyle \|D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q) - DV_N (\Phi_{\infty})\|_{L^p} \leqslant C_{N, p} q^{- 1 / 2} .\) (16.2)

Thus the explicit force at the approximate forecast converges to the bare force at the terminal field.

The error. Subtract the approximate coordinate identity \(P_q = K_q Y_q + D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q)\) from the exact posterior identity (10.10), \(P_q = K_q \psi_q +\mathbb{E}_q [DV_N (\Phi_{\infty})]\):

\(\displaystyle qZ_q = qC_q [D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q) -\mathbb{E}_q DV_N (\Phi_{\infty})] .\) (16.3)

Let \(G = DV_N (\Phi_{\infty})\) and \(G^0_q = DV_N (\Phi_q)\), which is \(\mathcal{F}_q\)-measurable. Then \(\|\mathbb{E}_q G - G\|_{L^p} \leqslant \|\mathbb{E}_q (G - G^0_q)\|_{L^p} + \|G^0_q - G\|_{L^p} \leqslant 2 \|G - G^0_q \|_{L^p} = O (q^{- 1 / 2})\), by the cubic Lipschitz bound for \(DV_N\) and the first step. With (16.2) and \(\|qC_q \| \leqslant 1\) this proves the claim.\(\Box\)

The proof uses no estimate uniform in \(N\), and no concentration of the posterior: only that the observed estimator \(\Phi_q = C_q P_q\), which is \(\mathcal{F}_q\)-measurable, converges to the hidden field. In Lecture 4 language, the forecast and the field at resolution \(q\) have the same limit at infinite resolution, and the force \(F_q = K_q (\psi_q - \Phi_q)\) converges to the bare force \(- DV_N (\Phi_{\infty})\) (Exercise 16.1; recall \(F_q =\mathbb{E}_q [- DV_N (\Phi_{\infty})]\) by (10.9), so this is the conditional-expectation step of the proof).

16.2UV stability

Now let \(T \to \infty\) at fixed \(N\) in (2): the paths and the law \(\nu_N\) do not depend on \(T\), \(E_T\) and \(J_T\) increase to \(E_{\infty}\) and \(J_{\infty}\), and \(\delta_{N, T, p} \to 0\) by Lemma 16.1. Only then take the supremum over \(N\).

Theorem 16.2. (UV stability [FBSDE26]) For \(q_{\ast}\) large enough, depending on \(\gamma\),

\(\displaystyle \sup_N (\|E^N_{\infty} \|_{L^p} +\|J^N_{\infty} \|_{L^p}) \leqslant C_{\gamma} (1 + p^5), \qquad \sup_N \mathbb{E} \|Z^N_q \|_2^2 \leqslant C_{\gamma} q^{- 5 / 2},\) (16.4)

and \(\sup_N \mathbb{E} \exp \{c (E^N_{\infty} + J^N_{\infty})^{1 / 5} \} < \infty\) for small \(c > 0\).

Proof. Monotone convergence in (2), then the representation formula (15.2) on \([q, \infty)\), whose terminal term vanishes by Lemma 16.1. The exponential moment follows from the polynomial growth by Markov's inequality with \(p \asymp R^{1 / 5}\).\(\Box\)

The order of limits matters. The constant \(C_{N, p}\) in Lemma 16.1 diverges with \(N\), and we never take a supremum over \(N\) of the remainder \(C_{N, p} / T\). The infinite-resolution boundary is used only qualitatively, at each lattice separately.

16.3Restarted laws

The coordinate is Lipschitz for the uniform norm, by the maximum principle.

Lemma 16.3. (Lipschitz coordinate) Uniformly in \(N\), \(\|y_s (x) - y_s (x')\|_{\infty} \leqslant 3 \|x - x' \|_{\infty}\) for \(s \geqslant q_{\ast}\) and with the constant \(6\) for \(s < q_{\ast}\). So the drift in (18.2) is Lipschitz on \(L^{\infty}\) with constant \(7 / s\).

Proof. With \(\delta = y - y'\), \(h = x - x'\), \(a = \delta + \varrho_s (y) - \varrho_s (y')\), (12.5) gives \(K_s (a - h) + \frac{\gamma}{3} (y^3 - (y')^3) = 0\). At a positive maximum of \(a - h\), \(K_s (a - h) \geqslant s (a - h) > 0\), so \(y^3 < (y')^3\) there, i.e. \(\delta < 0\) and \(a - h \leqslant \| \varrho_s (y) - \varrho_s (y')\|_{\infty} + \|h\|_{\infty}\); similarly at a negative minimum. Since \(\varrho_s\) is \(\varepsilon_s\)-Lipschitz on \(L^{\infty}\), \(\| \delta \|_{\infty} \leqslant 2 \|h\|_{\infty} + 2 \varepsilon_s \| \delta \|_{\infty}\). For \(s < q_{\ast}\) use (12.18) and \(\|C_{q_{\ast}} K_s \|_{\infty \to \infty} \leqslant 2\). Finally \(\|C_s \|_{\infty \to \infty} = 1 / s\).\(\Box\)

The estimates above use the start of the flow only through the noise. So they hold, with the changes below, for the flow restarted from any field at any scale, and these estimates are the input of Section 18.2. Let \(\mathrm{e}^{- V_q (x)} =\mathbb{E} \mathrm{e}^{- V_N (x + \int_q^{\infty} C_s \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W_s)}\) be the exact potential, and \(\mathbb{P}^{q, x}\) the exact flow conditioned on \(\Phi_q = x\), \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \Phi = - C_s^2 DV_s (\Phi) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + C_s \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W\). Fix \(N\), \(q \geqslant q_0\) and \(x \in H_N\), \(R = \|x\|_{\infty}\). By the Markov property of the flow, \(\mathbb{P}^{q, x}\) is the law of \(P_t = K_q x + (t - q) \hat{\Phi} + B'_{t - q}\), \(t \geqslant q\), where \(B'\) is a Brownian motion independent of \(\hat{\Phi}\), and \(\hat{\Phi}\) has the law

\(\displaystyle \nu^{[q, x]} (\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} z) \propto \exp (\langle z, K_q x \rangle - \frac{1}{2} \langle z, K_q z \rangle - V_N (z)) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} z. \) (16.5)

Note that \(\nu^{[q_0, 0]} = \nu_N\), so \(\mathbb{P}^{q_0, 0}\) is the flow itself. The posterior given \(P_t\) is \(\propto \exp (\langle z, P_t \rangle - \frac{1}{2} \langle z, K_t z \rangle - V_N (z)) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} z\), the same function of \(P_t\) as for \(\nu_N\). So \(\psi_t = D\mathcal{K}_t (P_t)\), and \(Y_t\), \(Z_t = \psi_t - Y_t\) and the system (1) are unchanged, with the same coefficients and with \(Y_q = y_q (x)\), \(\|Y_q \|_{\infty} \leqslant 6 R\) by Lemma 16.3. Let \(\eta_t = P_t - (t - q) \psi_t = K_q x +\mathbb{E}_t B'_{t - q}\), \(\zeta_t = C_t \eta_t\). Then

\(\displaystyle \zeta_t = d_t + \zeta^0_t, \quad d_t = C_t K_q x, \quad \zeta^0_t = C_t \mathbb{E}_t B'_{t - q} . \) (16.6)

Here \(C_t K_q = I - (t - q) C_t\) and \((t - q) C_t\) has a positive kernel of total mass at most \(1\), so \(\|d_t \|_{\infty} \leqslant 2 R\). By conditional Jensen, \(\zeta^0\) satisfies Lemma 13.2, with \((t - q) a_2 (t) \leqslant C \sqrt{t}\). With the field \(V_t = Y_t + \varrho_t (Y_t) - \zeta_t\) of (13.3) (not the potential \(V_t (x)\), always written with its argument), the computation of (13.4) with \(q_0\) replaced by \(q\) gives the field equation

\(\displaystyle K_q V_t + \frac{\gamma}{3} Y_t^3 = (t - q) (\zeta_t - \varrho_t (Y_t) + Z_t) . \) (16.7)

17Tightness and tails

17.1One Hilbert space

To compare lattices, partition \(\mathbb{T}^3\) into cubic cells \(\square_x\) of side \(N^{- 1}\) centred at \(x \in \Lambda_N\), and let

\(\displaystyle (\iota_N u) (z) = u (x) (z \in \square_x), \qquad (j_N f) (x) = N^3 \int_{\square_x} f, \qquad \textsf{P}_N = \iota_N j_N .\)

\(\iota_N : H_N \to H = L^2 (\mathbb{T}^3)\) is an isometry, \(j_N = \iota_N^{\ast}\), and \(\textsf{P}_N \to I\) strongly. Fields are embedded by \(\iota_N\), operators by \(\iota_N \cdot j_N\). The embedded innovation has covariance \(\textsf{P}_N\); in the limit it becomes a cylindrical Brownian motion on \(H\), obtained as the limit of the lattice innovations.

17.2The terminal field

The approximate forecast converges at infinite resolution in negative Sobolev spaces, uniformly in \(N\) (to the same limit as the forecast \(\psi\)). The martingale part of \(Y\) has bracket \(\int H^2\), and the positive inequality \(H_q^2 \leqslant \frac{C}{q} C_q\) gives

\(\displaystyle \int_Q^{\infty} \|(1 - \Delta)^{- s / 2} H_q \|_{\text{HS}}^2 \mathrm{d} q \leqslant C \int_Q^{\infty} \frac{1}{q} \sum_k \frac{(1 + |k|^2)^{- s}}{q + c |k|^2} \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} q \leqslant C_s \hspace{0.17em} Q^{- (s - \frac{1}{2})}, \qquad \tfrac{1}{2} < s < \tfrac{3}{2},\) (17.1)

where \(q + c |k|^2\) bounds the lattice symbol from below (aliases of the cell embedding only increase the frequency). The drifts are integrable at infinity in \(H\): the bounded-field drift is \(O (q^{- 5 / 2})\), the large-field drift is controlled by \(\int q^{7 / 2} \|b^{\text{l}} \|^2 \leqslant C \gamma^3 J_{\infty}\) and Cauchy–Schwarz, and \(\int \|H_q Z_q \| \mathrm{d} q \leqslant C \int q^{- 1} \|Z_q \| \leqslant C \gamma^{1 / 2} E_{\infty}^{1 / 2}\). More precisely, the squared drift tails beyond \(Q\) are \(O (Q^{- 3})\) (bounded-field), \(O (Q^{- 5 / 2}) \gamma^3 J_{\infty}\) (large-field) and \(O (Q^{- 3 / 2}) \gamma E_{\infty}\) (the \(HZ\) term), all at most \(Q^{- (s - \frac{1}{2})}\) for \(s < \frac{3}{2}\). By Doob's inequality, for \(\frac{1}{2} < s < \frac{3}{2}\),

\(\displaystyle \sup_N \mathbb{E} \sup_{t \geqslant Q} \|Y_t - Y_Q \|^2_{H^{- s}} \leqslant C_s \hspace{0.17em} Q^{- (s - \frac{1}{2})} .\) (17.2)

Letting \(t \to \infty\) at fixed \(N\) (Lemma 16.1) this holds with \(Y_t\) replaced by \(\Phi_{\infty}\).

The field at the lower scale. Proposition 14.3 at \(q = q_{\ast}\) gives \(\gamma \|t_{q_{\ast}} \|_6^6 \leqslant C (q_{\ast} \| \xi_{q_{\ast}} \|_4^4 + \gamma \| \xi_{q_{\ast}} \|_6^6 + \gamma^{- 1} q_{\ast}^2 \|Z_{q_{\ast}} \|_2^2)\), and Lemma 15.1 gives \(\|Z_{q_{\ast}} \|_2^2 \leqslant C (\gamma^7 q_{\ast}^{- 3} + q_{\ast}^{- 5 / 2} \mathbb{E}_{q_{\ast}} A^{\text{l}})\). Since \(\|Y_{q_{\ast}} \|_2 \leqslant r_{q_{\ast}} + \|t_{q_{\ast}} \|_6\), it has all moments, uniformly in \(N\). So \(\sup_N \mathbb{E} \| \iota_N \Phi^N_{\infty} \|_{H^{- s'}}^2 < \infty\) for \(\frac{1}{2} < s' < \frac{3}{2}\), and as \(H^{- s'}\) embeds compactly in \(H^{- s}\) for \(s > s'\):

Corollary 17.1. (Tightness of the lattice measures) For every \(s > \frac{1}{2}\) the embedded laws \(\nu_N \circ \iota_N^{- 1}\) are tight in \(H^{- s} (\mathbb{T}^3)\), and \(\sup_N \mathbb{E} \| \iota_N \Phi^N_{\infty} \|^p_{H^{- s}} < \infty\) for every \(p\).

For the moments: the drift integrals have all moments by (16.4), and the \(H^{- s}\) martingale has a deterministic bracket bound by (17.1) at \(Q = q_{\ast}\), the field at the lower scale has all moments by the paragraph above, so the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality gives all moments.

The threshold \(s > \frac{1}{2}\) is that of the free field in \(d = 3\): the interacting field is no rougher than the Gaussian one. At every finite resolution its part \(V\) is smoother (\(H^1\)).

17.3Exponential moments

Theorem 16.2 gives only stretched exponential moments of the energies. Hairer and Steele [HSt22] showed that the measure has quartic exponential tails, as its formal density suggests, by tilting it and using a priori bounds for the tilted dynamics. Here is a proof in the language of this lecture, through linear sources: the filter is available for every prior, and the noise bound does not depend on the prior.

Let \(f \in H_N\), \(h \in \mathbb{R}\), and let \(\hat{\nu} \propto \mathrm{e}^{h \langle \varphi, f \rangle} \nu_N\), which has all moments at fixed \(N\). Observe \(\hat{\Phi} \sim \hat{\nu}\) through the same channel, with \(\hat{\psi}_q =\mathbb{E}_q \hat{\Phi}\). Keep the action, and shift the observation:

\(\displaystyle K_q Y_q + D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q) = P_q + hf, \quad Z_q = \hat{\psi}_q - Y_q . \) (17.3)

Proposition 17.2. (The source changes the field equation, not the system) For \(q \geqslant q_{\ast}\): the pair \((Y, Z)\) solves (1) with the same \(b, H\); \(TZ_T \to 0\) in every \(L^p\) at fixed \(N\); the field equation (13.4) holds with the extra term \(hf\) on the right side. If \(q_1 \geqslant q_{\ast}\) and \(q_1^{3 / 2} \geqslant 70 \gamma^{1 / 2} |h| \|f\|_{\infty}\), then the conclusion of Theorem 16.2 holds on \([q_1, \infty)\), with constants independent of \(N, h, f, q_1\).

Proof. This is the flow restarted at \((q_0, x)\) with \(x = hC_{q_0} f\): then \(\nu^{[q_0, x]} = \hat{\nu}\) and \(P_q + hf = K_{q_0} x + (q - q_0) \hat{\Phi} + B_{q - q_0}\). So the system and the boundary are as in Section 16.3; the extra term in the field equation is \((q - q_0) d_q + K_{q_0} d_q = hf\) with \(d_q = hC_q f\). Since \(\|d_q \|_{\infty} \leqslant |h| \|f\|_{\infty} / q \leqslant r_q / 64\) for \(q \geqslant q_1\), as \(64 < 70\), the proof of Proposition 16.4 applies with no range below the threshold. The backward estimate and the closure only improve with \(q_1\).\(\Box\)

There is also an exact identity with no cube and no derivative. As \(\eta_q = P_q - (q - q_0) \hat{\psi}_q\) and \(P_q = (q - q_0) \hat{\Phi} + B_{q - q_0}\), for every prior and \(q > q_0\),

\(\displaystyle \hat{\Phi} = Y_q + Z_q + \frac{\eta_q - B_{q - q_0}}{q - q_0} . \) (17.4)

Theorem 17.3. (Quartic tails) Let \(\|f\|_{\ast} = \|f\|_1 + \|f\|_2 + \|f\|_{\infty}\). There are \(\beta, C\) independent of \(N\) and \(f\) such that

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}| \langle \Phi_{\infty}^N, f \rangle | \leqslant C \|f\|_{\ast}, \quad \mathbb{E} \exp (\beta \langle \Phi_{\infty}^N, f \rangle^4 / \|f\|_{\ast}^4) \leqslant C. \) (17.5)

\(\displaystyle |X| \leqslant r_q \|f\|_1 + \|t_q \|_6 \|f\|_{6 / 5} + \|Z_q \|_2 \|f\|_2 + \frac{2}{q} | \langle \eta_q - B_{q - q_0}, f \rangle |.\)

By Proposition 17.2, \(\hat{\mathbb{E}} J_{\infty} \leqslant C\) on \([q_h, \infty)\); since \(\gamma (2 q_h)^{- 3 / 2} \int_{q_h}^{2 q_h} \|t_q \|_6^6 \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} q \leqslant J_{\infty}\), Hölder's inequality gives the average of \(\hat{\mathbb{E}} \|t_q \|_6\) at most \(C (q_h^{1 / 2} / \gamma)^{1 / 6}\). 18Identifying the limit

18.1The deterministic coefficients

The coefficients \(M^N_q\), \(h^{[j], N}_q\), \(H^N_q (y)\), \(b^N_q (y)\) are constructed at each lattice. Their uniform bounds (Proposition 11.1) do not by themselves identify a limit: a weakly convergent vertex cannot in general be contracted against a varying covariance. The three tools are:

  • Weak vertex measures. Derivative kernels with bounded rooted norms are signed measures with bounded mass; they converge weakly along subsequences, and the centred kernel \(\Theta_q\) converges in a weak topology controlled by its first moment. The limiting coefficients solve the same fixed-point problem, whose solution is unique in its ball; so the whole sequence converges.

  • Compact sandwich. If \(G_N \to G\) in Hilbert–Schmidt norm, and \(\textsf{B}_N\) are symmetric, uniformly bounded and converge weakly to \(\textsf{B}\), with \(\sup_N \|G_N \textsf{B}_N \| \leqslant \theta < 1\) and \(\|G \textsf{B} \| \leqslant \theta\), then \((I + G_N \textsf{B}_N)^{- 1} G_N \to (I + G \textsf{B})^{- 1} G\) in Hilbert–Schmidt norm. The free resolvent is the compact factor that turns weak convergence of the Hessian into strong convergence of the response.

  • Contraction against a moving covariance. The rows of the inverses are Hölder (any exponent \(a < \frac{1}{2}\)); with the Hilbert–Schmidt convergence given by the sandwich, the squared kernels \((H^N_q)^2\) converge uniformly to a continuous kernel. Weakly convergent vertices of bounded variation can therefore be contracted against them, and the trace steps of the recursion pass to the limit.

With these, if \(Y^N_q \to Y_q\) uniformly on \(\mathbb{T}^3\) for almost every \(q \in I\), with \(\sup_I \|Y^N \|_{\infty}\) bounded, in probability, then \(H^N_q (Y^N_q) \to H_q (Y_q)\) and \(b^N_q (Y^N_q) \to b_q (Y_q)\) in \(L^2 (I ; \text{HS})\) and \(L^2 (I ; H)\), in probability. By the resolvent identity \(H^N = (I - H^N D^2 \bar{\Gamma}^N) C^N\), for bounded \(y\), \(H^N\) is small in Hilbert–Schmidt norm on high modes, uniformly in \(N\); so the Fourier projection and \(j_N\) give the same limit, and \(\int_I \iota_N H^N (Y^N) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W^N \to \int_I H (Y) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W\) in \(H\), in probability, when \(W^N\) is the projection of \(W\), and the processes are adapted to a filtration for which \(W\) is a Brownian motion. For \(q < q_{\ast}\) this holds with the frozen coefficients, as \(D \Upsilon\) in (12.19) is the trace part of the gradient, and \(Y\) is continuous across \(q_{\ast}\) although \(b\) jumps.

18.2The exact flow as a controlled explicit flow

Compactness would give limit points, and nothing in the FBSDE selects among them. A change of measure does better, following the idea of Gubinelli and Meyer for sine–Gordon [GM24]: the exact flow is an explicit flow with control \(Z\), and that control has finite relative entropy.

Let \(y = y_q (x)\) solve \(K_q y + D \bar{\Gamma}_q (y) = K_q x\), and let \(\bar{V}_q (x) = \bar{\Gamma}_q (y) + \frac{1}{2} \langle x - y, K_q (x - y) \rangle\) be the Moreau transform of the action. Recall the defect \(e_q\) and the exact potential \(V_q\) of Section 16.3, with force \(F_q = - DV_q\). By the envelope theorem and \(Dy = H_q K_q\),

\(\displaystyle \partial_q \bar{V} + \frac{1}{2} \text{Tr} (C_q^2 D^2 \bar{V}) - \frac{1}{2} \|C_q D \bar{V} \|_2^2 = e_q \circ y + c (q), \quad c (q) = \frac{1}{2} \text{Tr} (C_q - H_q (0)) . \) (18.1)

So \(\bar{V}\) solves the Polchinski equation up to its defect. Since \(C_s^2 D \bar{V}_s (x) = C_s (x - y_s (x))\), the explicit flow \(\mathbb{R}^{q, x}\) from \(x\) at scale \(q\) is

\(\displaystyle \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} x = - C_s (x - y_s (x)) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + C_s \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W, \quad x_q = x. \) (18.2)

Its coordinate \(Y = y_s (x_s)\) solves \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} Y = b (Y) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + H (Y) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W\), \(b = - HDe\): indeed \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} (K_s x) = Y \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W\), and one applies Itô's formula to \(K_s Y + D \bar{\Gamma}_s (Y) = K_s x\). It contains no \(Z\). All this holds for all \(q \geqslant q_0\); below \(q_{\ast}\) the defect is (12.19).

Lemma 18.1. (The defect on all fields) Let \(\pi_s\) be the pointwise projection on \([- r_s, r_s]\). For all \(y\) and \(s \geqslant q_{\ast}\),

\(\displaystyle |e_s (y) | \leqslant C \gamma^5 s^{- 3} \| \pi_s y\|_4^4 + C \gamma^3 s^{- 5 / 2} \|t_s (y)\|_6^6 . \) (18.3)

Proof. On the ball \(e_s = e_s^{\text{o}}\), so \(|e_s (\pi_s y) | \leqslant C \gamma^5 s^{- 3} \| \pi_s y\|_4^4\) by the zero jets and (11.12). With \(z_{\theta} = \pi_s y + \theta (y - \pi_s y)\), \(e_s (y) - e_s (\pi_s y) = \int_0^1 \langle De_s (z_{\theta}), y - \pi_s y \rangle \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \theta\), and \(y - \pi_s y\) is supported on the set \(\mathcal{A}\) where \(|y| > r_s\), with norm at most \(\|t_s \|_2\). Write \(De = D \kappa De^{\text{o}} \circ \kappa +\mathfrak{D}\). Then \(|De^{\text{o}} | \leqslant S_4 (e^{\text{o}}) (2 r_s)^3 \leqslant C \gamma^{7 / 2} s^{- 3 / 2}\) and \(|\mathcal{A}|^{1 / 2} \leqslant r_s^{- 1} \|t_s \|_2\), so the first term contributes at most \(C \gamma^4 s^{- 2} \|t_s \|_2^2\). By the term-by-term bounds behind Proposition 12.2 (the four groups of Section 12.3 of Lecture 5, [FBSDE26]), which bound \(\mathfrak{D}\) before multiplication by \(H\) (the bound stated for \(b^{\text{l}} = - H\mathfrak{D}\) would not suffice, as \(H^{- 1}\) is unbounded), \(\|\mathfrak{D}_s (z)\|_2 \leqslant Cg_s (\|t_s (z)\|_2 + r_s^{- 1} \|z^2 \chi_s (z)\|_2)\); since \(|z_{\theta} | \leqslant |y|\) and \(z_{\theta}\) has size above \(r_s\) only on \(\mathcal{A}\), the second term contributes at most \(Cg_s (\|t_s \|_2^2 + r_s^{- 1} \|t_s \|_4^2 \|t_s \|_2)\). On the support of \(t_s\) we have \(|t_s | \geqslant r_s\), so \(\|t_s \|_2 \leqslant r_s^{- 2} \|t_s \|_6^3\) and \(\|t_s \|_4^2 \leqslant r_s^{- 1} \|t_s \|_6^3\). All these are at most \(Cg_s r_s^{- 4} \|t_s \|_6^6 = C \gamma^3 s^{- 5 / 2} \|t_s \|_6^6\).\(\Box\)

With \(\Delta = V - \bar{V}\) (\(\Delta\) is not the Laplacian in this section), \(\mathcal{C}_N (q) = \int_q^{\infty} c (s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s\) and \(A_N (q, x) = V_q (x) - \bar{V}_q (x) -\mathcal{C}_N (q)\), subtracting (18.1) from the Polchinski equation gives

\(\displaystyle \partial_s \Delta + \frac{1}{2} \text{Tr} (C_s^2 D^2 \Delta) - \langle C_s^2 D \bar{V}_s, D \Delta \rangle - \frac{1}{2} \|C_s D \Delta \|_2^2 + e_s \circ y_s + c (s) = 0. \) (18.4)

This is a Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation with the control \(u\), added as a drift \(C_s u_s\) to the explicit flow, \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} Y = (b (Y) + H (Y) u) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + H (Y) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W\), and running cost \(e + c + \frac{1}{2} \|u\|^2\), with optimal feedback \(u^{\ast} = - C_s D \Delta_s\). Moreover \(- C_s D \Delta_s = C_s (F_s - \bar{f}_s) = Z_s\), since \(F = K (\psi - x)\) and \(\bar{f} = - D \bar{V} = - K (x - y)\). So the exact flow is the explicit flow with control \(Z\).

Theorem 18.2. (Density and sandwich) At fixed \(N\), \(\mathbb{P}^{q, x}\) and \(\mathbb{R}^{q, x}\) are equivalent on the paths on \([q, \infty)\), and

\(\displaystyle \frac{\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \mathbb{P}^{q, x}}{\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \mathbb{R}^{q, x}} = \mathrm{e}^{A_N (q, x)} \exp (- \int_q^{\infty} e_s (Y_s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s), \quad \mathrm{e}^{- A_N (q, x)} =\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{R}^{q, x}} \exp (- \int_q^{\infty} e_s (Y_s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s) . \) (18.5)

Further,

\(\displaystyle \text{Ent} (\mathbb{P}^{q, x} |\mathbb{R}^{q, x}) = \frac{1}{2} \int_q^{\infty} \mathbb{E}^{q, x} \|Z_s \|_2^2 \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s, \) (18.6)
\(\displaystyle A_N (q, x) = \text{Ent} (\mathbb{P}^{q, x} |\mathbb{R}^{q, x}) +\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}^{q, x}} \int_q^{\infty} e_s (Y_s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s, \) (18.7)

and so

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}^{q, x}} \int_q^{\infty} e_s (Y_s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s \leqslant A_N (q, x) \leqslant \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{R}^{q, x}} \int_q^{\infty} e_s (Y_s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s. \) (18.8)

Uniformly in \(N\), for \(\|x\|_{\infty} \leqslant R\) and \(T \geqslant q^{\vee}\),

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}^{q, x}} \int_T^{\infty} |e_s (Y_s) | \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s +\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{R}^{q, x}} \int_T^{\infty} |e_s (Y_s) | \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s \leqslant C_R T^{- 1 / 2} . \) (18.9)

The integrals over \([q, q_{\ast}]\), if \(q < q_{\ast}\), are at most \(C_R\). In particular \(|A_N (q, x) | \leqslant C_R\).

Proof. Density on \([q, T]\). Both are absolutely continuous with respect to the free flow from \(x\), \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \xi = C_s \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W\). As \(\mathrm{e}^{- V}\) is harmonic the first density is \(\mathrm{e}^{- V_T (\xi_T) + V_q (x)}\). By (18.1) and Itô's formula the second is \(\exp (- \bar{V}_T (\xi_T) + \bar{V}_q (x) + \int_q^T (e_s (y_s (\xi_s)) + c (s)) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s)\); it is a true martingale since the drift is Lipschitz at fixed \(N\). So

\(\displaystyle M_T : = \frac{\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \mathbb{P}^{q, x}}{\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \mathbb{R}^{q, x}} |_{[q, T]} = \exp (\Delta_q (x) - \Delta_T (x_T) - \int_q^T (e_s (Y_s) + c (s)) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s) .\)

Entropy on \([q, T]\). Under \(\mathbb{P}^{q, x}\) the explicit flow has the extra drift \(C_s Z_s\), so \(\log M_T = \int_q^T \langle Z_s, \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W_s \rangle + \frac{1}{2} \int_q^T \|Z_s \|_2^2 \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s\), and \(\text{Ent}_T = \frac{1}{2} \mathbb{E} \int_q^T \|Z\|_2^2\), as the stochastic integral is a true martingale at fixed \(N\). So \(M_T\) is uniformly integrable under \(\mathbb{R}^{q, x}\), and \(\mathbb{P}^{q, x} \ll \mathbb{R}^{q, x}\) on \([q, \infty)\) with density \(M_{\infty} = \text{lim}_T M_T\), and (18.6) holds.

The limit of \(M_T\). Both laws have, at fixed \(N\), \(x_T \to x_{\infty}\) almost surely. Under \(\mathbb{P}^{q, x}\), \(x_T = C_T P_T \to \hat{\Phi}\), from \(P_T = K_q x + (T - q) \hat{\Phi} + B'_{T - q}\). Under \(\mathbb{R}^{q, x}\), \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} Y = b (Y) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + H (Y) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W\) with \(\|b (Y)\|_2 \leqslant C \gamma s^{- 3 / 2} \|Y\|_2\) and \(\|H\| \leqslant C / s\), so \(\sup_s \|Y_s \|_2\) is finite and \(Y_s\) converges almost surely, the drift being integrable and the martingale bounded in \(L^2\); and \(x - y = \varrho_s (y) + \frac{\gamma}{3} C_s y^3 = O_N (s^{- 1} (1 + \|y\|_{\infty}^3)) \to 0\). Then \(\Delta_T (x_T) \to 0\): at fixed \(N\) the coefficients converge with rates and \(y - x = O_N (T^{- 1} (\|x\|_{\infty} + \|x\|_{\infty}^3))\), so \(\bar{V}_T \to V_N\), and \(V_T \to V_N\) by Gaussian integration, uniformly on bounded sets. By the bounds below \(\int_q^{\infty} |e_s (Y_s) | \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s < \infty\) almost surely under both laws, and \(c\) is integrable at fixed \(N\). So \(M_{\infty} = \exp (\Delta_q (x) -\mathcal{C}_N (q) - \int_q^{\infty} e) = \mathrm{e}^{A_N} \mathrm{e}^{- \int e}\), which is positive; hence the equivalence and (18.5), the second formula by \(\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{R}} M_{\infty} = 1\).

The identity. Take \(\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}}\) of \(\log M_{\infty}\) in (18.5): this is (18.7), the integrability coming from (18.9). The left inequality in (18.8) is \(\text{Ent} \geqslant 0\), the right one Jensen's inequality in (18.5). So the exact and explicit potentials are compared using only expectations of the defect and the entropy of the control.

The expectations. For \(s < q_{\ast}\) use (12.20): under \(\mathbb{P}^{q, x}\) by (16.10); under \(\mathbb{R}^{q, x}\) by Itô's formula for \(\|Y\|_2^2\), the bounds (12.20) and \(\|Y_q \|_2 \leqslant 6 R\). Under \(\mathbb{P}^{q, x}\): the second term integrates on \([T, \infty)\) to at most \(C \gamma^2 T^{- 1} J_{\infty}\). For the first, with \(\| \pi_s Y\|_4^4 \leqslant r_s^2 \|Y\|_2^2\), (16.9) gives a bound \(C_R T^{- 1 / 2}\). Under \(\mathbb{R}^{q, x}\): with \(\ell_s = Y_s - Y_q\), by the linear bound \(\|b (Y)\|_p \leqslant C \gamma s^{- 3 / 2} \|Y\|_p\) of Proposition 12.2, valid for every \(p\), \(\text{diag} H^2 \leqslant Cs^{- 1 / 2}\) and Itô's formula, \(\mathbb{E} \| \ell_s \|_j^j \leqslant C (s^{j / 4} + (g_{q^{\vee}} R)^j)\) for \(j = 2, 6, 12\), with \(C\) depending on \(q_{\ast}\) if \(q < q_{\ast}\), by (12.20) on \([q, q_{\ast}]\); in that case read \(g_q\) below as \(g_{q_{\ast}}\), only \(C_R\) being claimed. 18.3The limit law

Couple the lattices by common Fourier Gaussians, \(W^N\) being the projection of a cylindrical \(W\) on a fixed probability space. The consistency of the fixed-scale coefficients of Section 18.1 gives the following, along \(b_N \to b\).

Lemma 18.3. (Consistency) If \(x_N \in H_N\), \(x\) is continuous, and the interpolations \(\iota_N x_N \to x\) uniformly, then for each \(s\), \(\iota_N y^N_s (x_N) \to y_s (x)\) uniformly, and \(e^N_s (y^N_s (x_N)) \to e_s (y_s (x))\).

Proof. For \(y\): boundedness by the maximum principle, equicontinuity by Lemma 16.3 and translation covariance, uniqueness of the coordinate equation among continuous \(y\) by testing with the difference, as \(y - x \in H^1\). For \(e\): \(e_s (0) = 0\), so \(e_s (y) = \int_0^1 \langle De_s (ty), y \rangle \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} t\). By the consistency of the coefficients [FBSDE26], \(De^N_s (\phi_N)\) converges weakly in \(L^2\) at each \(s\) for smooth bounded \(\phi\), and uniformly in \(N\), \(\|De^N_s (y) - De^N_s (z)\|_2 \leqslant C (1 + \|z\|_{\infty}) (\|y - z\|_2 + \|y^2 - z^2 \|_2)\). A three-term approximation extends the weak convergence to uniformly convergent fields, and dominated convergence in \(t\) gives the claim. Moreover \(|e^N_s (y) | \leqslant Cg_s \|y\|_2^2\) by the global linear bound on \(De\) behind Proposition 12.2 [FBSDE26], which dominates on \([q, T]\). For \(s < q_{\ast}\) the same applies to \(\Upsilon\) in (12.19), and \(\|y\|_2^2\) is continuous; for \(y_s\) use (12.18) and the convergence of the kernel of \(C_{q_{\ast}}\) in \(L^1\).\(\Box\)

By Lemma 16.3, \(x = \xi + v\) with \(\xi_t = x_q + \int_q^t C_s \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W_s\) and \(v\) solving an ODE on \(C (\mathbb{T}^3)\), uniquely, for each path \(\xi \in C ([q, T] ; L^{\infty})\). So the explicit flow \(\mathbb{R}^{q, x}\) is a continuum strong solution, adapted to \(W\), for continuous \(x\). By Lemma 18.3 and the continuity of the ODE, if \(\iota_N x_N \to x\) uniformly, then along the coupling \(\sup_{s \leqslant T} \| \iota_N x^N_s - x_s \|_{\infty} \to 0\) almost surely, since \(\mathbb{E} \sup_{s \leqslant T} \| \iota_N \xi^N_s - \xi_s \|_{\infty}^p \leqslant C_p N^{- \delta p}\) by Kolmogorov interpolation between the covariance difference and the Hölder bounds, and Borel–Cantelli. Testing, \(\langle Y_s, h \rangle\) converges as \(s \to \infty\) in \(L^2\) for \(h \in L^2\), uniformly in \(N\), since \(\|H_s h\|_2 \leqslant Cs^{- 1} \|h\|_2\) and \(\|b\|_2 \leqslant C \gamma s^{- 3 / 2} \|Y\|_2\). So \(\langle Y_{\infty}, h \rangle\) is defined; by (17.1) and the Itô bound on \(\mathbb{E} \|Y_s \|_2^2\), \(Y_s\) converges also in \(H^{- s'}\), \(s' > \frac{1}{2}\), in \(L^2 (\mathbb{R}^{q, x})\), uniformly in \(N\).

Theorem 18.4. (Limit of the kernels) Let \(\iota_N x_N \to x\) uniformly, \(x\) continuous. The densities (18.5) converge in \(L^1\) along the coupling, \(A_N (q, x_N) \to A (q, x)\), and for bounded continuous cylinder functions \(O\) of the terminal field

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}^{q, x_N}} O (\hat{\Phi}) \to \Pi_q O (x) : = \mathrm{e}^{A (q, x)} \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{R}^{q, x}} [O (Y_{\infty}) \exp (- \int_q^{\infty} e_s (Y_s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s)], \quad \mathrm{e}^{- A (q, x)} =\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{R}^{q, x}} \exp (- \int_q^{\infty} e_s (Y_s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s) . \) (18.10)

The same holds for bounded functionals of the path \((x_s)_{s \leqslant T}\) and finitely many \(\langle \varphi, h_i \rangle\) jointly, continuous for the uniform norm, and for \(O (\varphi) = \langle \varphi, h \rangle\), \(h \in L^2\). So \(A\) and \(\Pi_q O (x)\) are jointly continuous in \((q, x) \in [q_0, \infty) \times C (\mathbb{T}^3)\), also for \(O = \langle \cdot, h \rangle\). We write \(\langle m_q (x), h \rangle = \Pi_q \langle \cdot, h \rangle (x)\), the limit decoupling field; \(\|m_q (x)\|_2 \leqslant C_R\) by Proposition 16.4.

Proof. Let \(a_N = \exp (- \int_q^{\infty} e^N_s (Y^N_s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s)\), \(f_N = \mathrm{e}^{A_N} a_N\). On \([q, T]\) the integrals converge almost surely by Lemma 18.3, since the paths are bounded; the tail on \([T, \infty)\) is small in \(L^1\) uniformly in \(N\) by (18.9). So \(a_N \to a = \exp (- \int_q^{\infty} e)\) in probability. By (18.6), (16.9) and (16.10), \(\mathbb{E}f_N \log f_N \leqslant C_R\), so \((f_N)\) is uniformly integrable by de la Vallée Poussin's criterion, and \(|A_N | \leqslant C_R\) by Theorem 18.2. Along any subsequence with \(A_N \to A\), \(f_N \to \mathrm{e}^A a\) in \(L^1\), so \(\mathrm{e}^{- A} =\mathbb{E}a\). Thus the limit is unique and \(A_N \to A\). As \(\langle Y^N_{\infty}, h \rangle \to \langle Y_{\infty}, h \rangle\) in probability, \(L^1\) convergence of \(f_N\) gives (18.10) for bounded continuous \(O\) and, the paths converging uniformly along the coupling, for the joint functionals. For \(\langle \varphi, h \rangle\) use also the uniform second moment of \(\langle \hat{\Phi}, h \rangle\) under \(\mathbb{P}^{q, x_N}\), from the system (1) and (16.9). Continuity: the limit of \(\Pi^N\) along every uniformly convergent sequence exists, so the limit function is continuous in \(x\). If \((q_n, x_n) \to (q, x)\), the flows driven by the same \(W\) converge uniformly on compact scales by Lemma 16.3, \(\int_{q \wedge q_n}^{q \vee q_n} |e_s (Y_s) | \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s \to 0\) by the bounds on the defect, and the tails and the entropy are uniform; the same argument gives joint continuity.\(\Box\)

Theorem 18.5. (The measure) For bounded continuous cylinder \(O\), the whole sequence converges, and with \(\mathbb{R}\) the explicit flow \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} Y = b (Y) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + H (Y) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W\) on \([q_0, \infty)\), \(Y_{q_0} = 0\),

\(\displaystyle \int O \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \nu = \frac{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{R}} [O (Y_{\infty}) \mathrm{e}^{- \int_{q_0}^{\infty} e_s (Y_s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s}]}{\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{R}} [\mathrm{e}^{- \int_{q_0}^{\infty} e_s (Y_s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s}]} . \) (18.11)

With \(V_N (0) = 0\) and \(\mu_C\) the Gaussian measure of covariance \(C_{q_0}\), the renormalised free energy converges:

\(\displaystyle - \log \int \mathrm{e}^{- V_N} \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \mu_C -\mathcal{C}_N (q_0) \to A (q_0, 0) = - \log \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{R}} \mathrm{e}^{- \int_{q_0}^{\infty} e_s (Y_s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s} . \) (18.12)

Proof. This is Theorem 18.4 at \((q, x) = (q_0, 0)\), as \(\mathbb{P}^{q_0, 0}\) is the flow for \(\nu_N\), and \(\bar{V}_{q_0} (0) = 0\), \(V_{q_0} (0) = - \log \int \mathrm{e}^{- V_N} \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \mu_C\). With the tightness of Corollary 17.1 this identifies the limit along the whole sequence.\(\Box\)

18.4The filtering model and the FBSDE

We now read off the limit FBSDE from the density. Let \(\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{R}^{q_0, 0}\), \(\mathbb{P}= f\mathbb{R}\) with \(f = \mathrm{e}^{A (q_0, 0)} \mathrm{e}^{- \int_{q_0}^{\infty} e}\), on the paths of \(x\). Let \(\Phi = Y_{\infty}\), \(\Phi_q = x_q\), \(P_q = K_q x_q\), tested with smooth functions, and \(\mathcal{F}_q = \sigma (x_s : s \leqslant q)\).

Theorem 18.6. (Construction of \(\Phi^4_3\) [FBSDE26]) Let \(\lambda, q_0 > 0\), \(r \in \mathbb{R}\), with the counterterm of Theorem 11.2 (or \(q_{\ast}\) large and \(b_N \to b\), \(|b_N | \leqslant B \gamma q_{\ast}^{1 / 2}\)). Under \(\mathbb{P}\):

  1. The filtering model. \(\Phi \sim \nu\), and \(P_q = (q - q_0) \Phi + B_{q - q_0}\) with \(B\) a cylindrical Brownian motion independent of \(\Phi\).

  2. The forecast. The conditional law of \(\Phi\) given \(\mathcal{F}_q\) is \(\Pi_q (\Phi_q, \cdot)\). So for smooth \(h\),

    \(\displaystyle \langle \psi_q, h \rangle : =\mathbb{E}[\langle \Phi, h \rangle |\mathcal{F}_q] = \langle m_q (\Phi_q), h \rangle, \) (18.13)

    which is a function of \(P_q\), and \(\psi_q\) is the conditional mean of the terminal field.

  3. The FBSDE. \(W = P - \int_{q_0}^{\cdot} \psi_s \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s\) is a cylindrical Brownian motion for \((\mathcal{F}_q)\). With \(Y_q = y_q (x_q)\), \(Z = \psi - Y\), and for \(q \geqslant q_0\),

    \(\displaystyle Y_q = \int_{q_0}^q (b_s (Y_s) + H_s (Y_s) Z_s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + \int_{q_0}^q H_s (Y_s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W_s, \quad Z_q = - \int_{q_0}^q (b_s (Y_s) + H_s (Y_s) Z_s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + \int_{q_0}^q \Xi_s \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W_s, \) (18.14)

    where \(\Xi = \text{Cov} - H (Y)\), \(\psi = \int \text{Cov} \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W\) with no orthogonal part, and \(\text{Cov}\) is the conditional covariance,

    \(\displaystyle \langle \text{Cov}_q h, k \rangle =\mathbb{E}[\langle \Phi, h \rangle \langle \Phi, k \rangle |\mathcal{F}_q] - \langle \psi_q, h \rangle \langle \psi_q, k \rangle . \) (18.15)

    Moreover \(Y_{q_0} = Z_{q_0} = 0\), and \(\mathbb{E} \|Z_q \|_2^2 \leqslant Cq^{- 5 / 2}\) for large \(q\).

  4. The terminal value. \(Y_q \to \Phi\) in \(H^{- s}\) in mean square, for every \(s > \frac{1}{2}\).

  5. The lattice. Along the whole sequence, in law and jointly with \(\Phi^N_{\infty}\): \(x^N\) converges uniformly on compact sets of scales and space, \(Y^N_q\) uniformly in space at each \(q\), \(\langle \psi^N_q, h \rangle\), \(\langle Z^N_q, h \rangle\) at each \(q\), the innovations \(W^N\) in \(C (I ; H^{- \vartheta})\), \(\vartheta > \frac{3}{2}\), to \(W\). The energies \(E_{\infty}, J_{\infty}\) satisfy the bounds of Theorem 16.2 in the limit.

Proof. (a). By Theorem 18.4 at \((q_0, 0)\), the pair \((x^N, \Phi^N_{\infty})\) under the exact law converges in law to \((x, \Phi)\) under \(\mathbb{P}\). At each \(N\), \(P^N_q - (q - q_0) \Phi^N_{\infty}\) is a Brownian motion independent of \(\Phi^N_{\infty}\); test with \(j_N h\), as \(\iota_N K_q j_N h\) converges in every Sobolev norm. So \(\langle B_t, h \rangle\) for smooth \(h\) are jointly Gaussian, independent of \(\Phi\), with covariance \((s \wedge t) \langle h, k \rangle\); by density \(B\) is a cylindrical Brownian motion, with paths in \(H^{- \vartheta}\), \(\vartheta > \frac{3}{2}\). Moreover \(\mathcal{F}_q \subset \sigma (\Phi, B_r : r \leqslant q - q_0)\), as the continuous field \(x_r\) is determined by \(\langle P_r, h \rangle\).

(b). Under \(\mathbb{R}\) the flow is Markov, and by Theorem 18.4 at \((t, x_t)\),

\(\displaystyle D_t : =\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{R}} [f|\mathcal{F}_t] = \exp (A (q_0, 0) - A (t, x_t) - \int_{q_0}^t e_s (Y_s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s), \) (18.16)

and \(\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}} [O (\Phi) |\mathcal{F}_t] =\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{R}} [fO (\Phi) |\mathcal{F}_t] / D_t = \Pi_t O (x_t)\). The right side of (18.16) is continuous in \(t\), so (18.16) holds for all \(t\) simultaneously, and \(D\) is continuous and positive.

(c): the forward equation. By Lemma 16.3 the flow is a strong solution, and \(x\) and \(W^{\mathbb{R}}\) determine each other; so \(\mathcal{F}\) is a Brownian filtration under \(\mathbb{R}\). As \(f > 0\) has finite entropy, by lower semicontinuity from Theorem 18.2, martingale representation gives \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} D = D \langle Z', \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W^{\mathbb{R}} \rangle\) with \(\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}} \int \|Z' \|_2^2 = 2 \text{Ent} (\mathbb{P}|\mathbb{R})\), and \(W : = W^{\mathbb{R}} - \int Z'\) is a \(\mathbb{P}\)-Brownian motion for \(\mathcal{F}\) by Girsanov's theorem. Under \(\mathbb{R}\), the lattice equation \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} Y = b (Y) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + H (Y) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W^{\mathbb{R}}\) passes to the limit by Section 18.1, along the convergence of the coupling at each scale. So under \(\mathbb{P}\), \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} Y = (b (Y) + H (Y) Z') \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + H (Y) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W\), and \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} P = (Y + Z') \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W\).

The drift is the forecast. By (a), the increments of \(B\) after \(s\) are independent of \(\Phi\) and of \(\mathcal{F}_s\), so \(\mathbb{E}[\langle P_t - P_s, h \rangle |\mathcal{F}_s] = (t - s) \langle \psi_s, h \rangle\) for smooth \(h\). As \(\psi\) is a martingale, \(\langle P, h \rangle - \int_{q_0}^{\cdot} \langle \psi_u, h \rangle \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} u\) is an \(\mathcal{F}\)-martingale. So is \(\langle W, h \rangle = \langle P, h \rangle - \int_{q_0}^{\cdot} \langle Y_u + Z'_u, h \rangle \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} u\), where \(Y + Z'\) is locally in \(L^2 (\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} u \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \mathbb{P}; L^2)\), by (16.9), (16.10) and lower semicontinuity. Their difference is a continuous martingale of finite variation, null at \(q_0\), so it vanishes. With a countable dense set of \(h\), \(\psi_u = Y_u + Z'_u\) for almost every \((u, \omega)\), where \(\psi_u = m_u (x_u)\) is defined at every scale. So \(Z : = \psi - Y = m (x) - y (x)\) is defined at every scale, \(Z' = Z\) almost everywhere, and \(W\) is the innovation. Moreover \(\mathbb{E} \| \psi_Q \|_2^2 \leqslant \text{liminf}_N \mathbb{E} \| \psi^N_Q \|_2^2 < \infty\), by Fatou's lemma along (e) and the same bounds, so \(\psi\) is square integrable.

Representation, with no orthogonal part. Let \(S = \langle \Phi, h \rangle\) and \(M =\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{P}} [S|\mathcal{F}]\). Under \(\mathbb{R}\), \(N =\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{R}} [fS|\mathcal{F}]\) is a Brownian integral, \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} N = \langle n, \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W^{\mathbb{R}} \rangle\), and \(M = N / D\), so Itô's formula gives \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} M = \langle n / D - MZ, \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W^{\mathbb{R}} - Z \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s \rangle = \langle n / D - MZ, \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W \rangle\). This defines \(\text{Cov} h\). Here \(S \in L^2 (\mathbb{P})\) by Theorem 18.4 and Fatou's lemma, so \(M\) is a true \(L^2\)-martingale and \(\mathbb{E} \int \| \text{Cov}_s h\|_2^2 \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s = \text{Var} S\). Summing over an orthonormal basis, \(\mathbb{E} \int_{q_0}^Q \| \text{Cov}_s \|_{\text{HS}}^2 \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s =\mathbb{E} \| \psi_Q \|_2^2\).

The covariance. For \(G\) bounded and \(\mathcal{F}_s\)-measurable and \(k\) smooth, \(W_t - W_s\) tested with \(k\) is \((t - s) \langle \Phi, k \rangle + \langle B_{t - q_0} - B_{s - q_0}, k \rangle - \int_s^t \langle \psi_u, k \rangle \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} u\), and the term in \(B\) is independent of \((G, S)\). So \(\mathbb{E}[GS \langle W_t - W_s, k \rangle] = \int_s^t \mathbb{E}[GS (\langle \Phi, k \rangle - \langle \psi_u, k \rangle)] \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} u\). By the representation and Itô's isometry the left side is \(\int_s^t \mathbb{E}[G \langle \text{Cov}_u h, k \rangle] \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} u\). On the right, condition on \(\mathcal{F}_u \supset \mathcal{F}_s\). So the integral over \([q_0, \cdot]\) of the difference of the two sides of (18.15) is a continuous martingale of finite variation, hence null. This is (18.15) for almost every \((q, \omega)\), and \(\text{Cov}\) is symmetric and nonnegative, Hilbert–Schmidt almost everywhere.

The rest of (c). The equation for \(Z\) is the difference of those for \(\psi\) and \(Y\). At \(q_0\), \(P = 0\): so \(Y_{q_0} = 0\) by evenness, and \(Z_{q_0} = \psi_{q_0} =\mathbb{E}_{\nu} \Phi = 0\). The bound on \(Z\) is weak lower semicontinuity from Theorem 16.2, by (e).

(d). (17.2) passes to the limit by lower semicontinuity.

(e). For the path and \(\Phi\) this is Theorem 18.4; for \(\psi^N = m^N (x^N)\) and \(Z^N\) use continuous convergence in the same theorem and the uniform bound of Proposition 16.4. Then \(W^N = P^N - \int \psi^N\) converges tested, and \(W^N\) is tight in \(C (I ; H^{- \vartheta})\) as a Brownian motion. For the energies, \(\zeta_q = \Phi_q - (q - q_0) C_q \psi_q\) and \(V_q = C_q [(q - q_0) \psi_q - \frac{\gamma}{3} Y_q^3]\) converge uniformly, since the rows of the kernel of \(C^N_q\) converge uniformly in \(L^2\), and along the coupling \(\psi^N_q = m^N_q (x^N_q)\) is bounded in \(L^2\) and converges weakly. The integrands of \(E\) and \(J\) are lower semicontinuous in these variables, and Fatou's lemma applies.\(\Box\)

Remark 18.7. (Hidden noise) Let \(B\) be a real Brownian motion and \(L^n_t = \int_0^t (1 + \text{sin} (ns)) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} B_s\). Then \((L^n, B)\) converges in law to \((B + 2^{- 1 / 2} \tilde{B}, B)\), with \(\tilde{B}\) an independent Brownian motion: the integrands converge weakly to \(1\), but their squares converge weakly to \(\frac{3}{2}\). A limiting FBSDE obtained by compactness could in this way have an extra martingale, orthogonal to the driving noise. A compactness proof must exclude this by strong compactness of \(\text{Cov}^N\), for which [FBSDE26] uses the Riccati equation of the posterior covariance (Exercise 18.3). Here the density does it: the filtration is Brownian under the reference. What remains open is whether \(\mathcal{F}\) is generated by \(W\) (Section 18.8).

So the posterior structure survives the continuum limit: \(\psi = Y + Z\) is a closed observable, the conditional mean of the terminal field; \(Y\) is computed from the explicit action; and the error \(Z\) is small at high resolution. This is the FBSDE of Lecture 4, in forecast coordinates, for the limit \(\nu\) of the renormalised lattice measures, with the full noise retained.

18.5The renormalised field equation

The terminal law satisfies an integration by parts formula in which the renormalised cube appears only tested in expectation.

Theorem 18.8. (Integration by parts) Let \(h, h_1, \ldots, h_k\) be smooth and \(G (\varphi) = g (\langle \varphi, h_1 \rangle, \ldots, \langle \varphi, h_k \rangle)\) with \(g \in C_b^{\infty} (\mathbb{R}^k)\), and let \(D_h G (\varphi) = \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d} a} G (\varphi + ah) |_{a = 0}\). Along the sequence of Theorem 18.6 (recall \(C = (m^2 - \Delta_N)^{- 1}\) with \(m^2 = q_0\)),

\(\displaystyle \lim_N \mathbb{E} \left[ G (\iota_N \Phi^N_{\infty}) \hspace{0.17em} \langle \mu_N \Phi^N_{\infty} + \lambda (\Phi^N_{\infty})^3, j_N h \rangle_{H_N} \right] =\mathbb{E}_{\nu} [D_h G (\Phi) - G (\Phi) \langle \Phi, (q_0 - \Delta) h \rangle] .\) (18.17)

Proof. At fixed \(N\), Gaussian integration by parts in the density \(\mathrm{e}^{- V_N - \frac{1}{2} \langle \varphi, K_{q_0} \varphi \rangle}\) gives, with \(x = \Phi^N_{\infty}\) and \(h_N = j_N h\),

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E} [G (\iota_N x) \langle \mu_N x + \lambda x^3, h_N \rangle] =\mathbb{E} [D_{h_N} (G \circ \iota_N) (x)] -\mathbb{E} [G (\iota_N x) \langle x, K_{q_0} h_N \rangle] .\)

Cell averaging commutes with lattice translations, so \(K_{q_0} j_N h = j_N (q_0 - \Delta^{\text{tr}}_N) h\) with \(\Delta^{\text{tr}}_N\) the finite-difference Laplacian acting on smooth functions, which converges to \(\Delta h\) in every Sobolev norm. The right side converges by Corollary 17.1 (weak convergence in \(H^{- s}\) and uniform moments), and the derivative term is a finite sum with converging coefficients \(\langle j_N h, j_N h_i \rangle \to \langle h, h_i \rangle\).\(\Box\)

This is the terminal case of the Dyson–Schwinger equation of Theorem B.1, (B.3) of Lecture 4, for the limiting theory. It does not say that \(\mu_N \Phi + \lambda \Phi^3\) converges as a random distribution: only its expectation against cylinder functions is identified. This is consistent with the expectation of Lecture 4 (Figure 7.1 and the remark following it) that the limiting force is an observable without a terminal representative; it is not proved here that no such representative exists. At every finite resolution, on the other hand, the force is explicit: \(F_q = K_q Z_q - D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q)\) (Exercises 16.1 and 18.1).

Theorem 18.8 is the statement at infinite resolution, where the cube can only be tested in expectation. At every finite resolution there is a statement between observables, in which no divergent quantity appears. In the limit of Theorem 18.6, let

\(\displaystyle P_q = W_q + \int_{q_0}^q \psi_s \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s, \quad \Phi_q = C_q P_q, \quad \eta_q = P_q - (q - q_0) \psi_q, \quad \langle F_q, h \rangle = \langle \psi_q, K_q h \rangle - \langle P_q, h \rangle,\)

and \(J_q = (q - q_0) C_q\), which is \(K_{q_0} Q_q\) in the notation of Lecture 4 and tends to \(I\) as \(q \to \infty\). Here \(\Phi_q \in H\), while \(F_q\) and \(\eta_q\) are distributions, tested with smooth \(h\).

Theorem 18.9. (The field equation at resolution \(q\)) For the limit of Theorem 18.6, for every \(q \geqslant q_0\) and all smooth \(h\), \(G\) as in Theorem 18.8:

  1. Field equation. Almost surely, as distributions,

    \(\displaystyle K_{q_0} \Phi_q = J_q F_q + \eta_q . \) (18.18)

  2. Observables. \(\langle F, h \rangle\) and \(\langle \eta, h \rangle\) are centred martingales,

    \(\displaystyle \langle F_q, h \rangle = \int_{q_0}^q \langle (\operatorname{Cov}_s K_s - I) h, \mathrm{d} W_s \rangle, \qquad \langle \eta_q, h \rangle = \int_{q_0}^q \langle (I - (s - q_0)\operatorname{Cov}_s) h, \mathrm{d} W_s \rangle,\)

    and \(\mathbb{E} \mathrm{e}^{\langle \eta_q, h \rangle} \leqslant \mathrm{e}^{(q - q_0) \|h\|_2^2 / 2}\).

  3. Dyson–Schwinger equation at resolution \(q\).

    \(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}D_{J_q h} G (\Phi_q) =\mathbb{E} [G (\Phi_q) \langle \eta_q, h \rangle] =\mathbb{E} [G (\Phi_q) (\langle \Phi_q, K_{q_0} h \rangle - \langle F_q, J_q h \rangle)] . \) (18.19)

  4. The renormalised cube as an observable. For large \(q\), with \(\langle D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q), h \rangle := \langle P_q, h \rangle - \langle Y_q, K_q h \rangle\), the explicit force of Lecture 5,

    \(\displaystyle \| \langle F_q, h \rangle + \langle D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q), h \rangle \|_{L^2 (\mathbb{P})} \leqslant C_{\gamma} \|h\|_{H^2} q^{- 1 / 4} . \) (18.20)

    So \(\langle F, h \rangle\) is the unique martingale asymptotically equivalent to the explicit process \(- \langle D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q), h \rangle\), in the sense of Lemma 5.4 of Lecture 3.

  5. Infinite resolution. As \(q \to \infty\), both sides of (18.19) converge, and

    \(\displaystyle \lim_{q \to \infty} \mathbb{E} [G (\Phi_q) \langle D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q), J_q h \rangle] =\mathbb{E}_{\nu} [D_h G (\Phi) - G (\Phi) \langle \Phi, (q_0 - \Delta) h \rangle], \) (18.21)

    the right side of (18.17).

Proof. (a) is algebra: \(J_q K_q = q - q_0\) and \(K_{q_0} = K_q - (q - q_0)\) give, for \(\langle \Phi_q, K_q k \rangle = \langle P_q, k \rangle\), the identity \(K_{q_0} \Phi_q - J_q F_q = P_q - (q - q_0) \Phi_q - (q - q_0) (\psi_q - \Phi_q) = \eta_q\).

(c) By Theorem 18.6(a),(b), \(\eta_q = P_q - (q - q_0) \psi_q =\mathbb{E}_q [B_{q - q_0}]\), and \(G (\Phi_q)\) is \(\mathcal{F}_q\)-measurable, so \(\mathbb{E}[G (\Phi_q) \langle \eta_q, h \rangle] =\mathbb{E}[G (\Phi_q) \langle B_{q - q_0}, h \rangle]\). Now \(\Phi_q = C_q P_q = J_q \Phi + C_q B_{q - q_0}\), with \(B\) independent of \(\Phi\). Gaussian integration by parts in \(B\) gives \((q - q_0)\mathbb{E}D_{C_q h} G (\Phi_q) =\mathbb{E}D_{J_q h} G (\Phi_q)\). The second equality in (18.19) is (a).

(b) By Theorem 18.6, \(\psi_q = \int \text{Cov}_s \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W_s\); insert \(P = W + \int \psi\) and use the product rule, the drifts cancelling: \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} (K_q \psi_q - P_q) = (K_q \text{Cov}_q - I) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W_q\), tested with \(h\), and similarly for \(\eta\). The exponential bound is conditional Jensen in \(\eta_q =\mathbb{E}_q [B_{q - q_0}]\).

(d) \(P_q = K_q Y_q + D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q)\) and \(F_q = K_q Z_q - D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q)\). So the left side of (18.20) is \(\| \langle Z_q, K_q h \rangle \|_{L^2} \leqslant \|K_q h\|_2 (\mathbb{E} \|Z_q \|_2^2)^{1 / 2} \leqslant C \|h\|_{H^2} q^{- 1 / 4}\), by Theorem 18.6(c).

(e) By (18.20) with the exact \(K_q J_q h = (q - q_0) h\), replacing \(- F_q\) by \(D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q)\) in (18.19) costs \(O (q^{- 1 / 4})\). Moreover \(\langle \Phi_q, k \rangle = \langle \psi_q, J_q k \rangle + \langle \zeta_q, k \rangle\), where \(\mathbb{E} \langle \zeta_q, k \rangle^2 \leqslant (q - q_0) \|C_q k\|_2^2 \leqslant \|k\|_2^2 / q\), and \(\psi_q \to \Phi\) tested in mean square, by the martingale property. So \(\Phi_q \to \Phi\) tested with smooth functions, and \(J_q h \to h\) in every Sobolev norm.\(\Box\)

So the field equation holds at every resolution with no renormalisation constant, and in the strong sense of a pathwise identity between distributions. The nonlinearity enters only through the force observable \(F\), whose class contains the explicit process \(- D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q) \): the cube of the forecast with its counterterms, at resolution \(q\). This is the renormalised cube, as an element of the space of observables of Lecture 3. Theorem 18.8 is the shadow of this at infinite resolution: by (18.21), the two orders of limits, \(N \to \infty\) at the terminal resolution and \(q \to \infty\) after \(N\), give the same right side. What is not proved is that \(F\) is closed: the statement (18.20) gives no bound on \(\langle F_q, h \rangle\) uniform in \(q\).

18.6Dyson–Schwinger along the scales

Theorem 18.9 is stated for functionals of the field at one resolution. The same proof works along the whole observed path up to \(q\), and this is the scale-by-scale form of the Dyson–Schwinger equations of Lecture 4 for the interacting theory.

Corollary 18.10. (Dyson–Schwinger along the scales) For the limit of Theorem 18.6, let \(q_0 \leqslant q_1 \leqslant \ldots \leqslant q_k \leqslant q\), let \(h, h_1, \ldots, h_k\) be smooth, \(g \in C_b^{\infty} (\mathbb{R}^k)\) and \(G = g (\langle \Phi_{q_1}, h_1 \rangle, \ldots, \langle \Phi_{q_k}, h_k \rangle)\). Then

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}[G \langle \eta_q, h \rangle] =\mathbb{E}\mathcal{D}_h G, \quad \mathcal{D}_h G = \sum_{i = 1}^k \partial_i g \langle J_{q_i} h, h_i \rangle, \) (18.22)

that is, the derivative of \(G\) in the direction which shifts the field at each scale \(s\) by \(J_s h\). Equivalently, for \(\langle \eta_q, h \rangle = \langle \Phi_q, K_{q_0} h \rangle - \langle F_q, J_q h \rangle\),

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\mathcal{D}_h G =\mathbb{E}[G (\langle \Phi_q, K_{q_0} h \rangle + \langle D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q), J_q h \rangle)] + O (C_{\gamma} \|g\|_{\infty} \|h\|_{H^2} q^{- 1 / 4}) . \) (18.23)

So at every large resolution \(q\) the renormalised cube \(D \bar{\Gamma}_q (Y_q)\) closes the hierarchy, up to an error which vanishes as \(q \to \infty\). The increments are consistent: \(\mathbb{E}[G \langle \eta_q - \eta_s, h \rangle] = 0\) for \(q_k \leqslant s \leqslant q\).

Proof. \(G\) is \(\mathcal{F}_q\)-measurable, so \(\mathbb{E}[G \langle \eta_q, h \rangle] =\mathbb{E}[G \langle B_{q - q_0}, h \rangle]\). Moreover \(\Phi_s = J_s \Phi + C_s B_{s - q_0}\) with \(B\) independent of \(\Phi\), and for \(s \leqslant q\) the covariance of \(\langle B_{q - q_0}, h \rangle\) and \(\langle C_s B_{s - q_0}, k \rangle\) is \((s - q_0) \langle C_s h, k \rangle = \langle J_s h, k \rangle\). Gaussian integration by parts in the path of \(B\) gives (18.22). Finally (18.23) follows from (18.20) with \(J_q h\), since \(\|J_q h\|_{H^2} \leqslant \|h\|_{H^2}\).\(\Box\)

18.7Uniqueness in law and the variational formula

Along the exact flow the value \(A_t = A (t, x_t)\) solves a scalar backward equation, the integrated form of the vector one and the analogue of (4.13) of Gubinelli–Meyer. Indeed, by (18.16) and \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} D = D \langle Z, \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W^{\mathbb{R}} \rangle\),

\(\displaystyle A_t = \int_t^{\infty} (e_s (Y_s) + \frac{1}{2} \|Z_s \|_2^2) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + \int_t^{\infty} \langle Z_s, \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W_s \rangle . \) (18.24)

Definition 18.11. (Scalar FBSDE) Fix \(q \geqslant q_0\) and a continuous \(x\). A solution is a filtered probability space, of law \(\mathbb{Q}\), with an \(\mathcal{F}_t\)-cylindrical Brownian motion \(W\) and adapted processes \((x, Y, Z, A)\), \(x\) with paths in \(C ([q, \infty) ; C (\mathbb{T}^3))\), with \(Y_t = y_t (x_t)\), \(x_q = x\), \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} x = - C_s (x - Y) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + C_s (Z \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W)\), with \(\mathbb{E} \int_q^{\infty} \|Z\|_2^2 \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s +\mathbb{E} \int_q^{\infty} |e_s (Y_s) | \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s < \infty\), satisfying (18.24), \(A_q\) being deterministic, and such that \(\mathcal{E}_T = \exp (- \int_q^T \langle Z, \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W \rangle - \frac{1}{2} \int_q^T \|Z\|_2^2)\) is a uniformly integrable martingale.

The forward equation here is \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} P = (Y + Z) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W\) with \(P = K_s x\): the forward equation of (18.14). The limit law is a solution, by the proof of Theorem 18.6, at \((q, x)\) in place of \((q_0, 0)\): \(\mathcal{E}_t = D_t^{- 1}\) is uniformly integrable under the exact law, closed by \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \mathbb{R}/ \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \mathbb{P}\), and \(A (t, x_t) \to 0\) as \(D_t \to f > 0\).

Theorem 18.12. (Uniqueness in law) For every solution, the law of \(x\) on paths is the limit of \(\mathbb{P}^{q, x_N}\), \(A_q = A (q, x)\), and \(Z\) is the adapted functional of the path of \(x\) of the exact solution. So the terminal law is \(\Pi_q (x, \cdot)\).

Proof. Let \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \mathbb{Q}' =\mathcal{E}_{\infty} \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \mathbb{Q}\). Under \(\mathbb{Q}'\), \(W' = W + \int Z\) is a Brownian motion, and \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} x = - C_s (x - Y) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + C_s \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W'\). By pathwise uniqueness (Lemma 16.3), \(x\) under \(\mathbb{Q}'\) has law \(\mathbb{R}^{q, x}\). By (18.24) at \(t = q\), \(\mathcal{E}_{\infty}^{- 1} = \exp (\int_q^{\infty} \langle Z, \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W \rangle + \frac{1}{2} \int_q^{\infty} \|Z\|_2^2) = \exp (A_q - \int_q^{\infty} e_s (Y_s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s)\), a functional of the path of \(x\). So the law of \(x\) under \(\mathbb{Q}\) is \(\mathrm{e}^{A_q} \mathrm{e}^{- \int e} \mathbb{R}^{q, x}\), and normalisation gives \(A_q = A (q, x)\). By Theorem 18.4 this is the limit of \(\mathbb{P}^{q, x_N}\). For \(Z\): under \(\mathbb{Q}'\), for the filtration \(\mathcal{F}\), \(W'\) is a Brownian motion and \(x\) is Markov with respect to \(\mathcal{F}\), so the density process on \(\mathcal{F}_t\) is \(D_t =\mathbb{E}' [\mathrm{e}^{A_q - \int_q^{\infty} e} |\mathcal{F}_t]\), an adapted functional of the path of \(x\). The drift \(Z\) under \(\mathbb{Q}\) is determined by the covariation of \(\log D\) with \(W'\), which is computed from the path of \(x\) only, since \(C_s \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W'\) is a differential of \(x\) and its drift.\(\Box\)

So if the backward equation is written for the scalar value \(A\), the solution is unique in law. Its \(Z\) is the feedback \(m_s (x_s) - y_s (x_s)\), and by Theorem 18.6 it coincides with \(\psi - Y\) of (18.14); differentiability of \(A\) in \(x\) is not claimed.

Theorem 18.13. (Renormalised variational formula) For \(q \geqslant q_0\), \(x\) continuous, and \(O\) bounded measurable on paths, under the reference \(\mathbb{R}^{q, x}\),

\(\displaystyle - \log \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{R}^{q, x}} \mathrm{e}^{- O - \int_q^{\infty} e_s (Y_s) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s} = \inf_{\mathbb{Q} \ll \mathbb{R}^{q, x}, \text{Ent} (\mathbb{Q}|\mathbb{R}^{q, x}) < \infty} [\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}} [O + \int_q^{\infty} (e_s (Y_s) + \frac{1}{2} \|u_s \|_2^2) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s]], \) (18.25)

where, for \(\mathbb{Q}\) of finite entropy, \(u\) is the drift of \(W\) under \(\mathbb{Q}\) added to (18.2) as \(C_s u_s \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s\), so that \(Y\) solves \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} Y = (b (Y) + H (Y) u) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s + H (Y) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W^{\mathbb{Q}}\) and \(\text{Ent} (\mathbb{Q}|\mathbb{R}^{q, x}) = \frac{1}{2} \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}} \int \|u\|_2^2\). For \(O = 0\) the unique minimiser is \(u = Z\), and the value is \(A (q, x)\). In particular, at \((q, x) = (q_0, 0)\), for \(O\) a bounded continuous cylinder function of the terminal field,

\(\displaystyle - \log \int \mathrm{e}^{- O} \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \nu =\mathcal{G}(O) -\mathcal{G}(0), \quad \mathcal{G}(O) = \inf_u \mathbb{E}[O (Y^u_{\infty}) + \int_{q_0}^{\infty} (e_s (Y^u_s) + \frac{1}{2} \|u_s \|_2^2) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s], \quad Y^u_{q_0} = 0, \) (18.26)

the infimum being over the laws of finite entropy with respect to \(\mathbb{R}\), and \(\mathcal{G}(0) = A (q_0, 0)\) is the renormalised free energy (18.12). The minimiser for \(O = 0\) is \(u = Z\). No infinite constant occurs in \(\mathcal{G}\), and there is no initial law to optimise over.

Proof. The Gibbs variational principle: for a probability \(\mathbb{R}\) and a measurable \(\mathcal{S}\) with \(\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{R}} \mathrm{e}^{-\mathcal{S}} < \infty\), for every \(\mathbb{Q}\) with \(\text{Ent} (\mathbb{Q}|\mathbb{R}) < \infty\), \(\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}} \mathcal{S}^- \leqslant \text{Ent} (\mathbb{Q}|\mathbb{R}) + \log \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{R}} \mathrm{e}^{\mathcal{S}^-}\); where \(\mathrm{e}^{\mathcal{S}^-} \leqslant 1 + \mathrm{e}^{-\mathcal{S}}\). This gives that \(\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}} \mathcal{S}\) is well-defined in \((- \infty, \infty]\) and \(\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}} \mathcal{S}+ \text{Ent} (\mathbb{Q}|\mathbb{R}) \geqslant - \log \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{R}} \mathrm{e}^{-\mathcal{S}}\), with equality exactly at \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \mathbb{Q} \propto \mathrm{e}^{-\mathcal{S}} \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} \mathbb{R}\) if \(\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}} |\mathcal{S}| < \infty\) there. Apply this with \(\mathcal{S}= O + \int_q^{\infty} e\). The integrability \(\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{R}} \mathrm{e}^{-\mathcal{S}} < \infty\) is Theorem 18.4. At the minimiser for \(O = 0\), \(\mathbb{E}|\mathcal{S}| < \infty\) passes to the limit from (18.9) by Fatou; for \(O\) bounded the same holds as the tilted law has a bounded density with respect to it. Since \(\mathbb{R}^{q, x}\) is the strong solution driven by \(W\), every \(\mathbb{Q}\) of finite entropy has a drift \(u\) with \(\text{Ent} = \frac{1}{2} \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}} \int \|u\|^2\) by Föllmer's theorem. At \(O = 0\), the minimiser has control \(Z\), by Section 18.7 (\(u = Z\) is the Föllmer drift of \(\mathbb{P}^{q, x}\)). The equation for \(Y\) holds under \(\mathbb{R}^{q, x}\) by the proof of Theorem 18.6, hence under \(\mathbb{Q} \ll \mathbb{R}^{q, x}\) with \(\hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W = \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} W^{\mathbb{Q}} + u \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s\). (18.26) is (18.25) at \((q_0, 0)\), with (18.11).\(\Box\)

Remark 18.14. (Coercivity as a corollary) (18.25) with \(O = 0\) reads, for every control, \(\mathbb{E} \int (e_s (Y^u_s) + \frac{1}{2} \|u_s \|^2) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s \geqslant A (q, x) \geqslant - C_R\). This is the coercivity \(\mathbb{E} \int (e_s + \frac{\theta}{2} \|u_s \|^2) \hspace{0.17em} \mathrm{d} s \geqslant - C_R\) with constant \(\theta = 1\). It has been obtained from the exact solution without any estimate on the controlled equation. An independent proof with \(\theta < 1\), as suggested by the test with \(Y^3\) in the memory form, would give the exponential moments \(\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{R}} \mathrm{e}^{- p \int e}\), \(p > 1\), which are not needed here.

Remark 18.15. (Comparison) In the Barashkov–Gubinelli functional the Lagrangian contains \(\frac{1}{2} \|u\|^2\) for a control singular at the cutoff, and renormalisation is a change of variables in the cost, subtracting the Wick cube of the free field (Remark 15.4). Here the reference process carries the renormalisation, the control is the remainder \(Z\), it is in \(L^2\) uniformly in \(N\) (of size \(q^{- 5 / 4}\)), and the only interaction left in the cost is the defect \(e\), with \(\mathbb{E} \int |e|\) small at large \(q\).

Remark 18.16. (Comparison with sine–Gordon) In [GM24] the smoothed force and the defect are bounded uniformly in the field, there is a spatial cut-off, and the same formula follows from boundedness; (18.24) is the analogue of their (4.13). Here the defect is unbounded. What replaces the bound is the entropy of the control: UV stability of the restarted laws makes the densities uniformly integrable, and exponential moments of the defect are not needed. The backward error alone does not give the result: from a given law at scale \(q\), Girsanov's theorem reduces the terminal law to that one, which is the unknown. Only the start at \(q_0\), or the restart from a fixed field, removes it. The limit can also be identified as the invariant measure of the \(\Phi^4_3\) dynamics [HSt22, HMt18, HMa18, HSc22, MW17, MW20, GH21], where quartic tails hold also at \(H^{- 1 / 2 - \vartheta}\) on the literature's lattices; we have not used this.

18.8What is not proved, and where to go next

  • The vector FBSDE and strong solutions. Theorem 18.12 gives uniqueness in law for the scalar form. A solution of (18.14) alone need not have \(Z\) of gradient form. It would suffice that any solution has a Lipschitz decoupling field, which needs a pointwise bound on \(\text{Cov}_q (x) = D^2 \mathcal{K}_q\), or high-scale contraction with non-degeneracy on compact intervals; only \(L^2 (\text{HS})\) control of \(\text{Cov}\) is available. The same bound is what is missing for \(P\) to be adapted to the innovation \(W\): the explicit flow is a strong solution, the exact one is not known to be. The Allinger–Mitter theorem does not apply: its finite-energy hypothesis is on the signal \(\Phi\), and \(\| \Phi \|_2 = \infty\) here.

  • Quartic tails at the free threshold. Theorem 17.3 gives quartic moments in \(H^{- \rho}\) for \(\rho > \frac{3}{2}\), and Theorem 17.4 Gaussian moments for \(s > \frac{1}{2}\). The quartic moment at \(s\) near \(\frac{1}{2}\) would need spatially resolved sources.

  • Axioms. Reflection positivity holds at every lattice, for reflections in lattice and mid-lattice planes (nearest-neighbour action), and passes to \(\nu\) for every reflection in a coordinate plane, by approximating the plane with lattice planes (standard; Exercise 18.4). Rotation invariance (absent on the torus) and clustering need infinite volume.

  • Infinite volume. The force, its Burgers flow, and differences of free energies are intensive (Section 9.2). The estimates of this lecture must then be made local, with weights in space and constants per unit volume. A new difficulty appears in the backward equation: its integrand \(\Xi\) is a nonlocal operator, and the backward error at a point can receive noise from arbitrarily far away. Uniform-in-volume coefficient bounds and local energy moments are themselves open; they could give an infinite-volume Gibbs subsequence. Controlling the “noise from infinity” (together with local compactness of the Hessian blocks) is the main additional step needed to retain the full FBSDE without an orthogonal martingale [FBSDE26].

Summary of the course.
The six lectures went from the Wightman axioms to the Euclidean formulation (Lecture 1), to the free field (Lecture 2), to stochastic quantisation and the scale decomposition (Lecture 3), and to the three faces of an interacting field: the Polchinski equation, the FBSDE and the variational problem (Lecture 4). In Lectures 5–6 the FBSDE in forecast coordinates, together with an explicit solution of the Wetterich equation up to an integrable defect, gave a construction of \(\Phi^4_3\) in which every object has a probabilistic meaning: the forecast is a conditional mean, the response is a conditional covariance, the Brownian motion is an innovation, and UV stability is the statement that an explicit action predicts the conditional mean up to an error that is small at high resolution. In the end the exact flow is the explicit flow with control the error, the measure is an explicit density over the explicit flow, and the renormalised variational problem has that error as its optimal control.

Exercise 18.3. Exercise 18.4. (Reflection positivity) Show that \(\nu_N\) is reflection positive for reflections in lattice and mid-lattice planes (nearest-neighbour Gaussian times a local potential). Deduce reflection positivity of \(\nu\) for every coordinate plane \(\{x_1 = a\}\): take cylinder functions of tests supported in \(\{a < x_1 < a + \frac{1}{2} \}\) (one of the two components of the torus minus the fixed planes \(\{x_1 = a\}\) and \(\{x_1 = a + \frac{1}{2} \}\) of \(\theta_a\)), cell-average them, and use lattice planes \(a_N \to a\), so that for large \(N\) the averaged tests lie on the correct side.
