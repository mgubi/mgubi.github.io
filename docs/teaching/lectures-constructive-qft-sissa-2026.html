A constructive introduction to quantum field theory

Lectures at SISSA, Trieste, 20 October–6 November 2026, as part of the Probability and Stochastic Analysis Trimester Programme.

Lecturer. Massimiliano Gubinelli (University of Oxford).

This course is part of the Probability and Stochastic Analysis Trimester Programme at SISSA.

Abstract

In these lectures I will review some mathematical problems associated to the definition and construction of quantum field theories. Mainly from the probabilistic viewpoint but also paying some attention to the quantum aspects. The goal is to give a wide perspective on the open problems and opportunities for research, in the era of large language models.

Lecture materials

  • Lecture 1 (20 October 2026): Relativistic quantum fields and imaginary time. Wightman axioms and quantum fields as operator-valued distributions; the two-point function; positivity of energy and continuation to imaginary time; the Feynman–Kac representation, lattice spin systems and the Osterwalder–Schrader axioms. Notes: [PDF] [HTML]. Slides: [PDF] [HTML]. Revised 29 October 2026.

  • Lecture 2 (22 October 2026): The Gaussian free field. Construction on the torus and Euclidean space; reflection positivity and the free relativistic field; Nelson's Markov property and Symanzik's Brownian-path representation; Wick products; Gaussian integration by parts and the Dyson–Schwinger equation. Notes: [PDF] [HTML]. Slides: [PDF] [HTML]. Revised 30 October 2026.

  • Lecture 3 (30 October 2026): Stochastic quantisation and stochastic observables. Parabolic and elliptic stochastic quantisation of the Gaussian free field; decomposition of the field by resolution; stochastic observables, with examples from Brownian motion and the free field; Wick powers in every dimension, products of observables and the operator product expansion. Notes: [PDF] [HTML]. Slides: [PDF] [HTML]. Revised 30 October 2026.

Provisional materials for Lectures 4–7

Lecture 4 introduces interactions through the Polchinski flow; Lectures 5–7 give an FBSDE construction of \(\phi^4_3\) on the torus. These materials may be adjusted as the course progresses. Lecture 7 will not be delivered in class.

  • Lecture 4: The Polchinski flow and forward–backward equations. Effective potentials and the Polchinski equation; the FBSDE, whose force is an observable; the Wetterich equation and the variational structure of the flow; perturbation theory, counterterms and Dyson–Schwinger equations at every scale. Notes: [PDF] [HTML]. Slides: [PDF] [HTML].

  • Lecture 5: The Wetterich FBSDE, two renormalisations and the large-field problem. Notes: [PDF] [HTML]. Slides: [PDF] [HTML].

  • Lecture 6: A priori estimates for the forward–backward system. Notes: [PDF] [HTML]. Slides: [PDF] [HTML].

  • Lecture 7: UV stability, compactness and the limiting FBSDE (not delivered in class). Notes: [PDF] [HTML]. Slides: [PDF] [HTML].
